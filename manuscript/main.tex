\begin{abstract}
This paper investigates two-timescale secure transmission for a movable antenna (MA)-enabled multiuser downlink with a passive eavesdropper (Eve), where the antenna positions are designed based on statistical channel state information (CSI), whereas zero-forcing precoding with null-space artificial noise (AN) is adapted to the instantaneous CSI of the legitimate users (LUs). The antenna positions and the long-term power allocation are jointly optimized to maximize the ergodic secrecy sum rate. To tackle this nonconvex stochastic problem, we first approximate the ergodic rates of the LUs and Eve in closed form, from which we show that the MAs can reduce the information leakage and increase the AN received by Eve, even for a single LU. Then, we develop an alternating optimization algorithm that combines secrecy water-filling with a grid search and majorization--minimization for each antenna position. Simulation results show that the proposed design outperforms the benchmark schemes while requiring less AN power and fewer antennas.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Artificial noise, movable antenna, physical layer security, statistical CSI, two-timescale design.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
% =============================================================================
\IEEEPARstart{P}{hysical} layer security (PLS) exploits the randomness and spatial characteristics of wireless channels to protect confidential messages against eavesdropping~\cite{Wyner1975,Mukherjee2014,WuJSAC2018}. In multi-antenna systems, PLS is commonly achieved by secure precoding and artificial noise (AN)~\cite{Goel2008,Zhou2010}, which steer the confidential signals toward the legitimate users (LUs) and degrade the channel of the eavesdropper (Eve), respectively. Both techniques, however, rely on the spatial separability between the LUs and Eve, which is limited for a conventional fixed-position antenna (FPA) array if Eve is located in a direction close to that of an LU and the line-of-sight (LoS) path is dominant~\cite{Mukherjee2014,HuSPL2024}. Recently, movable antennas (MAs)~\cite{ZhuMag2024,ZhuTWC2024Model} have emerged as a promising technique to enhance this separability by adjusting the antenna positions within a given region, which reshapes the channel correlations among the receivers without additional radio-frequency (RF) chains~\cite{MaTWC2024,ZhuTWC2024MU,ZhuCL2023}.

For MA-enabled PLS, the transmit beamforming and the antenna positions have been jointly optimized to maximize the secrecy rate with perfect channel state information (CSI)~\cite{HuSPL2024} and without the instantaneous CSI of the eavesdroppers~\cite{HuTMC2024}. AN has further been combined with MAs in~\cite{TangTCOM2025,ChengArxiv2024}, and the ergodic secrecy rate with the statistical CSI of a multi-antenna Eve has been characterized in~\cite{XieArxiv2026}. However, the antenna positions in these works are optimized based on the instantaneous CSI of the legitimate receivers. Consequently, the antennas have to be repositioned in each channel coherence interval (CI), which incurs a high operational complexity~\cite{ZhengTCOM2025}, and the CSI has to be acquired over the entire movable region~\cite{HuTVT2025}. Moreover, the instantaneous CSI may become outdated when the antenna movement delay exceeds the coherence time~\cite{ChenArxiv2025EE}, whereas most MA-enabled PLS designs assume that this delay is negligible or tolerable~\cite{YuArxiv2025}. The sensing-assisted secure design in~\cite{ChenArxiv2026}, on the other hand, assumes LoS channels and thus ignores the small-scale fading.

To reduce the frequency of antenna movement and the CSI acquisition overhead, two-timescale designs have been developed for MA systems~\cite{ZhengTCOM2025,HuTVT2025,ZhuArxiv2024NF,YanArxiv2025}, where the antenna positions are optimized based on the statistical CSI, whereas the beamforming is designed based on the instantaneous CSI. However, these works focus on improving the communication performance without considering eavesdropping. In particular, the ergodic-rate approximations for zero-forcing (ZF) processing in~\cite{ZhengTCOM2025,HuTVT2025}, which build on~\cite{ZhangJSTSP2014}, depend on the antenna positions only through the channel correlations among the users, so that the MAs provide no rate gain for a single user~\cite[Remark~2]{ZhengTCOM2025}. To the best of our knowledge, the two-timescale MA design for multiuser secure transmission remains unexplored.

In this paper, we jointly optimize the antenna positions and the long-term power allocation of an MA-enabled multiuser downlink to maximize the ergodic secrecy sum rate (ESSR) based on the statistical CSI of the LUs and Eve, where the base station (BS) adopts ZF precoding and null-space AN in each CI. First, by decomposing the channel of Eve into its components along each ZF direction and within the AN subspace, we derive closed-form approximations of the average information leakage and the average AN power received by Eve. Unlike the approximations in~\cite{ZhengTCOM2025,HuTVT2025}, the resulting approximation of the ergodic wiretap rate depends on the LoS correlations between Eve and the LUs, through which the MAs can enhance secrecy even for a single LU. Then, for given antenna positions, we obtain the optimal power allocation among the LUs by secrecy water-filling~\cite{Gopala2008,Liang2008} and the AN power by a one-dimensional search. Finally, an alternating optimization (AO) algorithm is developed, where each antenna position is selected by a low-complexity grid search over the movable region and then refined by majorization--minimization (MM). Simulation results show that the proposed design outperforms the benchmark schemes and that \rev{its gain grows with the Rician factor of Eve}.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows: Section~\ref{sec:model} presents the system model and the problem formulation, Section~\ref{sec:analysis} analyzes the ergodic secrecy rate, Section~\ref{sec:opt} develops the AO algorithm, Section~\ref{sec:sim} provides the simulation results, and Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes the paper.

As illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:system}, we consider the downlink of a multiuser system, where a BS equipped with $N$ MAs serves $M$ single-antenna LUs in the presence of a single-antenna passive Eve. Let $\mathcal M\triangleq\{1,\ldots,M\}$ and $\mathcal N\triangleq\{1,\ldots,N\}$ denote the index sets of the LUs and the MAs, respectively. To transmit AN in the null space of the channels of the LUs, we assume $N\ge M+1$. The MAs are connected to the RF chains via flexible cables and can move within a two-dimensional square region $\mathcal C\triangleq[-A/2,A/2]\times[-A/2,A/2]$ of side length $A$~\cite{ZhuMag2024,ZhuTWC2024Model}. In the local Cartesian coordinate system of $\mathcal C$, the position of the $n$-th MA is denoted by $\bt_n\triangleq[x_n,y_n]^{\mathrm T}\in\mathcal C$, and the positions of all MAs are collected in $\bt\triangleq[\bt_1^{\mathrm T},\ldots,\bt_N^{\mathrm T}]^{\mathrm T}\in\mathbb R^{2N\times1}$. To mitigate mutual coupling, any two MAs must be separated by at least a minimum distance $\Dmin$, and the residual coupling is neglected.

Since the size of $\mathcal C$ is much smaller than the propagation distance, moving the MAs changes only the phases of the LoS paths, whereas their angles of departure (AoDs) and amplitudes remain unchanged~\cite{ZhuTWC2024Model}. Let $\theta_m\in[-\pi/2,\pi/2]$ and $\phi_m\in[-\pi/2,\pi/2]$ denote the elevation and azimuth AoDs of the LoS path from the BS to the $m$-th LU, respectively, and define the direction vector $\ba_m\triangleq[\cos\theta_m\sin\phi_m,\ \sin\theta_m]^{\mathrm T}$. Then, the propagation distance of the LoS path from the $n$-th MA differs from that from the origin of $\mathcal C$ by $\bt_n^{\mathrm T}\ba_m$~\cite{ZhuTWC2024Model}, which results in a phase difference of $2\pi\bt_n^{\mathrm T}\ba_m/\lambda$, where $\lambda$ is the carrier wavelength. Hence, the LoS component of the channel from the BS to the $m$-th LU is given by~\cite{ZhengTCOM2025}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hbar}
\hb_m(\bt)\triangleq\Big[e^{j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\bt_1^{\mathrm T}\ba_m},\ldots,e^{j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\bt_N^{\mathrm T}\ba_m}\Big]^{\mathrm T}\in\mathbb C^{N\times1}.
\end{equation}
As in~\cite{ZhengTCOM2025,HuTVT2025}, the channel from the BS to the $m$-th LU follows Rician fading and is given by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hm}
\bh_m(\bt)=\sqrt{\frac{\kappa_m\beta_m}{\kappa_m+1}}\,\hb_m(\bt)+\sqrt{\frac{\beta_m}{\kappa_m+1}}\,\htl_m,
\end{equation}
where $\beta_m$ is the large-scale fading coefficient, $\kappa_m\ge0$ is the Rician factor, and $\htl_m\sim\CN(\mathbf 0,\bI_N)$ is the non-LoS (NLoS) component. Similarly, the channel from the BS to Eve is given by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:g}
\bg(\bt)=\sqrt{\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm e}\beta_{\mathrm e}}{\kappa_{\mathrm e}+1}}\,\gb(\bt)+\sqrt{\frac{\beta_{\mathrm e}}{\kappa_{\mathrm e}+1}}\,\gtl,
\end{equation}
where $\beta_{\mathrm e}$, $\kappa_{\mathrm e}$, and $\gtl\sim\CN(\mathbf 0,\bI_N)$ are defined in the same way as in~\eqref{eq:hm}, and $\gb(\bt)$ is given by~\eqref{eq:hbar} with $\ba_m$ replaced by $\ba_{\mathrm e}\triangleq[\cos\theta_{\mathrm e}\sin\phi_{\mathrm e},\ \sin\theta_{\mathrm e}]^{\mathrm T}$ for the elevation and azimuth AoDs $\theta_{\mathrm e}$ and $\phi_{\mathrm e}$ of Eve. The NLoS components $\htl_1,\ldots,\htl_M$ and $\gtl$ are mutually independent and, as in~\cite{ZhengTCOM2025,HuTVT2025}, spatially uncorrelated over $\mathcal C$, so that the antenna positions affect the channel statistics only through the LoS components. Let $\bH(\bt)\triangleq[\bh_1(\bt),\ldots,\bh_M(\bt)]\in\mathbb C^{N\times M}$ denote the channel matrix from the BS to the LUs, and let $\Hb(\bt)\triangleq[\hb_1(\bt),\ldots,\hb_M(\bt)]$ and $\Htl\triangleq[\htl_1,\ldots,\htl_M]$ collect its LoS and NLoS components, respectively. The Rician factors of the LUs are collected in $\bK\triangleq\diag(\kappa_1,\ldots,\kappa_M)$.

The statistical CSI consists of the large-scale fading coefficients, the Rician factors, and the AoDs, i.e., $\{\beta_m,\kappa_m,\theta_m,\phi_m\}_{m\in\mathcal M}$ for the LUs and $\{\beta_{\mathrm e},\kappa_{\mathrm e},\theta_{\mathrm e},\phi_{\mathrm e}\}$ for Eve. These parameters vary much more slowly than the NLoS components $\Htl$ and $\gtl$, which change from one CI to another. Following~\cite{ZhengTCOM2025,HuTMC2024}, the statistical CSI of the LUs and Eve is assumed to be known at the BS.\footnote{The statistical CSI of Eve can be obtained, e.g., from prior knowledge of its location, by sensing its direction~\cite{ChenArxiv2026}, or from its historical signals if it is an untrusted node.}

Based on these two timescales, we adopt the transmission protocol in Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol}, where time is divided into frames, each consisting of $N_{\mathrm{CI}}\gg1$ CIs with constant statistical CSI. At the beginning of each frame, the BS optimizes the antenna positions and the long-term power allocation based on the statistical CSI and then moves the MAs to the optimized positions. In each CI of the frame, the BS estimates the channels of the LUs at these positions from uplink pilots by exploiting the channel reciprocity of time-division duplexing and then designs the precoders and the AN. For ease of exposition, the channels of the LUs are assumed to be perfectly estimated, whereas the NLoS component $\gtl$ of Eve is unknown to the BS.

\subsection{Signal Model and Ergodic Secrecy Rate}

In each CI, the transmit signal of the BS is given by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:x}
\bx=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\bw_m s_m+\bz,
\end{equation}
where $s_m\sim\CN(0,1)$ is the confidential symbol for the $m$-th LU, $\bw_m\in\mathbb C^{N\times1}$ is the corresponding precoding vector, and $\bz\sim\CN(\mathbf 0,\bSig)$ is the AN with covariance matrix $\bSig\succeq\mathbf 0$. The symbols $s_1,\ldots,s_M$ and the AN $\bz$ are mutually independent, and the transmit power satisfies $\sum_{m=1}^M\|\bw_m\|^2+\tr(\bSig)\le\Ptot$, where $\Ptot$ is the maximum transmit power of the BS.

The signal received by the $m$-th LU is given by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ym}
r_m=\bh_m^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\bx+n_m,
\end{equation}
where $n_m\sim\CN(0,\sigma_m^2)$ is the additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN) at the $m$-th LU. Accordingly, the signal-to-interference-plus-noise ratio (SINR) of the $m$-th LU is given by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sinr_gen}
\gamma_m=\frac{|\bh_m^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\bw_m|^2}{\sum_{i=1,i\neq m}^{M}|\bh_m^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\bw_i|^2+\bh_m^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\bSig\bh_m(\bt)+\sigma_m^2}.
\end{equation}
Similarly, the signal received by Eve is given by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ye}
r_{\mathrm e}=\bg^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\bx+n_{\mathrm e},
\end{equation}
where $n_{\mathrm e}\sim\CN(0,\sigma_{\mathrm e}^2)$ is the AWGN at Eve. Because the BS does not know the receiver structure of the passive Eve, we consider the worst case where Eve can cancel the signals of the other LUs when intercepting $s_m$, e.g., via multiuser detection~\cite{ZhuTWC2014}. The resulting SINR of Eve for $s_m$ is given by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sinre_gen}
\gamma_{\mathrm{e},m}=\frac{|\bg^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\bw_m|^2}{\bg^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\bSig\bg(\bt)+\sigma_{\mathrm e}^2},
\end{equation}
which upper-bounds that of an Eve treating the signals of the other LUs as interference.

As the instantaneous CSI of Eve is unknown, the BS cannot adapt the transmission rate to each realization of $\gtl$. Instead, the confidential message of each LU is encoded over the $N_{\mathrm{CI}}$ CIs of a frame, which experience independent realizations of the NLoS components. For $N_{\mathrm{CI}}\gg1$, this coding scheme achieves the following ergodic secrecy rate for the $m$-th LU~\cite{Gopala2008,ZhuTWC2014}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:esr}
R_m^{\mathrm{sec}}\triangleq\big[R_m-R_{\mathrm{e},m}\big]^+,
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ergodic}
R_m\triangleq\E\{\log_2(1+\gamma_m)\},\quad R_{\mathrm{e},m}\triangleq\E\{\log_2(1+\gamma_{\mathrm{e},m})\}
\end{equation}
denote the ergodic rate of the $m$-th LU and the ergodic wiretap rate of Eve for $s_m$, respectively, with the expectations taken over $\Htl$ and $\gtl$ for a given $\bt$. The ESSR is then given by $\sum_{m=1}^MR_m^{\mathrm{sec}}$.

\subsection{Problem Formulation}

Since the precoders and the AN covariance matrix are adapted to the instantaneous CSI of the LUs, they are designed for each realization of $\bH(\bt)$, whereas the antenna positions are designed based on the statistical CSI. Accordingly, the ESSR maximization problem is formulated as
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:P1}
\begin{align}
(\mathrm{P1}):\ \max_{\bt,\{\bw_m\},\bSig}\ \ & \sum_{m=1}^{M}R_m^{\mathrm{sec}}\label{eq:P1obj}\\
\mathrm{s.t.}\ \ & \sum_{m=1}^M\|\bw_m\|^2+\tr(\bSig)\le\Ptot,\nonumber\\
& \bSig\succeq\mathbf 0,\label{eq:P1pow}\\
& \bt_n\in\mathcal C,\ \forall n\in\mathcal N,\label{eq:P1region}\\
& \|\bt_n-\bt_i\|\ge\Dmin,\ \forall n,i\in\mathcal N,\ n\neq i.\label{eq:P1dist}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}

Problem (P1) is a nonconvex stochastic optimization problem, which is challenging to solve. Specifically, the ergodic rates in~\eqref{eq:ergodic} have no closed-form expressions, and the precoders and the AN covariance matrix need to be optimized for every realization of $\bH(\bt)$. Moreover, the objective function is nonconcave with respect to $\bt$ due to the phases of the LoS components in~\eqref{eq:hbar}, and the minimum-distance constraints in~\eqref{eq:P1dist} are nonconvex. To tackle these challenges, we first adopt ZF precoding and null-space AN with a long-term power allocation and approximate the ergodic rates in closed form in Section~\ref{sec:analysis}, which yields a deterministic problem in the long-term variables only. Then, an AO algorithm is developed for this deterministic problem in Section~\ref{sec:opt}.

\section{Two-Timescale ZF-AN Design and Ergodic Secrecy Rate Analysis}\label{sec:analysis}

\subsection{ZF Precoding and Null-Space AN}\label{sec:zfan}

We adopt ZF precoding combined with null-space AN~\cite{Zhou2010,ZhuTWC2014}, referred to as the ZF-AN design, which requires only the instantaneous CSI of the LUs and yields tractable SINRs for the subsequent analysis. Specifically, the precoder of the $m$-th LU is given by $\bw_m=\sqrt{p_m}\,\wb_m$, where $p_m\ge0$ is the power allocated to the $m$-th LU, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zf}
\wb_m\triangleq\frac{\bH(\bt)\big(\bH^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\bH(\bt)\big)^{-1}\be_m}{\big\|\bH(\bt)\big(\bH^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\bH(\bt)\big)^{-1}\be_m\big\|}
\end{equation}
is the normalized ZF precoding vector. As $\bH(\bt)$ has full column rank with probability one, $\wb_m$ is well defined and satisfies $\bh_i^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\wb_m=0$, $\forall i\neq m$, which eliminates the inter-user interference. Moreover, as in~\cite{Zhou2010,ZhuTWC2014}, the AN is isotropically distributed in the null space of $\bH^{\mathrm H}(\bt)$ with the covariance matrix $\bSig=q\PHp$, where $q\ge0$ is the AN power per dimension, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:an}
\PHp\triangleq\bI_N-\bH(\bt)\big(\bH^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\bH(\bt)\big)^{-1}\bH^{\mathrm H}(\bt)
\end{equation}
is the orthogonal projector onto the null space of $\bH^{\mathrm H}(\bt)$. Because $\bH^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\PHp=\mathbf 0$ and $\tr(\PHp)=N-M$, the AN causes no interference to the LUs, and the transmit power in each CI is $\sum_{m=1}^Mp_m+(N-M)q$. \rev{Although the AN could also be steered toward the LoS component of Eve, the isotropic AN requires no CSI of Eve in each CI, impairs eavesdroppers unknown to the BS as well, and ensures that the secrecy gain obtained from the statistical CSI of Eve can be attributed to the MAs.}

The powers $\bp\triangleq[p_1,\ldots,p_M]^{\mathrm T}$ and $q$ constitute the long-term power allocation, which remains fixed within a frame so that it can be optimized jointly with the antenna positions based on the statistical CSI, whereas $\wb_m$ and $\PHp$ are updated in each CI according to $\bH(\bt)$. As a result, the ZF-AN design involves no optimization in each CI. With this design, the SINRs in~\eqref{eq:sinr_gen} and~\eqref{eq:sinre_gen} reduce to
\begin{align}
\gamma_m&=\frac{p_m|\bh_m^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\wb_m|^2}{\sigma_m^2}=\frac{p_m}{\sigma_m^2\big[(\bH^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\bH(\bt))^{-1}\big]_{m,m}},\label{eq:snr}\\
\gamma_{\mathrm{e},m}&=\frac{p_m|\bg^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\wb_m|^2}{q\,\bg^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\PHp\bg(\bt)+\sigma_{\mathrm e}^2},\label{eq:sinre}
\end{align}
where the second equality in~\eqref{eq:snr} follows from $|\bh_m^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\wb_m|^2=1/[(\bH^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\bH(\bt))^{-1}]_{m,m}$ by~\eqref{eq:zf}.

With the ZF-AN design, (P1) is restricted to
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:P2}
\begin{align}
(\mathrm{P2}):\ \max_{\bt,\bp,q}\ \ & \sum_{m=1}^{M}R_m^{\mathrm{sec}}(\bt,p_m,q)\label{eq:P2obj}\\
\mathrm{s.t.}\ \ & \sum_{m=1}^{M}p_m+(N-M)q\le\Ptot,\label{eq:P2pow}\\
& p_m\ge0,\ \forall m\in\mathcal M,\quad q\ge0,\label{eq:P2pos}\\
& \eqref{eq:P1region},\ \eqref{eq:P1dist},\nonumber
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
where $R_m^{\mathrm{sec}}(\bt,p_m,q)$ denotes~\eqref{eq:esr} with the SINRs in~\eqref{eq:snr} and~\eqref{eq:sinre}, and~\eqref{eq:P2pow} and~\eqref{eq:P2pos} guarantee~\eqref{eq:P1pow} in every CI. Since the ZF-AN design is a feasible choice of the precoders and the AN covariance matrix in (P1), the optimal value of (P2) is a lower bound on that of (P1). However, (P2) is still intractable because the ergodic rates in~\eqref{eq:P2obj} have no closed-form expressions. To address this issue, the ergodic rates of the LUs and Eve are approximated in Sections~\ref{sec:lu} and~\ref{sec:eve}, respectively.

\subsection{Ergodic Rate of the LUs}\label{sec:lu}

Let $\tilde\beta_m\triangleq\beta_m/(\kappa_m+1)$ denote the power of the NLoS component of the $m$-th LU, and define $\bB\triangleq\diag(\tilde\beta_1,\ldots,\tilde\beta_M)$ and $\bL(\bt)\triangleq\Hb(\bt)\bK^{1/2}$. From~\eqref{eq:hm}, the channel matrix of the LUs can be written as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Hdecomp}
\bH(\bt)=\big(\bL(\bt)+\Htl\big)\bB^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
By~\eqref{eq:snr}, the ergodic rate of the $m$-th LU is determined by the distribution of $[(\bH^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\bH(\bt))^{-1}]_{m,m}$. Since $\log_2(1+p_m/(\sigma_m^2z))$ is convex in $z>0$, applying Jensen's inequality to~\eqref{eq:snr} yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lujensen}
R_m\ge\log_2\bigg(1+\frac{p_m}{\sigma_m^2\E\big\{[(\bH^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\bH(\bt))^{-1}]_{m,m}\big\}}\bigg).
\end{equation}
For Rician fading, the expectation in~\eqref{eq:lujensen} has no closed-form expression. Following~\cite{ZhangJSTSP2014}, we approximate the Gram matrix $\bH^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\bH(\bt)$ by a central Wishart matrix with $N$ degrees of freedom and the same mean $\bB^{1/2}\bPhi(\bt)\bB^{1/2}$, where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Phi}
\bPhi(\bt)\triangleq\bL^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\bL(\bt)+N\bI_M.
\end{equation}
The inverse of this Wishart matrix has the mean $N\bB^{-1/2}\bOm(\bt)\bB^{-1/2}/(N-M)$ with $\bOm(\bt)\triangleq\bPhi^{-1}(\bt)$. Substituting this mean into~\eqref{eq:lujensen}, we approximate the ergodic rate of the $m$-th LU by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Rm}
R_m\approx\hat R_m(\bt,p_m)\triangleq\log_2\big(1+\eta_m(\bt)\,p_m\big),
\end{equation}
where $\eta_m(\bt)\triangleq\zeta_m(N-M)/(N\omega_m(\bt))$ is the effective channel gain of the $m$-th LU, $\zeta_m\triangleq\tilde\beta_m/\sigma_m^2$, and $\omega_m(\bt)\triangleq[\bOm(\bt)]_{m,m}$.

The approximation in~\eqref{eq:Rm} is equivalent to that in~\cite[Sec.~IV-A]{ZhengTCOM2025}. It reduces to the well-known lower bound $\log_2(1+\zeta_mp_m(N-M))$ for Rayleigh fading~\cite{Ngo2013} and underestimates the ergodic rate in the LoS-dominant regime, as shown in Section~\ref{sec:sim_acc}. In~\eqref{eq:Rm}, the antenna positions affect $\hat R_m(\bt,p_m)$ only through $\omega_m(\bt)$. From $\bPhi(\bt)\succeq N\bI_M$ and $[\bOm(\bt)]_{m,m}\ge1/[\bPhi(\bt)]_{m,m}=1/(N(\kappa_m+1))$~\cite{Horn2013}, it follows that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:etabound}
\zeta_m(N-M)\le\eta_m(\bt)\le\zeta_m(N-M)(\kappa_m+1),
\end{equation}
where the upper bound is achieved if $\hb_m^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\hb_i(\bt)=0$ for all $i\neq m$ with $\kappa_i>0$. By decorrelating the LoS component of the $m$-th LU from those of the other LUs, the antenna positions can thus increase $\eta_m(\bt)$ by a factor of at most $\kappa_m+1$.

\subsection{Ergodic Wiretap Rate of Eve}\label{sec:eve}

Unlike $\gamma_m$ in~\eqref{eq:snr}, which depends on $\bH(\bt)$ only through $[(\bH^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\bH(\bt))^{-1}]_{m,m}$, the SINR of Eve in~\eqref{eq:sinre} depends on the ZF direction $\wb_m$ and the AN subspace, both of which vary randomly with $\bH(\bt)$. To handle this dependence, we first characterize the information leakage $X_m\triangleq|\bg^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\wb_m|^2$ and the AN power $Y\triangleq\bg^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\PHp\bg(\bt)$ received by Eve for a given $\bH(\bt)$. Because $\gtl$ is independent of $\bH(\bt)$, \eqref{eq:g} yields $\bg(\bt)\,|\,\bH(\bt)\sim\CN\big(\sqrt{\kappa_{\mathrm e}\tilde\beta_{\mathrm e}}\,\gb(\bt),\tilde\beta_{\mathrm e}\bI_N\big)$, where $\tilde\beta_{\mathrm e}\triangleq\beta_{\mathrm e}/(\kappa_{\mathrm e}+1)$ is the power of the NLoS component of Eve. Hence, the conditional means of $X_m$ and $Y$ are given by (see Appendix~\ref{app:indep})
\begin{align}
\E\{X_m|\bH(\bt)\}&=\tilde\beta_{\mathrm e}\big(\kappa_{\mathrm e}\bar X_m+1\big),\label{eq:EX}\\
\E\{Y|\bH(\bt)\}&=\tilde\beta_{\mathrm e}\big(\kappa_{\mathrm e}\bar Y+N-M\big),\label{eq:EY}
\end{align}
where $\bar X_m\triangleq|\gb^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\wb_m|^2$ and $\bar Y\triangleq\gb^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\PHp\gb(\bt)$ are the LoS counterparts of $X_m$ and $Y$, respectively. Since $X_m$ and $Y$ are nonnegative random variables, we approximate $R_{\mathrm{e},m}=\E\{\log_2(1+p_mX_m/(qY+\sigma_{\mathrm e}^2))\}$ by replacing them with their means~\cite[Lemma~1]{ZhangJSTSP2014}. This approximation is accurate when $X_m$ and $Y$ concentrate around their means, e.g., for weak NLoS components (see Appendix~\ref{app:indep}), although it is neither an upper nor a lower bound. Using \eqref{eq:EX} and~\eqref{eq:EY} and taking the expectation over $\Htl$, we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Re0}
R_{\mathrm{e},m}\approx\log_2\bigg(1+\frac{p_m\big(\kappa_{\mathrm e}\E\{\bar X_m\}+1\big)}{q\big(\kappa_{\mathrm e}\E\{\bar Y\}+N-M\big)+\zeta_{\mathrm e}^{-1}}\bigg),
\end{equation}
where $\zeta_{\mathrm e}\triangleq\tilde\beta_{\mathrm e}/\sigma_{\mathrm e}^2$ is defined similarly to $\zeta_m$ in~\eqref{eq:Rm}.

The expectations $\E\{\bar X_m\}$ and $\E\{\bar Y\}$ in~\eqref{eq:Re0}, i.e., the average LoS energies of Eve along the ZF direction of the $m$-th LU and in the AN subspace, still have no closed-form expressions. Because $\wb_m$ and $\PHp$ are invariant to the scaling of the columns of $\bH(\bt)$, they are determined by $\bL(\bt)+\Htl$ in~\eqref{eq:Hdecomp}, whose Gram matrix has the mean $\bPhi(\bt)$. As derived in Appendix~\ref{app:det}, replacing the inverse of this Gram matrix with $\bOm(\bt)$ and calibrating the NLoS contribution to the AN subspace by the identity $\tr(\PHp)=N-M$ yield $\E\{\bar X_m\}\approx c_m(\bt)$ and $\E\{\bar Y\}\approx d(\bt)$ with
\begin{align}
c_m(\bt)&\triangleq\frac{\big|[\bOm(\bt)\bv(\bt)]_m\big|^2+N[\bOm^2(\bt)]_{m,m}}{\omega_m(\bt)},\label{eq:cm}\\
d(\bt)&\triangleq\frac{(N-M)\big(N-\bv^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\bOm(\bt)\bv(\bt)\big)}{N-M+N\tr(\bOm(\bt))},\label{eq:d}
\end{align}
where $\bv(\bt)\triangleq\bL^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\gb(\bt)=\bK^{1/2}\Hb^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\gb(\bt)$ collects the LoS correlations between Eve and the LUs weighted by $\bK^{1/2}$. Substituting~\eqref{eq:cm} and~\eqref{eq:d} into~\eqref{eq:Re0}, we approximate the ergodic wiretap rate of Eve for $s_m$ by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Re}
R_{\mathrm{e},m}\approx\hat R_{\mathrm{e},m}(\bt,p_m,q)\triangleq\log_2\bigg(1+\frac{\xi_m(\bt)\,p_m}{q\,\psi(\bt)+\zeta_{\mathrm e}^{-1}}\bigg),
\end{equation}
where $\xi_m(\bt)\triangleq\kappa_{\mathrm e}c_m(\bt)+1$ and $\psi(\bt)\triangleq\kappa_{\mathrm e}d(\bt)+N-M$ represent the normalized information leakage for the $m$-th LU and the normalized AN power received by Eve, respectively. The approximations $c_m(\bt)$ and $d(\bt)$ have the following properties.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:eve}
The quantities $c_m(\bt)$, $d(\bt)$, $\E\{\bar X_m\}$, and $\E\{\bar Y\}$ lie in $[0,N]$. Moreover, $c_m(\bt)=\E\{\bar X_m\}$ and $d(\bt)=\E\{\bar Y\}$ if $\kappa_m=0$, $\forall m\in\mathcal M$. If the LoS components of the LUs are linearly independent, $c_m(\bt)-\E\{\bar X_m\}$ and $d(\bt)-\E\{\bar Y\}$ vanish as $\kappa_1\to\infty$ with fixed ratios $\kappa_m/\kappa_1>0$, $m\in\mathcal M$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{IEEEproof}
See Appendix~\ref{app:det}.
\end{IEEEproof}

Unlike the ergodic-rate approximations in~\cite{ZhangJSTSP2014,ZhengTCOM2025}, which involve only the diagonal entries of $\bOm(\bt)$, \eqref{eq:Re} captures the LoS correlations between Eve and the LUs via $\bv(\bt)$.

\subsection{Secrecy Mechanisms of the MAs}\label{sec:insight}

By~\eqref{eq:Rm} and~\eqref{eq:Re}, the antenna positions affect the approximate ergodic rates only through $\omega_m(\bt)$, $c_m(\bt)$, and $d(\bt)$. By~\eqref{eq:Phi}, \eqref{eq:cm}, and~\eqref{eq:d}, these quantities in turn depend on $\bt$ only through the LoS correlations among the LUs and between Eve and the LUs. In this subsection, we examine how the MAs improve the secrecy performance by reshaping these correlations.

\textit{1) LoS decorrelation:} The LoS correlations are given by
\begin{align}
[\Hb^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\Hb(\bt)]_{i,m}&=\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\bt_n^{\mathrm T}(\ba_{m}-\ba_i)},\quad i,m\in\mathcal M,\label{eq:corrLU}\\
[\Hb^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\gb(\bt)]_{i}&=\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\bt_n^{\mathrm T}(\ba_{\mathrm e}-\ba_i)},\quad i\in\mathcal M,\label{eq:corrEve}
\end{align}
respectively. As shown in Section~\ref{sec:lu}, decorrelating the LoS components of the LUs raises $\eta_m(\bt)$ toward its upper bound in~\eqref{eq:etabound}. This mechanism is the only one exploited by the MA design in~\cite{ZhengTCOM2025}, which maximizes the ergodic sum rate and is referred to as the rate-oriented MA design. For secure transmission, the MAs offer two additional mechanisms: a smaller $c_m(\bt)$ reduces the information leakage, and a larger $d(\bt)$ increases the AN received by Eve. \rev{For a given $\bPhi(\bt)$, both effects are strongest if $\bv(\bt)=\mathbf 0$, i.e., if the LoS component of Eve is orthogonal to those of all LUs with $\kappa_m>0$.} In contrast, an FPA array cannot effectively decorrelate Eve from the LUs when Eve is located in a direction close to that of an LU. If $\kappa_{\mathrm e}=0$, then $\hat R_{\mathrm{e},m}=\log_2(1+p_m/(q(N-M)+\zeta_{\mathrm e}^{-1}))$ does not depend on $\bt$, since the statistical CSI of Eve then carries no spatial information. In this case, only the decorrelation among the LUs remains.

\textit{2) Single LU:} If $M=1$, then $\bPhi(\bt)=N(\kappa_1+1)$ is a scalar, and $\eta_1=\zeta_1(N-1)(\kappa_1+1)$ does not depend on $\bt$.\footnote{For $M=1$, even the exact ergodic rate $\E\{\log_2(1+p_1\|\bh_1(\bt)\|^2/\sigma_1^2)\}$ does not depend on $\bt$, because the distribution of $\|\bh_1(\bt)\|^2$ depends on $\hb_1(\bt)$ only through $\|\hb_1(\bt)\|^2=N$.} In contrast, with $\rho_1(\bt)\triangleq|\hb_1^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\gb(\bt)|/N\in[0,1]$ denoting the normalized LoS correlation between Eve and the LU, we have
\begin{align}
c_1(\bt)&=\frac{\kappa_1N\rho_1^2(\bt)+1}{\kappa_1+1},\label{eq:c1}\\
d(\bt)&=\frac{(N-1)(\kappa_1+1)}{(N-1)(\kappa_1+1)+1}\Big(N+\frac{1}{\kappa_1+1}-c_1(\bt)\Big),\label{eq:d1}
\end{align}
which depend on $\bt$ through $\rho_1(\bt)$. Therefore, unlike in the rate-oriented MA design, where the MAs provide no gain for a single user~\cite[Remark~2]{ZhengTCOM2025}, the MAs can enhance the secrecy of a single LU by reducing this correlation. \rev{In particular, $c_1(\bt)$ increases and $d(\bt)$ decreases with $\rho_1(\bt)$, so that the approximate wiretap rate in~\eqref{eq:Re} increases with $\rho_1(\bt)$ for any $p_1>0$ and $q\ge0$, whereas the approximate rate of the LU does not depend on $\bt$. Hence, any feasible $\bt$ with $\rho_1(\bt)=0$ maximizes the approximate secrecy rate for any $p_1$ and $q$.}

\textit{3) High-SNR behavior and AN power:} To gain insight into the AN power, we consider the information--AN power splitting with equal power among the LUs, where $p_m=\alpha\Ptot/M$ and $q=(1-\alpha)\Ptot/(N-M)$ with the power-splitting factor $\alpha\in(0,1)$. As $\Ptot\to\infty$, i.e., in the high signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) regime, the approximate secrecy rate of the $m$-th LU satisfies
\begin{align}
\hat R_m-\hat R_{\mathrm{e},m}&=\log_2\frac{\alpha\Ptot\eta_m(\bt)}{M}\nonumber\\
&\quad-\log_2\Big(1+\frac{\alpha\Xi_m(\bt)}{1-\alpha}\Big)+o(1),\label{eq:highsnr}
\end{align}
where $o(1)$ vanishes as $\Ptot\to\infty$, and $\Xi_m(\bt)\triangleq\frac{(N-M)\xi_m(\bt)}{M\psi(\bt)}$ is proportional to the ratio of the normalized information leakage to the normalized AN power received by Eve. Hence, the high-SNR slope of the approximate ESSR is $M$ regardless of $\bt$, and the antenna positions affect only the constant term. \rev{Since the terms in~\eqref{eq:highsnr} that depend on $\alpha$ equal $\log_2(\alpha(1-\alpha))-\log_2(1+\alpha(\Xi_m(\bt)-1))$, it can be verified from the second-order derivative that the sum of the constant terms is strictly concave in $\alpha\in(0,1)$. Its maximizer $\alpha^\star$ is thus the unique root of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:alphastar}
\frac{M}{\alpha}-\frac{M}{1-\alpha}=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\frac{\Xi_m(\bt)-1}{1+\alpha(\Xi_m(\bt)-1)},
\end{equation}
which reduces to $\alpha^\star=1/(1+\sqrt{\Xi})$ for $\Xi_m(\bt)=\Xi$, $\forall m\in\mathcal M$. \rev{For $M=1$ and $\kappa_{\mathrm e}=0$, $\Xi_1(\bt)=1$ for any $\bt$, and $\alpha^\star=1/2$ recovers the equal power split between the information signal and the AN, which is near-optimal for non-colluding eavesdroppers in Rayleigh fading~\cite{Zhou2010}.} Since the right-hand side of~\eqref{eq:alphastar} increases with each $\Xi_m(\bt)$, the MAs, which can decrease $c_m(\bt)$ and increase $d(\bt)$, decrease $\Xi_m(\bt)$ and thus increase $\alpha^\star$,} so that less power is required for the AN.

\subsection{Approximate ESSR Maximization Problem}\label{sec:discussion}

Combining~\eqref{eq:Rm} and~\eqref{eq:Re}, we approximate the ergodic secrecy rate of the $m$-th LU by $\hat R_m^{\mathrm{sec}}\triangleq[\hat R_m-\hat R_{\mathrm{e},m}]^+$, which depends only on the statistical CSI. Replacing the ergodic secrecy rates in (P2) with $\hat R_m^{\mathrm{sec}}$ yields the following deterministic problem:
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:P3}
\begin{align}
(\mathrm{P3}):\ \max_{\bt,\bp,q}\ \ & F(\bt,\bp,q)\triangleq\sum_{m=1}^{M}\bigg[\log_2\big(1+\eta_m(\bt)p_m\big)\nonumber\\
&\qquad\qquad-\log_2\bigg(1+\frac{\xi_m(\bt)p_m}{q\psi(\bt)+\zeta_{\mathrm e}^{-1}}\bigg)\bigg]\label{eq:P3obj}\\
\mathrm{s.t.}\ \ & \eqref{eq:P2pow},\ \eqref{eq:P2pos},\ \eqref{eq:P1region},\ \eqref{eq:P1dist},\nonumber
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
where the operator $[\cdot]^+$ in $\hat R_m^{\mathrm{sec}}$ is dropped without loss of optimality, because setting $p_m=0$ for any LU with a negative term in~\eqref{eq:P3obj} preserves feasibility and does not decrease the objective value.

% =============================================================================
\section{Proposed Alternating Optimization Algorithm}\label{sec:opt}
% =============================================================================
Problem (P3) is nonconvex because $F$ is nonconcave with respect to $\bt$, the minimum-distance constraints in~\eqref{eq:P1dist} are nonconvex, and the variables $\bt$, $\bp$, and $q$ are coupled in $F$. Nevertheless, (P3) has two favorable properties. First, for a given $\bt$, the optimal $\bp$ for any given $q$ can be derived in semi-closed form. Second, for given $\bp$ and $q$, each antenna position affects $F$ only through rank-one terms of $\bPhi(\bt)$ and $\bv(\bt)$, which allows $F$ to be evaluated at many candidate positions with low complexity. Based on these properties, we alternately optimize the power allocation and the antenna positions in Sections~\ref{sec:power} and~\ref{sec:position}, respectively.

\subsection{Power Allocation Optimization for Given $\bt$}\label{sec:power}

For a given $\bt$, (P3) reduces to
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:P31}
\begin{align}
(\mathrm{P3.1}):\ \max_{\bp,q}\ \ & \sum_{m=1}^{M}F_m(p_m;q)\\
\mathrm{s.t.}\ \ & \eqref{eq:P2pow},\ \eqref{eq:P2pos},\nonumber
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
where $F_m(p;q)\triangleq\log_2(1+\eta_mp)-\log_2(1+\eta_{\mathrm{e},m}(q)\,p)$, and $\eta_{\mathrm{e},m}(q)\triangleq\xi_m/(q\psi+\zeta_{\mathrm e}^{-1})$ denotes the effective channel gain of Eve for $s_m$, which decreases with $q$. For brevity, the argument $\bt$ is omitted in this subsection. Problem (P3.1) is nonconvex in general, because $F_m$ is not jointly concave in $p_m$ and $q$. For a given $q$, however, the optimal $\bp$ can be obtained in semi-closed form from the first-order derivative of $F_m$ with respect to $p$, i.e.,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fm1}
\frac{\partial F_m}{\partial p}=\frac{\eta_m-\eta_{\mathrm{e},m}}{\ln2\,(1+\eta_mp)(1+\eta_{\mathrm{e},m}p)},
\end{equation}
where the argument $q$ of $\eta_{\mathrm{e},m}$ is omitted. If $\eta_m\le\eta_{\mathrm{e},m}$, then $F_m(p;q)\le0$ for all $p\ge0$, and thus $p_m=0$ is optimal. If $\eta_m>\eta_{\mathrm{e},m}$, then $\frac{\eta_m}{1+\eta_mp}>\frac{\eta_{\mathrm{e},m}}{1+\eta_{\mathrm{e},m}p}$, so that $F_m$ is strictly increasing in $p\ge0$ by~\eqref{eq:fm1} and strictly concave because its second-order derivative is the difference between the squares of the right-hand and left-hand sides of this inequality divided by $\ln2$.

Let $\mathcal M_{\mathrm a}(q)\triangleq\{m\in\mathcal M\,|\,\eta_m>\eta_{\mathrm{e},m}(q)\}$ denote the set of LUs that can achieve positive secrecy rates, and let $P_{\mathrm s}(q)\triangleq\Ptot-(N-M)q$ denote the power available for the confidential signals. Then, (P3.1) reduces to a convex power allocation problem over parallel Gaussian wiretap channels for any $q$ with $P_{\mathrm s}(q)>0$ and $\mathcal M_{\mathrm a}(q)\neq\emptyset$, where the main-channel and wiretap-channel gains are $\{\eta_m\}$ and $\{\eta_{\mathrm{e},m}(q)\}$, respectively. Its optimal solution is given by the secrecy water-filling~\cite{Gopala2008,Liang2008}, i.e., $p_m^\star=0$ for $m\notin\mathcal M_{\mathrm a}(q)$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wf}
p_m^\star(\nu)=\bigg[\frac{2(\mu_m-1)}{\sqrt{(\eta_m-\eta_{\mathrm{e},m})^2+4\eta_m\eta_{\mathrm{e},m}\mu_m}+\eta_m+\eta_{\mathrm{e},m}}\bigg]^+
\end{equation}
for $m\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm a}(q)$, where $\mu_m=\mu_m(\nu)\triangleq(\eta_m-\eta_{\mathrm{e},m})/(\nu\ln2)$, and $\nu>0$ is the Lagrange multiplier associated with the power constraint in~\eqref{eq:P2pow}. Specifically, \eqref{eq:wf} is the positive root of the Karush--Kuhn--Tucker (KKT) stationarity condition~\cite{Boyd2004} $(1+\eta_mp)(1+\eta_{\mathrm{e},m}p)=\mu_m(\nu)$ obtained from~\eqref{eq:fm1}. Since $\sum_{m\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm a}(q)}p_m^\star(\nu)$ is continuous and strictly decreasing in $\nu$ whenever it is positive, the multiplier that satisfies $\sum_{m\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm a}(q)}p_m^\star(\nu)=P_{\mathrm s}(q)$ is unique and can be found by bisection. Moreover, \eqref{eq:wf} reduces to the classic water-filling solution $[1/(\nu\ln2)-1/\eta_m]^+$ as $\eta_{\mathrm{e},m}\to0$.\footnote{For multiple non-colluding single-antenna Eves, \eqref{eq:cm}--\eqref{eq:Re} apply to each Eve, and $\eta_{\mathrm{e},m}(q)$ is replaced by its maximum over the Eves.}

Let $F^\star(q)\triangleq\sum_{m\in\mathcal M}F_m(p_m^\star;q)$ denote the optimal value of (P3.1) for a given $q$, with $F^\star(q)=0$ if $\mathcal M_{\mathrm a}(q)=\emptyset$ or $P_{\mathrm s}(q)=0$. The AN power per dimension is then obtained by maximizing $F^\star(q)$ over $0\le q\le q_{\max}$, where $q_{\max}\triangleq\Ptot/(N-M)$. Increasing $q$ reduces the effective channel gains of Eve at the cost of less power for the confidential signals. \rev{If $q^\star\in(0,q_{\max})$ and the set of LUs with positive power does not change around $q^\star$, then the derivative of $F^\star(q)$ vanishes at $q^\star$. Since $\partial F_m/\partial p=\nu$ holds for these LUs by the KKT conditions and their powers sum to $P_{\mathrm s}(q)$, it can be verified that this condition is given by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:qstat}
\frac{1}{N-M}\sum_{m\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm a}(q^\star)}\frac{\psi\,\eta_{\mathrm{e},m}\,p_m^\star}{(q^\star\psi+\zeta_{\mathrm e}^{-1})(1+\eta_{\mathrm{e},m}p_m^\star)}=\nu\ln2,
\end{equation}
where $\eta_{\mathrm{e},m}=\eta_{\mathrm{e},m}(q^\star)$. The left-hand side is the decrease of the wiretap rates (in nats) per unit of AN power, and the right-hand side is the increase of the secrecy sum rate (in nats) per unit of signal power. Thus, $q^\star$ balances the secrecy gains of the two uses of the transmit power. In the high-SNR regime, the resulting AN power fraction $(N-M)q^\star/\Ptot$ is predicted by $1-\alpha^\star$ with $\alpha^\star$ in~\eqref{eq:alphastar}, which is examined in Section~\ref{sec:sim_snr}.} As $F^\star(q)$ is not necessarily unimodal, this one-dimensional problem is handled by a coarse grid search followed by a finer one around the best grid point, as summarized in Algorithm~\ref{alg:power}.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Power Allocation for Given $\bt$}\label{alg:power}
\small\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $\{\eta_m,\xi_m\}_{m\in\mathcal M}$, $\psi$, $\zeta_{\mathrm e}$, $\Ptot$, and the grid size $Q$.
\Ensure $q^\star$ and $\bp^\star$.
\For{each $q$ in a uniform grid of $Q$ points over $[0,q_{\max}]$}
  \State Obtain $\bp^\star$ by \eqref{eq:wf}, where $\nu$ is found by bisection.
  \State Evaluate $F^\star(q)$.
\EndFor
\State Repeat Lines 1--4 over a uniform grid of $Q$ points between the two neighbors of the best grid point.
\State \Return the best $q$ as $q^\star$ and its optimal power allocation as $\bp^\star$.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Antenna Position Optimization for Given $\bp$ and $q$}\label{sec:position}

For given $\bp$ and $q$, (P3) reduces to maximizing $F(\bt)\triangleq F(\bt,\bp,q)$ subject to~\eqref{eq:P1region} and~\eqref{eq:P1dist}, which is referred to as (P3.2). With this slight abuse of notation, the arguments of $F$ hereafter indicate the variables being optimized, whereas the other variables are fixed at their latest values. Problem (P3.2) is challenging for two reasons. First, by~\eqref{eq:corrLU} and~\eqref{eq:corrEve}, $F(\bt)$ depends on each $\bt_n$ through the phases $\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\bt_n^{\mathrm T}(\ba_{m}-\ba_i)$, $i,m\in\mathcal M\cup\{\mathrm e\}$. Since each phase varies with the period $\lambda/\|\ba_{m}-\ba_i\|$ along $\ba_{m}-\ba_i$, $F(\bt)$ oscillates over distances on the order of a wavelength and has many local maxima within $\mathcal C$. Second, the minimum-distance constraints are nonconvex. To reduce the problem dimension, we optimize the antenna positions in an element-wise manner as in~\cite{ZhengTCOM2025}, i.e., $\bt_n$ is updated with the other positions fixed, for $n=1,\ldots,N$ in turn. Accordingly, $F(\bt_n)$ denotes $F$ as a function of $\bt_n$, and the subproblem for the $n$-th MA is given by
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:P32n0}
\begin{align}
(\mathrm{P3.2}.n):\ \max_{\bt_n}\ \ & F(\bt_n)\\
\mathrm{s.t.}\ \ & \bt_n\in\mathcal C,\\
& \|\bt_n-\bt_i\|\ge\Dmin,\ \forall i\in\mathcal N\setminus\{n\}.
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
Although (P3.2.$n$) has only two real variables, the oscillation of $F(\bt_n)$ causes a local method, such as the MM method in~\cite{ZhengTCOM2025}, to converge to a local maximum close to the current position, which may trap the AO iterations at an inferior solution (see Section~\ref{sec:sim_acc}). To avoid this issue, we tackle (P3.2.$n$) in two stages: Stage~1 selects the best candidate position by a grid search over $\mathcal C$, and Stage~2 refines this position by the MM method. The cost of the grid search is reduced by the following rank-one structure.

\textit{1) Rank-one structure:} Let $\bl_n(\bt_n)\in\mathbb C^{M\times1}$ denote the conjugate transpose of the $n$-th row of $\bL(\bt)$, whose $m$-th entry is $\sqrt{\kappa_m}\,e^{-j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\bt_n^{\mathrm T}\ba_m}$. With $\bL^{\mathrm H}\bL=\sum_{i=1}^N\bl_i\bl_i^{\mathrm H}$ and $\bL^{\mathrm H}\gb=\sum_{i=1}^N\bl_ie^{j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\bt_i^{\mathrm T}\ba_{\mathrm e}}$, we have
\begin{align}
\bPhi(\bt)&=\bGam_n+\bl_n(\bt_n)\bl_n^{\mathrm H}(\bt_n),\label{eq:Phin}\\
\bv(\bt)&=\br_n+\bl_n(\bt_n)e^{j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\bt_n^{\mathrm T}\ba_{\mathrm e}},\label{eq:vn}
\end{align}
where $\bGam_n\triangleq\sum_{i\neq n}\bl_i(\bt_i)\bl_i^{\mathrm H}(\bt_i)+N\bI_M$ and $\br_n\triangleq\sum_{i\neq n}\bl_i(\bt_i)e^{j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\bt_i^{\mathrm T}\ba_{\mathrm e}}$ do not depend on $\bt_n$. Applying the Sherman--Morrison formula~\cite{Horn2013} to~\eqref{eq:Phin} yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sm}
\bOm(\bt)=\bGam_n^{-1}-\frac{\bGam_n^{-1}\bl_n(\bt_n)\bl_n^{\mathrm H}(\bt_n)\bGam_n^{-1}}{1+\bl_n^{\mathrm H}(\bt_n)\bGam_n^{-1}\bl_n(\bt_n)}.
\end{equation}
Given $\bGam_n^{-1}$ and $\br_n$, \eqref{eq:Phin}--\eqref{eq:sm} allow $\omega_m$, $c_m$, $d$, and thus $F(\bt_n)$ to be evaluated at a candidate position with only $\mathcal O(M^2)$ operations, compared with $\mathcal O(NM^2+M^3)$ operations for computing them from scratch.

\textit{2) Stage 1 (grid search):} Let $\mathcal G\subset\mathcal C$ denote a uniform grid over $\mathcal C$, $\bt_n^{\mathrm{cur}}$ the current position of the $n$-th MA, and $\mathcal G_n\triangleq\{\bt_n\in\mathcal G\,|\,\|\bt_n-\bt_i\|\ge\Dmin,\ \forall i\neq n\}$ the set of grid points that are feasible for (P3.2.$n$). Using~\eqref{eq:sm}, we evaluate $F(\bt_n)$ at all candidates in $\mathcal G_n\cup\{\bt_n^{\mathrm{cur}}\}$ and obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gridsearch}
\bt_n^{(0)}=\arg\max_{\bt_n\in\mathcal G_n\cup\{\bt_n^{\mathrm{cur}}\}}F(\bt_n),
\end{equation}
which requires $\mathcal O(|\mathcal G|M^2)$ operations. The grid spacing is set to a fraction of the shortest period $\lambda/\max_{i,m\in\mathcal M\cup\{\mathrm e\}}\|\ba_{m}-\ba_i\|$ of the above phases, which is at least $\lambda/2$ because $\|\ba_i\|\le1$.

\textit{3) Stage 2 (MM refinement):} Starting from $\bt_n^{(0)}$ in~\eqref{eq:gridsearch}, we refine the position by the MM method~\cite{Sun2017}, where $\bt_n^{(k)}$ denotes the position of the $n$-th MA in the $k$-th MM iteration. To construct a surrogate function, we derive the gradient of $F(\bt_n)$. Let $u_n\in\{x_n,y_n\}$ denote the coordinate of $\bt_n$ along the axis $u\in\{x,y\}$, and define $\bLam_u\triangleq\diag(a_{1,u},\ldots,a_{M,u})$, where $a_{m,x}\triangleq\cos\theta_m\sin\phi_m$ and $a_{m,y}\triangleq\sin\theta_m$ are the entries of $\ba_m$, and $a_{\mathrm{e},u}$ is the corresponding entry of $\ba_{\mathrm e}$. From~\eqref{eq:Phin}, \eqref{eq:vn}, and the definition of $\bl_n$, the derivatives of $\bl_n$, $\bv$, and $\bOm$ with respect to $u_n$ are given by
\begin{align}
\dot{\bl}_n&\triangleq\frac{\partial\bl_n}{\partial u_n}=-j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\bLam_u\bl_n,\label{eq:dl}\\
\dot{\bv}&\triangleq\frac{\partial\bv}{\partial u_n}=j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\big(a_{\mathrm{e},u}\bI_M-\bLam_u\big)\bl_ne^{j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\bt_n^{\mathrm T}\ba_{\mathrm e}},\label{eq:dv}\\
\dot{\bOm}&\triangleq\frac{\partial\bOm}{\partial u_n}=-\bOm\big(\dot{\bl}_n\bl_n^{\mathrm H}+\bl_n\dot{\bl}_n^{\mathrm H}\big)\bOm,\label{eq:dOm}
\end{align}
where~\eqref{eq:dOm} follows from $\partial\bPhi^{-1}=-\bPhi^{-1}(\partial\bPhi)\bPhi^{-1}$. Using~\eqref{eq:dl}--\eqref{eq:dOm}, the derivatives $\dot\omega_m\triangleq\partial\omega_m/\partial u_n$, $\dot c_m\triangleq\partial c_m/\partial u_n$, and $\dot d\triangleq\partial d/\partial u_n$ are derived in closed form in Appendix~\ref{app:grad}. Then, the derivatives of the effective channel gains $\eta_m$ and $\eta_{\mathrm{e},m}=\xi_m/(q\psi+\zeta_{\mathrm e}^{-1})$ are given by $\dot\eta_m\triangleq\partial\eta_m/\partial u_n=-\eta_m\dot\omega_m/\omega_m$ and $\dot\eta_{\mathrm{e},m}\triangleq\partial\eta_{\mathrm{e},m}/\partial u_n=\kappa_{\mathrm e}\eta_{\mathrm{e},m}\big(\dot c_m/\xi_m-q\dot d/(q\psi+\zeta_{\mathrm e}^{-1})\big)$, respectively. With $F(\bt_n)=\sum_{m=1}^M\big[\log_2(1+\eta_mp_m)-\log_2(1+\eta_{\mathrm{e},m}p_m)\big]$, the entries of the gradient $\nabla_{\bt_n}F=[\partial F/\partial x_n,\ \partial F/\partial y_n]^{\mathrm T}$ are given by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dF}
\frac{\partial F}{\partial u_n}=\frac{1}{\ln2}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\bigg[\frac{p_m\dot\eta_m}{1+\eta_mp_m}-\frac{p_m\dot\eta_{\mathrm{e},m}}{1+\eta_{\mathrm{e},m}p_m}\bigg].
\end{equation}

Because $F(\bt_n)$ is twice continuously differentiable and $\mathcal C$ is compact, $\nabla_{\bt_n}F$ is Lipschitz continuous over $\mathcal C$. By the descent lemma~\cite[Lemma~5.7]{Beck2017}, for any $\delta_n>0$ no smaller than this Lipschitz constant, the following quadratic function is a lower bound on $F(\bt_n)$ that is tight at $\bt_n^{(k)}$:
\begin{align}
F(\bt_n)\ge\hat F_n(\bt_n|\bt_n^{(k)})\triangleq\ &F(\bt_n^{(k)})+\nabla_{\bt_n}F(\bt_n^{(k)})^{\mathrm T}\big(\bt_n-\bt_n^{(k)}\big)\nonumber\\
&-\frac{\delta_n}{2}\big\|\bt_n-\bt_n^{(k)}\big\|^2.\label{eq:surrogate}
\end{align}
As a global bound on this Lipschitz constant is generally loose and would lead to small MM steps, $\delta_n$ is determined by backtracking~\cite{Beck2017}: starting from $\max\{\delta_0,\delta_n^{\mathrm{pre}}/2\}$, where $\delta_0>0$ is a constant and $\delta_n^{\mathrm{pre}}$ is the previously accepted value, $\delta_n$ is doubled until the update in~\eqref{eq:P32n} satisfies $F(\bt_n^{(k+1)})\ge\hat F_n(\bt_n^{(k+1)}|\bt_n^{(k)})$, which holds once $\delta_n$ reaches the Lipschitz constant. \rev{Let $L$ denote a Lipschitz constant of $\nabla_{\bt_n}F$ over $\mathcal C$ that is valid for all $n$, all feasible positions of the other MAs, and all feasible $\bp$ and $q$, which exists because $F$ is twice continuously differentiable over a compact set. Since every trial value $\delta_n\ge L$ is accepted, the backtracking terminates after at most $1+\lceil\log_2(L/\delta_0)\rceil^+$ trials, and every accepted $\delta_n$ satisfies $\delta_n\le\max\{\delta_0,2L\}$.}

For the minimum-distance constraints, since $\|\bt_n-\bt_i\|^2$ is convex with respect to $\bt_n$, replacing it with its first-order Taylor expansion at $\bt_n^{(k)}$~\cite{ZhengTCOM2025} yields the linear constraints
\begin{equation}\label{eq:linc}
\big(\bt_n^{(k)}-\bt_i\big)^{\mathrm T}\big(2\bt_n-\bt_n^{(k)}-\bt_i\big)\ge\Dmin^2,\quad\forall i\neq n.
\end{equation}
Since this expansion is a lower bound on $\|\bt_n-\bt_i\|^2$, the feasible set of~\eqref{eq:linc} is a convex subset of that of the minimum-distance constraints. Combining~\eqref{eq:surrogate} and~\eqref{eq:linc}, we update $\bt_n$ by solving
\begin{equation}\label{eq:P32n}
\bt_n^{(k+1)}=\arg\max_{\bt_n}\ \hat F_n(\bt_n|\bt_n^{(k)})\quad\mathrm{s.t.}\ \bt_n\in\mathcal C,\ \eqref{eq:linc}.
\end{equation}
Completing the square shows that~\eqref{eq:P32n} is equivalent to projecting $\bt_n^{(k)}+\nabla_{\bt_n}F(\bt_n^{(k)})/\delta_n$ onto the convex polygon defined by $\mathcal C$ and~\eqref{eq:linc}. The solution is this point itself if it is feasible and otherwise lies on an edge or at a vertex of the polygon. Hence, enumerating the projections onto the $N+3$ constraint lines and their pairwise intersections yields the exact solution. Since $\bt_n^{(k)}$ is feasible for~\eqref{eq:P32n}, the update satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mono}
F(\bt_n^{(k+1)})\ge\hat F_n(\bt_n^{(k+1)}|\bt_n^{(k)})\ge\hat F_n(\bt_n^{(k)}|\bt_n^{(k)})=F(\bt_n^{(k)}),
\end{equation}
where the first inequality is ensured by the backtracking. Moreover, $\bt_n^{(k+1)}$ is feasible for (P3.2.$n$) due to~\eqref{eq:linc}. The MM iterations are repeated until the increase of $F$ normalized by $\max\{1,|F|\}$ falls below a tolerance $\epsilon_{\mathrm{in}}$ or the maximum number of iterations $\NMM$ is reached.

\subsection{Overall Algorithm, Convergence, and Complexity}\label{sec:overall}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{AO Algorithm for (P3)}\label{alg:ao}
\small\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Statistical CSI, $\Ptot$, $\mathcal C$, $\Dmin$, $Q$, $\mathcal G$, $\delta_0$, $\epsilon_{\mathrm{in}}$, $\NMM$, $\epsilon$, $\NAO$.
\Ensure $\bt^\star$, $\bp^\star$, and $q^\star$.
\State Initialize feasible positions $\bt$ and obtain $(\bp,q)$ by Algorithm~\ref{alg:power}.
\Repeat
  \For{$n=1$ to $N$}
    \State Compute $\bGam_n^{-1}$ and $\br_n$.
    \State \textit{Stage 1:} Obtain $\bt_n^{(0)}$ by~\eqref{eq:gridsearch}, and set $k=0$.
    \Repeat
      \State \textit{Stage 2:} Obtain $\bt_n^{(k+1)}$ by~\eqref{eq:P32n} with backtracking on $\delta_n$, and set $k\leftarrow k+1$.
    \Until{the normalized increase of $F$ is below $\epsilon_{\mathrm{in}}$ or $k=\NMM$}
    \State Set $\bt_n\leftarrow\bt_n^{(k)}$.
  \EndFor
  \State Obtain $(\bp',q')$ by Algorithm~\ref{alg:power} for the updated $\bt$.
  \State Set $(\bp,q)\leftarrow(\bp',q')$ if $F(\bt,\bp',q')\ge F(\bt,\bp,q)$.
\Until{the increase of $F$ normalized by $\max\{1,|F|\}$ is below $\epsilon$ or $\NAO$ iterations are reached}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:ao} summarizes the overall AO algorithm for (P3), which terminates when the normalized increase of the objective value falls below a tolerance $\epsilon$ or after $\NAO$ iterations.

\textit{Convergence:} In each iteration of Algorithm~\ref{alg:ao}, the grid search in~\eqref{eq:gridsearch} does not decrease the objective value of (P3) because the current position is among the candidates, and neither do the MM updates in~\eqref{eq:P32n} due to~\eqref{eq:mono}. The power allocation is likewise accepted only if the objective value does not decrease, so that the objective value is nondecreasing over the iterations. Since it is also upper-bounded by $\sum_{m=1}^M\log_2(1+\zeta_m(N-M)(\kappa_m+1)\Ptot)$ according to~\eqref{eq:etabound} and $p_m\le\Ptot$, the objective value is guaranteed to converge. Note that this guarantee concerns the objective value only and does not imply convergence to a stationary point of (P3).

\textit{Initialization:} As (P3) is nonconvex, Algorithm~\ref{alg:ao} is run from several initializations, e.g., the positions of the FPA array and random feasible positions, and the best solution is retained. The grid search in Stage~1 further weakens the dependence on the initial positions, since each MA can move to any feasible grid point in every iteration.

\textit{Complexity:} In each AO iteration, forming $\bPhi$ and $\bOm$ requires $\mathcal O(NM^2+M^3)$ operations. For each MA, $\bGam_n^{-1}$ is obtained from $\bOm$ by a rank-one update. Stage~1 requires $\mathcal O(|\mathcal G|(M^2+N))$ operations, including the feasibility check of the grid points. In Stage~2, the gradient in~\eqref{eq:dF} and each evaluation of $F$ require $\mathcal O(M^2)$ operations based on~\eqref{eq:sm}, and solving~\eqref{eq:P32n} by enumeration requires $\mathcal O(N^3)$ operations. Algorithm~\ref{alg:power} requires $\mathcal O(QN_{\mathrm b}M)$ operations, where $N_{\mathrm b}$ is the number of bisection iterations. The complexity of Algorithm~\ref{alg:ao} per initialization is therefore $\mathcal O\big(\NAO\big(NM^2+M^3+N|\mathcal G|(M^2+N)+N\NMM N_{\delta}(M^2+N^3)+QN_{\mathrm b}M\big)\big)$, where $N_{\delta}$ is the average number of backtracking trials for $\delta_n$. Unlike the stochastic optimization in~\cite{ZhaoTWC2021}, this complexity does not depend on the number of channel realizations. Since Algorithm~\ref{alg:ao} relies on the slowly varying statistical CSI, it is executed once per frame, whereas the ZF precoding and AN generation in each CI require $\mathcal O(NM^2+M^3)$ operations.

\subsection{Simulation Setup and Benchmarks}\label{sec:sim_setup}

Unless otherwise specified, we set $N=8$, $M=3$, $A=4\lambda$, $\Dmin=\lambda/2$, $\kappa_m=\kappa_{\mathrm e}=10$~dB, $\beta_m=\beta_{\mathrm e}=1$, and $\sigma_m^2=\sigma_{\mathrm e}^2=\sigma^2$, so that $\Ptot/\sigma^2$ denotes the transmit SNR, which is set to $20$~dB by default. The elevation and azimuth AoDs of the LUs are independently and uniformly distributed within $[-\pi/3,\pi/3]$. As in~\cite{ChenArxiv2026}, Eve is located in a direction close to that of LU~$1$, which represents the challenging case discussed in Section~\ref{sec:intro}. Its AoDs are \rev{$\theta_{\mathrm e}=\theta_1+0.1\cos\tilde\phi$ and $\phi_{\mathrm e}=\phi_1+0.1\sin\tilde\phi$ in radians, i.e., they deviate from those of LU~$1$ by $0.1$~rad in the elevation--azimuth plane in the direction specified by $\tilde\phi$,} which is uniformly distributed over $[0,2\pi)$. \rev{For this offset,} the normalized LoS correlation $\rho_1(\bt)$ \rev{of the FPA exceeds $0.94$, so that} Eve lies well within the main lobe of the beam steered toward LU~$1$.

For Algorithm~\ref{alg:ao}, the grid $\mathcal G$ in Stage~1 has a spacing of $\lambda/10$, which is at most one fifth of the shortest phase period in Section~\ref{sec:position} and yields $|\mathcal G|=1681$ for $A=4\lambda$. Halving this spacing changes the average ESSR of the proposed design by only \rev{$0.04\%$} over $20$ AoD realizations. The remaining parameters are $\delta_0=1$, $\epsilon_{\mathrm{in}}=10^{-6}$, $\NMM=20$, $\epsilon=10^{-4}$, $\NAO=50$, $Q=41$, and $N_{\mathrm b}=60$. Algorithm~\ref{alg:ao} is initialized with a $2\times(N/2)$ uniform planar array (UPA) with $\lambda/2$ spacing and with two sets of random feasible positions, and the best solution is selected.

All the results are averaged over $100$ random AoD realizations, which are shared by all the schemes so that the comparisons are paired. At $20$~dB, the standard error of the average ESSR of the proposed design is \rev{$0.55\%$} of its value, and the standard error of the average ESSR difference between any two schemes does not exceed \rev{$0.22$}~bps/Hz. Since the closed-form approximations are used only for optimization, the ergodic rates of each design are evaluated with the exact SINRs in~\eqref{eq:snr} and~\eqref{eq:sinre} over \rev{$10^3$} channel realizations ($10^4$ in Fig.~\ref{fig:acc}).

\rev{The proposed design is compared with the following benchmarks. The first four adopt the power allocation in Algorithm~\ref{alg:power}, so that their performance differences from the proposed design result only from the antenna positions, whereas the last two isolate the gains of the power allocation and of the AN:}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textit{Rate-oriented MA:} The antenna positions maximize the approximate ergodic sum rate $\sum_{m=1}^M\log_2(1+\eta_m(\bt)\Ptot/M)$ of the LUs with equal power and ignore Eve~\cite{ZhengTCOM2025}. For a fair comparison, this problem is tackled by the position update in Section~\ref{sec:position} with the same initializations. \rev{This benchmark exploits only the decorrelation among the LUs in Section~\ref{sec:insight}.}
\item \rev{\textit{Eve-nulling MA:} The antenna positions minimize the LoS correlations $\|\Hb^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\gb(\bt)\|^2$ between Eve and the LUs, i.e., they drive $\bv(\bt)$ toward $\mathbf 0$, and ignore the correlations among the LUs. This problem is tackled by the grid search of Stage~1 with the same initializations, repeated until no antenna moves. Together with the rate-oriented MA, this benchmark separates the two groups of secrecy mechanisms in Section~\ref{sec:insight}.}
\item \rev{\textit{APS:} As in the alternating position selection of~\cite{HuTVT2025}, the antennas are restricted to the points of a grid with $\lambda/2$ spacing over $\mathcal C$, and each antenna is moved alternately to the best grid point by exhaustive search. Here, the objective of (P3) is used, i.e., APS is Algorithm~\ref{alg:ao} with a grid spacing of $\lambda/2$ and without Stage~2, which quantifies the gain of the continuous antenna positions over discrete ones.}
\item \textit{FPA:} The BS adopts the $2\times(N/2)$ UPA with $\lambda/2$ spacing.
\item \rev{\textit{Equal power:} The MAs and the FPA allocate the same power $p_m=q=\Ptot/N$ to all the $N$ spatial streams without power optimization, where the antenna positions of the MAs are optimized by Algorithm~\ref{alg:ao} under this power allocation. Since the power of an individual LU cannot be set to zero, the operator $[\cdot]^+$ is retained in the objective of these two benchmarks, and the gradient in Stage~2 is computed over the LUs with positive secrecy rates.}
\item \rev{\textit{No AN:} The MAs and the FPA transmit no AN, i.e., $q=0$, where the antenna positions of the MAs are optimized by Algorithm~\ref{alg:ao} with $q=0$.}
\end{itemize}

\rev{\textit{1) Accuracy of the approximations:}} Fig.~\ref{fig:acc} evaluates the accuracy of~\eqref{eq:Rm} and~\eqref{eq:Re} at the designs obtained by Algorithm~\ref{alg:ao}. The approximate wiretap rate in~\eqref{eq:Re} agrees with the Monte Carlo results within maximum absolute errors of $0.052$ and $0.013$~bps/Hz over the SNRs in Fig.~\ref{fig:acc}(a) and the Rician factors in Fig.~\ref{fig:acc}(b), respectively, which confirms its accuracy at the optimized antenna positions. In contrast, \eqref{eq:Rm} underestimates the ergodic sum rate of the LUs, and the gap grows with the Rician factor. This underestimation stems from Jensen's inequality and from the central Wishart approximation in Section~\ref{sec:lu}, which is exact for Rayleigh fading but overestimates $\E\{[(\bH^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\bH(\bt))^{-1}]_{m,m}\}$ by a factor approaching $N/(N-M)$ as the LoS components dominate. As a result, the approximate ESSR $\sum_{m=1}^M\hat R_m^{\mathrm{sec}}$ is lower than the actual ESSR in all tested cases, e.g., $18.98$ versus $20.75$~bps/Hz at $20$~dB. Because this gap varies by less than \rev{$0.2$~bps/Hz across the schemes in Fig.~\ref{fig:snr}(a)} at $20$~dB (from \rev{$1.57$ to $1.76$}~bps/Hz), which is much smaller than the ESSR gains of the proposed design, the approximation preserves the ranking of the designs at $20$~dB. \rev{Over all the SNRs in Fig.~\ref{fig:snr}(a), the approximate and actual rankings of the designs coincide.} Hence, the approximation remains a suitable design objective.

\rev{\textit{2) Convergence of Algorithm~\ref{alg:ao}:}} Fig.~\ref{fig:conv} shows the objective value of (P3) versus the number of iterations of Algorithm~\ref{alg:ao}, which is run without the stopping criterion. In all the settings, the objective value increases monotonically, attains more than \rev{$95\%$} of its total increase within three iterations, and converges within about \rev{$15$} iterations. This fast convergence results from the grid search in Stage~1, which moves each MA directly to the best feasible grid point instead of letting it traverse the movable region through local updates. With the MM updates only, the objective value increases much more slowly and remains lower, because the MAs are trapped in the local maxima near their initial positions.

\rev{For comparison, we replace the quadratic surrogate in~\eqref{eq:surrogate} by that in~\cite{ZhengTCOM2025}, which bounds the LU terms with a closed-form curvature, and bound the Eve terms by a quadratic with their largest curvature over $\mathcal C$, which is computed numerically. With the same Stage~1, this surrogate attains an average objective value within $0.1\%$ of that of Algorithm~\ref{alg:ao} over the $100$ AoD realizations. However, its curvature is about $12$ times the value of $\delta_n$ accepted by the backtracking, so that Stage~2 requires $2.6$ times as many MM iterations, each of which solves a convex problem numerically instead of by the projection in~\eqref{eq:P32n}.}

Fig.~\ref{fig:snr}(a) shows that the proposed design achieves the highest ESSR over the entire SNR range, e.g., \rev{$14.8\%$ and $31.1\%$} higher than those of the rate-oriented MA and the FPA at $20$~dB, respectively. Although the rate-oriented MA exploits the same movable region, its gain over the FPA is less than half of that of the proposed design, because its positions only decorrelate the LUs, whereas the proposed positions also exploit the two Eve-related mechanisms in Section~\ref{sec:insight}. \rev{Averaged over the AoD realizations at $20$~dB, the proposed positions reduce the normalized LoS correlation $\rho_1(\bt)$ from $0.98$ for the FPA to $0.52$, which decreases the normalized information leakage $\xi_1(\bt)$ of LU~$1$ from $54.5$ to $24.7$ and increases the normalized AN power $\psi(\bt)$ received by Eve from $13.3$ to $58.3$. As a result, the Eve-nulling MA outperforms the rate-oriented MA for $\Ptot/\sigma^2\ge10$~dB, where the information leakage limits the secrecy rates, but not at $0$~dB, where the noise dominates, and the proposed design outperforms it by $8.0\%$ at $20$~dB. Hence, neither group of secrecy mechanisms suffices alone, and the proposed design balances them through the approximate ESSR. The proposed design outperforms APS by $0.5\%$ to $1.5\%$, i.e., the $\lambda/2$ grid captures most of the positioning gain in this setting, and the finer positions of Algorithm~\ref{alg:ao} provide the remainder.} At high SNR, the absolute ESSR gains of the proposed design approach constants, since the antenna positions improve the constant term rather than the slope of the ESSR (see Section~\ref{sec:insight}). \rev{These gains do not rely on the proximity of Eve to LU~$1$: if the AoDs of Eve are drawn independently of those of the LUs, the proposed design still achieves a $10.3\%$ higher ESSR than the rate-oriented MA at $20$~dB.}

Fig.~\ref{fig:snr}(b) shows that the optimized AN power fraction increases with the SNR and saturates. For $\Ptot/\sigma^2\ge10$~dB, the proposed design allocates less power to the AN than all the benchmarks, e.g., an AN power fraction of \rev{$0.27$ versus $0.47$} for the FPA at $20$~dB, because the optimized positions decrease $\Xi_m(\bt)$ in~\eqref{eq:highsnr}. \rev{The saturation levels agree with the high-SNR limits $1-\alpha^\star$ by~\eqref{eq:alphastar}, which are evaluated with $\Xi_m(\bt)$ of the designs at $30$~dB and shown by dotted lines for the proposed design and the FPA: although~\eqref{eq:alphastar} assumes equal power among the LUs, these limits exceed the AN power fractions at $30$~dB by at most $0.011$ for all the schemes. In particular, $\alpha^\star=0.71$ for the proposed design, whereas $\alpha^\star=0.51$ for the FPA is close to the equal split of~\cite{Zhou2010}. Thus, \eqref{eq:alphastar} explicitly links the antenna positions to the optimal AN power.} \rev{The AN power fraction of APS is close to that of the proposed design, and that of the Eve-nulling MA lies between those of the proposed design and the rate-oriented MA.}

\rev{Fig.~\ref{fig:power} shows the gain of the long-term power allocation. With equal power, the ESSR of the MAs decreases by $1.79$ to $1.94$~bps/Hz over the considered SNRs, whereas that of the FPA decreases by $1.83$~bps/Hz at $0$~dB and by $0.64$~bps/Hz at $30$~dB. At low SNR, both losses are large, because equal power allocates the fraction $(N-M)/N=0.625$ of the transmit power to the AN, whereas the optimized AN power fraction is close to zero when the noise dominates. At high SNR, the loss of the MAs is larger, because the optimized positions decrease $\Xi_m(\bt)$, so that the optimal AN power fraction, which is predicted by $1-\alpha^\star$ in Fig.~\ref{fig:snr}(b) \rev{($0.29$ versus $0.49$ for the FPA at $30$~dB)}, departs further from $0.625$. Nevertheless, the MAs with equal power outperform the FPA with the optimized power allocation for $\Ptot/\sigma^2\ge10$~dB. Therefore, the antenna positions and the long-term power allocation provide complementary gains, both of which are achieved by the proposed design.}

\rev{Fig.~\ref{fig:power} also shows the ESSRs without AN. For $\Ptot/\sigma^2\le5$~dB, the ESSRs with and without AN nearly coincide, since the optimized AN power fraction is then close to zero. As the SNR increases, the ESSRs without AN saturate, because the SINR of Eve grows with the transmit power as fast as those of the LUs, e.g., the AN increases the ESSR of the proposed design by $58.9\%$ and $121.1\%$ at $20$ and $30$~dB, respectively. Even without AN, the MAs outperform the FPA by $30.5\%$ to $31.2\%$ for $\Ptot/\sigma^2\ge5$~dB, because the optimized positions still reduce the information leakage and decorrelate the LUs. Hence, the antenna positions and the AN also provide complementary gains.}

\rev{\textit{1) Region size and number of MAs:}} Fig.~\ref{fig:region}(a) shows the ESSR versus the side length $A$ of the movable region, where the \rev{ESSR of the FPA does} not depend on $A$. The gain of the proposed design grows with $A$, because a larger region provides more spatial degrees of freedom for decorrelating the LoS components of Eve and the LUs. \rev{The ESSR of the rate-oriented MA increases much more slowly, since it exploits the enlarged region only to decorrelate the LUs, whereas the Eve-nulling MA benefits more from the larger region and outperforms the rate-oriented MA for $A\ge3\lambda$. APS remains within $0.7\%$ of the proposed design for all the region sizes.}

Fig.~\ref{fig:region}(b) shows the ESSR versus the number of MAs $N$. All the schemes benefit from more antennas, which provide a larger array gain and more dimensions for the AN. \rev{The gain of the proposed design over the FPA decreases with $N$, because the aperture of the FPA grows with $N$ and allows it} to separate Eve from LU~$1$ better even without position optimization. Nevertheless, the proposed design with $N=4$ MAs, for which the AN occupies a single dimension, outperforms the FPA with $N=12$ antennas, and that with $N=8$ MAs slightly outperforms the rate-oriented MA with $N=16$ MAs. The secrecy-aware positioning can thus substantially reduce the number of antennas and RF chains required for a target ESSR. \rev{For $N=4$, the Eve-nulling MA performs worse than the rate-oriented MA, because nulling Eve with four antennas leaves the three LUs strongly correlated, whereas it outperforms the rate-oriented MA for $N\ge8$. Meanwhile, the gap between APS and the proposed design shrinks from $2.6\%$ for $N=4$ to zero for $N=16$, since the discrete positions suffice when more antennas are available.}

\rev{\textit{2) Number of LUs:} Fig.~\ref{fig:param}(a) shows the ESSR versus the number of LUs $M$. For $M=1$, the rate-oriented MA achieves the same ESSR as the FPA, because the approximate rate of a single LU does not depend on $\bt$ (see Section~\ref{sec:insight}), whereas the proposed design achieves a $77.2\%$ higher ESSR by reducing the LoS correlation between Eve and the LU. The Eve-nulling MA and APS achieve the ESSR of the proposed design within $0.01$~bps/Hz, which confirms that minimizing $\rho_1(\bt)$ maximizes the approximate secrecy rate of a single LU. In this case, the FPA allocates $84\%$ of the transmit power to the AN, whereas the proposed design allocates $50\%$. As $M$ increases, the gain of the proposed design over the rate-oriented MA remains between $2.47$ and $3.39$~bps/Hz, whereas the Eve-nulling MA falls below the rate-oriented MA for $M\ge5$, since the decorrelation among more LUs becomes more important than that between Eve and the LUs. Likewise, the gain over APS grows to $3.5\%$ at $M=6$, since more LUs require finer antenna positions. The FPA cannot decorrelate the LoS components of more LUs, which increases the power cost of the ZF precoding, so that its ESSR saturates for $M\ge5$ and the gain over the FPA reaches $64.4\%$ at $M=6$.}

\rev{\textit{3) Rician factors:} We first consider a common Rician factor $\kappa=\kappa_m=\kappa_{\mathrm e}$, for which the figure is omitted for brevity. For $\kappa=-10$~dB, the ESSRs of all the schemes differ by less than $0.04$~bps/Hz, because the antenna positions affect~\eqref{eq:Rm} and~\eqref{eq:Re} only through the LoS components, which are then weak, e.g., by~\eqref{eq:etabound}, the positions can increase $\eta_m(\bt)$ by a factor of at most $\kappa_m+1$. As $\kappa$ increases, the ESSR of the FPA decreases, since Eve lies within the main lobe of its beam toward LU~$1$, whereas the proposed design increases its ESSR by decorrelating the dominant LoS components of Eve and the LUs. Consequently, the gains of the proposed design over the rate-oriented MA and the FPA grow to $19.3\%$ and $49.4\%$ at $\kappa=20$~dB, respectively. Fig.~\ref{fig:param}(b) isolates the Rician factor of Eve with $\kappa_m=10$~dB. As $\kappa_{\mathrm e}$ decreases, the statistical CSI of Eve carries less spatial information (see Section~\ref{sec:insight}), and the gain of the proposed design over the rate-oriented MA decreases to $1.0\%$ at $\kappa_{\mathrm e}=-10$~dB. As $\kappa_{\mathrm e}$ increases, the ESSRs of the rate-oriented MA and the FPA decrease, whereas that of the proposed design increases, so that this gain grows to $17.0\%$ at $\kappa_{\mathrm e}=20$~dB. The Eve-nulling MA outperforms the rate-oriented MA only for $\kappa_{\mathrm e}\ge5$~dB and remains $5.4\%$ to $8.5\%$ below the proposed design, since it sacrifices the decorrelation among the LUs even when the LoS component of Eve is weak. Hence, the Eve-aware positioning is most beneficial when the channel of Eve has a strong LoS component, which the rate-oriented design cannot exploit, \rev{and it has to be balanced with the decorrelation among the LUs.}}

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
% =============================================================================
This paper has investigated two-timescale secure transmission for an MA-enabled multiuser downlink with a passive Eve. Using the derived closed-form approximation of the ergodic wiretap rate, we have shown that, besides decorrelating the LUs, the MAs enhance secrecy by reducing the information leakage and increasing the AN received by Eve, even for a single LU. Based on this approximation, we have developed an AO algorithm, which combines secrecy water-filling with a two-stage position update and whose objective value is guaranteed to converge. Simulation results have shown that the proposed design outperforms \rev{the MA designs that exploit only one group of these mechanisms, the design with discrete antenna positions, and the FPA, with gains that grow with the Rician factor of Eve,} while reducing the AN power and the number of antennas required for secure transmission. Extending this design to multiple multi-antenna eavesdroppers, spatially correlated NLoS components, and imperfect CSI of the LUs\rev{, as well as the joint design of the AN covariance and the antenna positions,} is left for future work.

Let $\bU\in\mathbb C^{N\times(N-M)}$ have orthonormal columns that span the null space of $\bH^{\mathrm H}(\bt)$, so that $\PHp=\bU\bU^{\mathrm H}$ and $[\wb_m,\bU]$ has orthonormal columns. Therefore, the conditional relative variances of $X_m$ and $Y$ are small when $\kappa_{\mathrm e}\bar X_m$ and $\kappa_{\mathrm e}\bar Y+N-M$ are large, respectively.

\section{Derivation of~\eqref{eq:cm} and~\eqref{eq:d} and Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:eve}}\label{app:det}
% =============================================================================
For brevity, the argument $\bt$ is omitted. Since $\wb_m$ and $\PHp$ are invariant to the scaling of the columns of $\bH$, we assume $\bB=\bI_M$ without loss of generality, so that $\bH=\bL+\Htl$ by~\eqref{eq:Hdecomp}, and $\wb_m$ and $\PHp$ are given by~\eqref{eq:zf} and~\eqref{eq:an}, respectively. We also use $\E\{\bH^{\mathrm H}\bH\}=\bPhi$, $\E\{\Htl\}=\mathbf 0$, and $\E\{\Htl\mathbf A\Htl^{\mathrm H}\}=\tr(\mathbf A)\bI_N$ for any deterministic $\mathbf A$.

\textit{Derivation of~\eqref{eq:cm}:} Replacing $(\bH^{\mathrm H}\bH)^{-1}$ in the unnormalized ZF direction by $\bOm=\bPhi^{-1}$ yields $\wb_m\approx\bH\bOm\be_m/\|\bH\bOm\be_m\|$. Then, we approximate the mean of $|\gb^{\mathrm H}\bH\bOm\be_m|^2/\|\bH\bOm\be_m\|^2$ by the ratio of the means of its numerator and denominator, as in~\cite[Lemma~1]{ZhangJSTSP2014}. These are two separate approximations, since $\E\{(\bH^{\mathrm H}\bH)^{-1}\}\neq\bOm$ in general. Using $\bH=\bL+\Htl$ and $\bL^{\mathrm H}\gb=\bv$, we obtain
\begin{align}
\E\{|\gb^{\mathrm H}\bH\bOm\be_m|^2\}&=|\gb^{\mathrm H}\bL\bOm\be_m|^2+\|\gb\|^2\|\bOm\be_m\|^2\nonumber\\
&=\big|[\bOm\bv]_m\big|^2+N[\bOm^2]_{m,m},\label{eq:app1}\\
\E\{\|\bH\bOm\be_m\|^2\}&=\be_m^{\mathrm T}\bOm\bPhi\bOm\be_m=\omega_m,\label{eq:app2}
\end{align}
whose ratio is given by~\eqref{eq:cm}.

\textit{Derivation of~\eqref{eq:d}:} Replacing $(\bH^{\mathrm H}\bH)^{-1}$ in $\bI_N-\PHp$ by $\bOm$ and using $\E\{\Htl\bOm\Htl^{\mathrm H}\}=\tr(\bOm)\bI_N$ yield $\E\{\bI_N-\PHp\}\approx\bPi+\tr(\bOm)\bI_N$ with $\bPi\triangleq\bL\bOm\bL^{\mathrm H}$, where $\bPi$ and $\tr(\bOm)\bI_N$ represent the parts of the signal subspace induced by the LoS and NLoS components, respectively. However, this approximation lets the NLoS component capture the fraction $\tr(\bOm)$ of every direction, including those captured by the LoS component, so that $\gb^{\mathrm H}(\bI_N-\bPi-\tr(\bOm)\bI_N)\gb$ may become negative when $\gb$ is closely aligned with the column space of $\bL$. To avoid this double counting, we let the NLoS component capture a fraction $\chi\in[0,1]$ of the complement of $\bPi$ only, i.e.,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:app4}
\E\{\bI_N-\PHp\}\approx\bPi+\chi(\bI_N-\bPi).
\end{equation}
The fraction $\chi$ is determined by the identity $\tr(\PHp)=N-M$, which holds with probability one. Since $\tr(\bPi)=\tr\big(\bOm(\bPhi-N\bI_M)\big)=M-N\tr(\bOm)$, taking the trace of~\eqref{eq:app4} yields $M-N\tr(\bOm)+\chi\big(N-M+N\tr(\bOm)\big)=M$, i.e., $\chi=N\tr(\bOm)/(N-M+N\tr(\bOm))$. Therefore, $\E\{\PHp\}\approx(1-\chi)(\bI_N-\bPi)$, which yields~\eqref{eq:d} due to $\gb^{\mathrm H}\bPi\gb=\bv^{\mathrm H}\bOm\bv$.

\textit{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:eve}:} For the range, the matrix inversion lemma yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:app5}
\begin{aligned}\bI_N-\bPi&=\bI_N-\bL(\bL^{\mathrm H}\bL+N\bI_M)^{-1}\bL^{\mathrm H}\\&=(\bI_N+\bL\bL^{\mathrm H}/N)^{-1}\succ\mathbf 0.\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Together with $\bPi\succeq\mathbf 0$, this implies $\mathbf 0\preceq\bPi\prec\bI_N$. Moreover, $\bPhi\succeq N\bI_M$ implies $0<\tr(\bOm)\le M/N$, i.e., $1-\chi\in[1-M/N,1)$. Hence, $d=(1-\chi)\gb^{\mathrm H}(\bI_N-\bPi)\gb\in[0,N]$ due to $\|\gb\|^2=N$. For $c_m$, applying the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality to $|[\bOm\bv]_m|^2=|\gb^{\mathrm H}\bL\bOm\be_m|^2$ and using $N\ge1$ bound the numerator of~\eqref{eq:cm} as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:app6}
\big|[\bOm\bv]_m\big|^2+N[\bOm^2]_{m,m}\le N\be_m^{\mathrm T}\bOm\bPhi\bOm\be_m=N\omega_m,
\end{equation}
which yields $c_m\in[0,N]$. The exact counterparts also lie in $[0,N]$, since $\bar X_m,\bar Y\le\|\gb\|^2$ for the unit-norm $\wb_m$ and the projector $\PHp$.

If $\kappa_m=0$, $\forall m\in\mathcal M$, then $\bL=\mathbf 0$, $\bH=\Htl$, $\bPhi=N\bI_M$, and $\bPi=\mathbf 0$, so that~\eqref{eq:cm} and~\eqref{eq:d} reduce to $c_m=1$ and $d=N-M$. In this case, the distribution of $\Htl$ is invariant to left multiplication by any unitary matrix, which is inherited by $\wb_m$ and $\PHp$. As a result, $\wb_m$ is uniformly distributed on the unit sphere, which yields $\E\{\bar X_m\}=\|\gb\|^2/N=1$, and $\E\{\PHp\}$ commutes with every unitary matrix and thus equals $(1-M/N)\bI_N$ due to $\tr(\PHp)=N-M$, which yields $\E\{\bar Y\}=N-M$.

Finally, let $\kappa_1\to\infty$ with fixed ratios $\kappa_m/\kappa_1>0$, $m\in\mathcal M$, i.e., $\bK=\kappa_1\bK_0$ with the fixed diagonal matrix $\bK_0\triangleq\diag(1,\kappa_2/\kappa_1,\ldots,\kappa_M/\kappa_1)\succ\mathbf 0$, so that $\bL=\sqrt{\kappa_1}\bL_0$ with $\bL_0\triangleq\Hb\bK_0^{1/2}$, which has full column rank if the LoS components of the LUs are linearly independent. As $\kappa_1\to\infty$, $\bH/\sqrt{\kappa_1}\to\bL_0$ almost surely. Since $\wb_m$ and $\PHp$ are invariant to the scaling of $\bH$ and continuous at full-column-rank matrices, and $\bar X_m$ and $\bar Y$ are bounded by $N$, the dominated convergence theorem yields $\E\{\bar X_m\}\to|\gb^{\mathrm H}\wb_m^{\infty}|^2$ and $\E\{\bar Y\}\to\gb^{\mathrm H}\mathbf P_{\infty}^\perp\gb$, where $\wb_m^{\infty}$ and $\mathbf P_{\infty}^\perp$ are given by~\eqref{eq:zf} and~\eqref{eq:an}, respectively, with $\bH(\bt)$ replaced by $\bL_0$. On the other hand, $\kappa_1\bOm\to(\bL_0^{\mathrm H}\bL_0)^{-1}$, $\bPi\to\bI_N-\mathbf P_{\infty}^\perp$, and $\tr(\bOm)\to0$. Hence, $d$ converges to $\gb^{\mathrm H}\mathbf P_{\infty}^\perp\gb$, and $c_m$ converges to $|[(\bL_0^{\mathrm H}\bL_0)^{-1}\bL_0^{\mathrm H}\gb]_m|^2/[(\bL_0^{\mathrm H}\bL_0)^{-1}]_{m,m}=|\gb^{\mathrm H}\wb_m^{\infty}|^2$, since $N[\bOm^2]_{m,m}$ decays as $\mathcal O(\kappa_1^{-2})$ whereas $\omega_m$ decays as $\mathcal O(\kappa_1^{-1})$. This completes the proof.

\section{Derivatives of $\omega_m$, $c_m$, and $d$}\label{app:grad}
% =============================================================================
Let $\tau_m\triangleq[\bOm^2]_{m,m}$ and $\check{\bv}\triangleq\bOm\bv$. Since $\bOm$ is Hermitian, \eqref{eq:dOm} can be rewritten as $\dot{\bOm}=-(\bOm\dot{\bl}_n)(\bOm\bl_n)^{\mathrm H}-(\bOm\bl_n)(\bOm\dot{\bl}_n)^{\mathrm H}$, which yields
\begin{align}
\dot\omega_m&=-2\RE\big\{[\bOm\dot{\bl}_n]_m[\bOm\bl_n]_m^*\big\},\label{eq:a1}\\
\dot\tau_m&=-2\RE\big\{[\bOm^2\dot{\bl}_n]_m[\bOm\bl_n]_m^*+[\bOm^2\bl_n]_m[\bOm\dot{\bl}_n]_m^*\big\},\label{eq:a2}\\
\frac{\partial\tr(\bOm)}{\partial u_n}&=-2\RE\big\{\bl_n^{\mathrm H}\bOm^2\dot{\bl}_n\big\},\label{eq:a3}\\
\frac{\partial(\bv^{\mathrm H}\bOm\bv)}{\partial u_n}&=2\RE\big\{\check{\bv}^{\mathrm H}\dot{\bv}-(\check{\bv}^{\mathrm H}\dot{\bl}_n)(\bl_n^{\mathrm H}\check{\bv})\big\},\label{eq:a6}
\end{align}
where~\eqref{eq:a2} and~\eqref{eq:a6} follow from $\dot\tau_m=2\RE\{[\bOm\dot{\bOm}]_{m,m}\}$ and $\bv^{\mathrm H}\dot{\bOm}\bv=-2\RE\{(\check{\bv}^{\mathrm H}\dot{\bl}_n)(\bl_n^{\mathrm H}\check{\bv})\}$, respectively. Since $d=(1-\chi)(N-\bv^{\mathrm H}\bOm\bv)$ with $1-\chi=(N-M)/(N-M+N\tr(\bOm))$ by Appendix~\ref{app:det}, differentiating~\eqref{eq:cm} and~\eqref{eq:d} yields
\begin{align}
\dot{\check{\bv}}&=-\bOm\dot{\bl}_n\big(\bl_n^{\mathrm H}\check{\bv}\big)-\bOm\bl_n\big(\dot{\bl}_n^{\mathrm H}\check{\bv}\big)+\bOm\dot{\bv},\label{eq:a4}\\
\dot c_m&=\frac{2\RE\big\{[\check{\bv}]_m^*[\dot{\check{\bv}}]_m\big\}+N\dot\tau_m-c_m\dot\omega_m}{\omega_m},\label{eq:a5}\\
\dot d&=-(1-\chi)\frac{\partial(\bv^{\mathrm H}\bOm\bv)}{\partial u_n}-\frac{Nd}{N-M+N\tr(\bOm)}\,\frac{\partial\tr(\bOm)}{\partial u_n}.\label{eq:a7}
\end{align}
Substituting~\eqref{eq:a1}, \eqref{eq:a5}, and~\eqref{eq:a7} into~\eqref{eq:dF} yields $\nabla_{\bt_n}F$. All quantities in~\eqref{eq:a1}--\eqref{eq:a7} follow from matrix-vector products with $\bOm$, requiring $\mathcal O(M^2)$ operations.
