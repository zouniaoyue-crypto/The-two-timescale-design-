\begin{abstract}
Movable antennas (MAs) can reshape the spatial correlation between the channels of legitimate users (LUs) and an eavesdropper (Eve), which provides a new degree of freedom for physical layer security. However, most existing MA-aided secure designs reposition the antennas based on instantaneous channel state information (CSI), which is hindered by the antenna movement delay, the overhead of acquiring the CSI over the entire movable region, and the unavailability of the instantaneous CSI of a passive Eve. To address these issues, this paper proposes a two-timescale secure transmission framework for an MA-enabled multiuser downlink system. In the large timescale, the antenna positions and the long-term power allocation among the confidential signals and artificial noise (AN) are optimized based on the statistical CSI of the LUs and Eve. In the small timescale, zero-forcing (ZF) precoding and null-space AN are designed based on the instantaneous CSI of the LUs only. We prove that the information leakage and the AN received by Eve are conditionally independent given the channels of the LUs, and derive a closed-form approximation of the ergodic wiretap rate of Eve based on approximations of the average leakage and AN energies that are exact in the non-line-of-sight (NLoS)-dominant regime and asymptotically exact in the line-of-sight (LoS)-dominant regime. The resulting approximate ergodic secrecy sum rate (ESSR) depends on the antenna positions only through three geometric quantities, which characterize the decorrelation among the LUs, the information leakage, and the AN received by Eve. Then, an alternating optimization algorithm is developed to maximize the ESSR, where the power allocation admits a secrecy water-filling structure and the antenna positions are updated element-wise via majorization--minimization. Numerical results show that the proposed design outperforms both the rate-oriented two-timescale MA design and fixed-position antenna arrays, e.g., by $@@G20_RATE@@\%$ and $@@G20_FPA@@\%$ at a transmit signal-to-noise ratio of $20$~dB, respectively, improves the secrecy rate even for a single LU, and reduces the AN power required for secure transmission.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% =============================================================================
\IEEEPARstart{P}{hysical} layer security (PLS) exploits the randomness and the spatial characteristics of wireless channels to protect confidential messages against eavesdropping, which is a promising complement to upper-layer cryptography~\cite{Wyner1975,Mukherjee2014,WuJSAC2018}. In multi-antenna systems, secure precoding and artificial noise (AN) are two main enablers of PLS~\cite{Goel2008,Zhou2010}. However, their performance is fundamentally limited by how well the legitimate users (LUs) and the eavesdroppers can be spatially separated. When an eavesdropper (Eve) is located in a direction close to that of an LU and the line-of-sight (LoS) propagation is dominant, the channels of the LU and Eve are highly correlated. In this case, a conventional fixed-position antenna (FPA) array can hardly steer the information-bearing beams away from Eve while maintaining the beamforming gain at the LU.

Movable antennas (MAs), also known as fluid antennas, have recently emerged as a promising technology to overcome this limitation~\cite{ZhuMag2024,ZhuTWC2024Model,WongTWC2021}. By adjusting the antenna positions within a given region via flexible cables and drivers, MAs reshape the channel correlation among different receivers without additional radio-frequency (RF) chains~\cite{MaTWC2024,ZhuTWC2024MU,ZhuCL2023}. This capability is particularly appealing for PLS, since the antenna positions can be designed to decorrelate the channels of the LUs from that of Eve. Motivated by this, MA-aided secure transmission has attracted growing research interest. For example, the authors of~\cite{HuSPL2024} jointly optimized the transmit beamforming and the positions of an MA array to maximize the secrecy rate with perfect channel state information (CSI). MA-aided secure transmission without the instantaneous CSI of the eavesdroppers was investigated in~\cite{HuTMC2024}, where the statistical CSI of the eavesdroppers was exploited. More recently, for a multiple-input multiple-output multiple-eavesdropper-antenna (MIMOME) MA system in which only the LoS component and the statistics of the non-LoS (NLoS) component of the eavesdropper's channel are known, a deterministic equivalent of the ergodic secrecy rate was derived via random matrix theory in~\cite{XieArxiv2026}, and a sensing-assisted secure design was developed in~\cite{ChenArxiv2026}, where the MA positions were optimized to enhance both the estimation of the direction of Eve and the secrecy rate.

Despite these advances, most of the above works optimize the antenna positions based on the instantaneous CSI of at least the LUs, which requires the antennas to be repositioned in each channel coherence interval (CI) under fast fading. Such single-timescale designs are difficult to implement in practice for three reasons. First, the mechanical movement of antennas incurs non-negligible delay and energy consumption, and thus cannot keep pace with fast channel variations. Second, optimizing the antenna positions based on instantaneous CSI requires the CSI over the entire movable region. Under Rician fading, the NLoS components at different positions cannot be characterized by a few path parameters, and thus acquiring them requires the antennas to traverse the region~\cite{ZhengTCOM2025,HuTVT2025}. Third, a passive Eve does not transmit, and thus its instantaneous CSI is generally unavailable at the transmitter.

To reduce the implementation overhead, two-timescale designs have been proposed for MA-enabled systems~\cite{ZhengTCOM2025,HuTVT2025}, following the two-timescale paradigm developed for intelligent reflecting surfaces~\cite{ZhaoTWC2021}. In these designs, the antenna positions are optimized based on the statistical CSI in the large timescale, whereas the transmit or receive beamforming is designed based on the instantaneous CSI of the resulting effective channels in the small timescale. However, these works focused on the ergodic sum rate, and the two-timescale design of MA-enabled secure transmission has not been investigated yet. In particular, how to exploit the statistical CSI of a passive Eve to position the MAs, and how the MAs interact with AN in the two-timescale framework, remain open problems.

Extending the two-timescale MA design to secure transmission is not straightforward, since the role of the antenna positions fundamentally changes in the presence of Eve. First, since the instantaneous CSI of Eve is unknown, the achievable ergodic secrecy rate involves the ergodic wiretap rate of Eve, which is determined by the random precoding directions and AN subspace induced by the instantaneous CSI of the LUs. The existing ergodic-rate approximations for zero-forcing (ZF) precoding~\cite{ZhangJSTSP2014,ZhengTCOM2025,HuTVT2025} characterize only the signal power received by the intended users and cannot be applied to Eve. Second, in the rate-oriented design, the antenna positions improve the ergodic rate only by decorrelating the channels of the users, and thus bring no gain for a single user~\cite{ZhengTCOM2025}. In contrast, in secure transmission, the antenna positions also determine the information leakage to Eve and the AN received by Eve. Third, the power allocation between the confidential signals and the AN is coupled with the antenna positions, since reducing the channel correlation between the LUs and Eve changes the amount of AN needed.

Motivated by the above, this paper investigates the two-timescale design of MA-enabled secure multiuser downlink transmission in the presence of a passive Eve. The main contributions are summarized as follows.
\begin{itemize}
\item We propose a two-timescale secure transmission framework for MA-enabled multiuser systems. In the large timescale, the antenna positions and the long-term power allocation among the confidential signals and AN are optimized based on the statistical CSI of the LUs and Eve. In the small timescale, ZF precoding and null-space AN are designed based on the instantaneous CSI of the LUs, which requires neither the instantaneous CSI of Eve nor frequent antenna movement.
\item We derive a closed-form approximation of the ergodic wiretap rate of Eve. Specifically, we prove that the information leakage and the AN received by Eve are conditionally independent given the channels of the LUs, and develop approximations of their average LoS energies that are exact in the NLoS-dominant regime and asymptotically exact in the LoS-dominant regime. Combined with the existing ergodic-rate approximation for ZF precoding, the resulting approximate ergodic secrecy sum rate (ESSR) depends on the antenna positions only through three geometric quantities, which reveals that the MAs enhance secrecy by decorrelating the LUs, reducing the information leakage, and increasing the AN received by Eve. In particular, different from the rate-oriented design, the MAs enhance the secrecy performance even for a single LU.
\item We formulate an ESSR maximization problem by jointly optimizing the antenna positions and the long-term power allocation, and develop an alternating optimization (AO) algorithm to solve it. The power allocation subproblem admits a secrecy water-filling solution combined with a one-dimensional search over the AN power, which switches off the LUs with non-positive secrecy rates. The antenna positions are optimized in an element-wise manner by the majorization--minimization (MM) method with analytical gradients. The proposed algorithm converges monotonically, and its complexity does not depend on the number of channel realizations.
\item Numerical results validate the accuracy of the derived approximation and show that, at a transmit signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) of $20$~dB, the proposed design improves the ESSR by $@@G20_RATE@@\%$, $@@G20_SPA@@\%$, and $@@G20_FPA@@\%$ compared with the rate-oriented two-timescale MA design~\cite{ZhengTCOM2025}, a sparse array spanning the movable region, and a compact $\lambda/2$ array, respectively, while requiring considerably less AN power. The secrecy gain persists for a single LU, for which the rate-oriented design brings no gain. Moreover, the per-user power allocation is shown to be beneficial for LUs with heterogeneous large-scale fading at low-to-moderate SNR.
\end{itemize}

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section~\ref{sec:model} presents the system model and formulates the two-timescale ESSR maximization problem. Section~\ref{sec:analysis} specifies the ZF precoding and null-space AN and derives the approximate ESSR. Section~\ref{sec:opt} develops the two-timescale optimization algorithm. Section~\ref{sec:sim} provides numerical results, and Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes this paper.

As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:system}, we consider the downlink of a multiuser system, where a base station (BS) equipped with $N$ MAs serves $M$ single-antenna LUs in the presence of a single-antenna passive Eve. The LUs and the MAs are indexed by $m\in\mathcal M\triangleq\{1,\ldots,M\}$ and $n\in\mathcal N\triangleq\{1,\ldots,N\}$, respectively, where $N\ge M+1$ is assumed so that AN can be transmitted in the null space of the LU channels. The MAs are connected to the RF chains via flexible cables, which allows them to move within a two-dimensional (2D) square region $\mathcal C\triangleq[-A/2,A/2]\times[-A/2,A/2]$~\cite{ZhuMag2024,ZhuTWC2024Model}. The position of the $n$-th MA is denoted by $\bt_n\triangleq[x_n,y_n]^T\in\mathcal C$, and the positions of all MAs are collected in $\bt\triangleq[\bt_1^T,\ldots,\bt_N^T]^T\in\mathbb R^{2N\times1}$. To avoid the coupling effect between adjacent antennas, any two MAs are separated by at least a minimum distance $\Dmin$, i.e., $\|\bt_n-\bt_i\|\ge\Dmin$, $\forall n\neq i$.

Since the size of $\mathcal C$ is much smaller than the signal propagation distance, the far-field condition holds between the BS and the receivers. As a result, moving the MAs changes only the phase of the LoS path, whereas the angle of departure (AoD) and the amplitude of the LoS path remain unchanged~\cite{ZhuTWC2024Model}. Let $\theta_m\in[-\pi/2,\pi/2]$ and $\phi_m\in[-\pi/2,\pi/2]$ denote the elevation and azimuth AoDs of the LoS path from the BS to LU~$m$, respectively, and define the corresponding wave vector $\ba_m\triangleq[\cos\theta_m\sin\phi_m,\ \sin\theta_m]^T$. Then, the propagation distance difference of the LoS path between position $\bt_n$ and the origin of $\mathcal C$ is $\bt_n^T\ba_m$, and the LoS component of the channel from the BS to LU $m$ is given by~\cite{ZhengTCOM2025}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hbar}
\hb_m(\bt)\triangleq\Big[e^{j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\bt_1^T\ba_m},\ldots,e^{j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\bt_N^T\ba_m}\Big]^T\in\mathbb C^{N\times1},
\end{equation}
where $\lambda$ is the carrier wavelength. Following~\cite{ZhengTCOM2025,HuTVT2025}, the channel from the BS to LU $m$ is modeled as Rician fading, i.e.,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hm}
\bh_m(\bt)=\sqrt{\frac{\kappa_m\beta_m}{\kappa_m+1}}\,\hb_m(\bt)+\sqrt{\frac{\beta_m}{\kappa_m+1}}\,\htl_m,
\end{equation}
where $\beta_m$ is the large-scale fading coefficient, $\kappa_m\ge0$ is the Rician factor, and $\htl_m\sim\CN(\mathbf 0,\bI_N)$ is the NLoS component.\footnote{As in~\cite{ZhengTCOM2025,HuTVT2025}, the NLoS components at different antennas are assumed to be independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.), which is reasonable in rich-scattering environments with $\Dmin\ge\lambda/2$. Spatially correlated NLoS components can be incorporated at the cost of more involved expressions.} Similarly, the channel from the BS to Eve is given by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:g}
\bg(\bt)=\sqrt{\frac{\kappa_e\beta_e}{\kappa_e+1}}\,\gb(\bt)+\sqrt{\frac{\beta_e}{\kappa_e+1}}\,\gtl,
\end{equation}
where $\beta_e$, $\kappa_e$, and $\gtl\sim\CN(\mathbf 0,\bI_N)$ are defined similarly to those in~\eqref{eq:hm}, and $\gb(\bt)$ is given by~\eqref{eq:hbar} with $\ba_m$ replaced by $\ba_e\triangleq[\cos\theta_e\sin\phi_e,\ \sin\theta_e]^T$, with $\theta_e$ and $\phi_e$ being the elevation and azimuth AoDs of Eve, respectively. The NLoS components $\htl_1,\ldots,\htl_M$ and $\gtl$ are mutually independent. For convenience, we define $\bH(\bt)\triangleq[\bh_1(\bt),\ldots,\bh_M(\bt)]\in\mathbb C^{N\times M}$, $\Hb(\bt)\triangleq[\hb_1(\bt),\ldots,\hb_M(\bt)]$, $\Htl\triangleq[\htl_1,\ldots,\htl_M]$, and $\bK\triangleq\diag(\kappa_1,\ldots,\kappa_M)$. Note that the antenna positions affect the channels only through the LoS components $\Hb(\bt)$ and $\gb(\bt)$.

Under Rician fading, the NLoS components at different antenna positions are random. Acquiring the instantaneous CSI over the entire region $\mathcal C$ would require the MAs to traverse the region, which incurs prohibitive antenna movement delay and channel training overhead~\cite{ZhengTCOM2025,HuTVT2025}. Moreover, since Eve is passive, the instantaneous CSI of its channel is unavailable at the BS. In contrast, the statistical CSI, i.e., $\{\beta_m,\kappa_m,\theta_m,\phi_m\}_{m\in\mathcal M}$ of the LUs and $\{\beta_e,\kappa_e,\theta_e,\phi_e\}$ of Eve, is determined by the large-scale fading and the LoS geometry, which vary much more slowly than the small-scale fading. Following~\cite{HuTMC2024,ChenArxiv2026}, we assume that the statistical CSI of Eve is known at the BS, which can be obtained, e.g., from the prior knowledge of the location of Eve, by sensing the direction of Eve~\cite{ChenArxiv2026}, or from the historical signals of Eve if it is an untrusted node of the network.

Accordingly, we adopt the two-timescale transmission protocol illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol}. The time is divided into frames, each of which consists of $N_{\mathrm{CI}}\gg1$ CIs over which the statistical CSI remains constant. At the beginning of each frame, the BS optimizes the antenna positions $\bt$ and the long-term transmission parameters based on the statistical CSI, and moves the MAs accordingly. Then, with $\bt$ fixed, the BS estimates the instantaneous CSI of the LUs, i.e., $\bH(\bt)$, via uplink pilots in each CI by exploiting the channel reciprocity of time-division duplexing, and designs the transmit precoding and AN. For the purpose of exposition, $\bH(\bt)$ is assumed to be perfectly estimated in each CI, whereas the instantaneous CSI of Eve, i.e., $\gtl$, is never available at the BS. Compared with the instantaneous-CSI-based MA designs, the proposed protocol moves the MAs only once per frame and requires the instantaneous CSI of the LUs only at the current antenna positions, which significantly reduces the antenna movement and channel estimation overhead.

\subsection{Signal Model}

In each CI, the BS transmits the confidential symbols $s_m\sim\CN(0,1)$, $m\in\mathcal M$, together with AN, i.e., the transmitted signal is given by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:x}
\bx=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\bw_m s_m+\bz,
\end{equation}
where $\bw_m\in\mathbb C^{N\times1}$ is the precoding vector for LU $m$, and $\bz\sim\CN(\mathbf 0,\bSig)$ is the AN vector with covariance matrix $\bSig\succeq\mathbf 0$, which is independent of the confidential symbols. The symbols $\{s_m\}$ are independent of each other. Under the two-timescale protocol, $\{\bw_m\}$ and $\bSig$ are designed in each CI based on $\bH(\bt)$, and are thus functions of the instantaneous CSI of the LUs, which are denoted by $\bw_m(\bH)$ and $\bSig(\bH)$ when necessary. The transmit power of the BS is limited by $\sum_{m=1}^M\|\bw_m\|^2+\tr(\bSig)\le\Ptot$.

The signal received at LU $m$ is $y_m=\bh_m^H(\bt)\bx+n_m$, where $n_m\sim\CN(0,\sigma_m^2)$ is the additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN). Hence, the signal-to-interference-plus-noise ratio (SINR) of LU $m$ is given by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sinr_gen}
\gamma_m=\frac{|\bh_m^H\bw_m|^2}{\sum_{i\in\mathcal M\setminus\{m\}}|\bh_m^H\bw_i|^2+\bh_m^H\bSig\bh_m+\sigma_m^2},
\end{equation}
where the dependence of the channels on $\bt$ is omitted for brevity. The signal received at Eve is $y_e=\bg^H(\bt)\bx+n_e$, where $n_e\sim\CN(0,\sigma_e^2)$ is the AWGN. To guarantee secrecy in the worst case, we assume that Eve is able to cancel the signals of the other LUs when intercepting $s_m$, e.g., via multiuser detection~\cite{ZhuTWC2014}. Accordingly, the SINR of Eve for intercepting $s_m$ is given by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sinre_gen}
\gamma_{e,m}=\frac{|\bg^H\bw_m|^2}{\bg^H\bSig\bg+\sigma_e^2},
\end{equation}
which upper-bounds the SINR of Eve when the signals of the other LUs are treated as interference.

\subsection{Ergodic Secrecy Rate and Problem Formulation}

Since the instantaneous CSI of Eve is unknown, the BS cannot adapt the secrecy coding to the instantaneous channel of Eve. Instead, the confidential message of each LU is encoded over the CIs of a frame, which experience independent realizations of the NLoS components. By coding over these fading realizations, the following ergodic secrecy rate is achievable for LU $m$~\cite{Gopala2008,ZhuTWC2014}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:esr}
R_m^{\mathrm{sec}}\triangleq\big[\bar R_m-\bar R_{e,m}\big]^+,
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ergodic}
\bar R_m\triangleq\E\{\log_2(1+\gamma_m)\},\quad \bar R_{e,m}\triangleq\E\{\log_2(1+\gamma_{e,m})\}
\end{equation}
denote the ergodic rate of LU $m$ and the ergodic wiretap rate of Eve for $s_m$, respectively, and the expectations are taken over the NLoS components $\Htl$ and $\gtl$ for given $\bt$. Note that $R_m^{\mathrm{sec}}$ depends on both the antenna positions $\bt$ and the short-timescale design policies $\{\bw_m(\cdot)\}$ and $\bSig(\cdot)$.

We aim to maximize the ESSR of the LUs by jointly optimizing the long-term antenna positions and the short-term precoding and AN design policies. The problem is formulated as
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:P1}
\begin{align}
(\mathrm{P1}):\ \max_{\bt,\{\bw_m(\cdot)\},\bSig(\cdot)}\ \ & \sum_{m=1}^{M}R_m^{\mathrm{sec}}\label{eq:P1obj}\\
\mathrm{s.t.}\ \ & \sum_{m=1}^M\|\bw_m(\bH)\|^2+\tr\big(\bSig(\bH)\big)\le\Ptot,\nonumber\\
& \bSig(\bH)\succeq\mathbf 0,\ \forall\bH,\label{eq:P1pow}\\
& \bt_n\in\mathcal C,\ \forall n\in\mathcal N,\label{eq:P1region}\\
& \|\bt_n-\bt_i\|\ge\Dmin,\ \forall n\neq i.\label{eq:P1dist}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
Problem (P1) is challenging to solve for the following reasons. First, the short-timescale variables are policies, i.e., mappings from the instantaneous CSI of the LUs to the precoders and the AN covariance matrix. Since the instantaneous CSI of Eve is unavailable and $\bar R_{e,m}$ is averaged over all CIs, the optimal policies cannot be obtained by maximizing an instantaneous secrecy rate in each CI. Second, the ergodic rates in~\eqref{eq:ergodic} have no closed-form expressions, even for a given $\bt$ and given policies. Third, the long-term variable $\bt$ is coupled with the short-term policies in~\eqref{eq:P1obj}, and constraint~\eqref{eq:P1dist} is non-convex. Although stochastic optimization methods based on channel samples~\cite{ZhaoTWC2021} can be applied to (P1), their complexity increases with the number of samples, and the obtained solutions provide little insight into the role of the MAs. To strike a balance between performance and complexity, we restrict the short-timescale design to ZF precoding and null-space AN with a long-term power allocation in the next section, which leads to tractable expressions of the ergodic rates.

\section{Two-Timescale ZF-AN Design and Ergodic Secrecy Rate Analysis}\label{sec:analysis}
% =============================================================================
In this section, we first specify the short-timescale ZF precoding and null-space AN, based on which (P1) is transformed into a problem of the antenna positions and the long-term power allocation. Then, we derive closed-form approximations of the ergodic rates of the LUs and Eve, and reveal how the antenna positions affect the ESSR.

\subsection{Short-Timescale ZF Precoding and Null-Space AN}\label{sec:zfan}

Given $\bt$ and the instantaneous CSI $\bH=\bH(\bt)$ in a CI, the BS adopts ZF precoding with a long-term power allocation, i.e., $\bw_m=\sqrt{p_m}\,\wb_m$, where $p_m\ge0$ is the transmit power allocated to LU $m$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zf}
\wb_m\triangleq\frac{\bH(\bH^H\bH)^{-1}\be_m}{\big\|\bH(\bH^H\bH)^{-1}\be_m\big\|}
\end{equation}
is the normalized ZF precoding direction of LU $m$. Since $\bH^H\bH(\bH^H\bH)^{-1}\be_m=\be_m$, we have $\bh_i^H\wb_m=0$, $\forall i\neq m$, i.e., the inter-user interference is completely eliminated. Meanwhile, the AN is isotropically distributed in the null space of $\bH^H$~\cite{Zhou2010,ZhuTWC2014}, i.e.,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:an}
\bSig=q\,\PHp,\qquad \PHp\triangleq\bI_N-\bH(\bH^H\bH)^{-1}\bH^H,
\end{equation}
where $q\ge0$ denotes the AN power per dimension. Since $\PHp$ is the orthogonal projection matrix onto the null space of $\bH^H$, whose dimension is $N-M$ with probability one, we have $\bH^H\PHp=\mathbf 0$ and $\tr(\PHp)=N-M$. Hence, the AN causes no interference to the LUs, and the transmit power in each CI is $\sum_{m=1}^M p_m+(N-M)q$. Let $\bp\triangleq[p_1,\ldots,p_M]^T$. The power allocation $(\bp,q)$ is optimized based on the statistical CSI and remains fixed within a frame, whereas $\{\wb_m\}$ and $\PHp$ are updated in each CI based on $\bH$.

With the above design, the SINRs in~\eqref{eq:sinr_gen} and~\eqref{eq:sinre_gen} reduce to
\begin{align}
\gamma_m&=\frac{p_m|\bh_m^H\wb_m|^2}{\sigma_m^2}=\frac{p_m}{\sigma_m^2\big[(\bH^H\bH)^{-1}\big]_{m,m}},\label{eq:snr}\\
\gamma_{e,m}&=\frac{p_m|\bg^H\wb_m|^2}{q\,\bg^H\PHp\bg+\sigma_e^2},\label{eq:sinre}
\end{align}
respectively, where the second equality in~\eqref{eq:snr} holds since $\bh_m^H\bH(\bH^H\bH)^{-1}\be_m=1$ and $\|\bH(\bH^H\bH)^{-1}\be_m\|^2=[(\bH^H\bH)^{-1}]_{m,m}$.

The adopted ZF-AN design has several appealing properties in the considered two-timescale framework. First, it requires only the instantaneous CSI of the LUs, which is consistent with the protocol in Section~\ref{sec:model}-B. Second, it eliminates both the inter-user interference and the AN at the LUs, so that $\gamma_m$ in~\eqref{eq:snr} depends only on the channels of the LUs, whereas the information leakage $|\bg^H\wb_m|^2$ and the AN power $\bg^H\PHp\bg$ received by Eve in~\eqref{eq:sinre} are determined by the ZF precoding directions and the AN subspace, respectively. Third, since the instantaneous CSI of Eve is unknown, the BS has no preference among the directions in the null space of $\bH^H$, and thus distributes the AN isotropically in this subspace~\cite{Goel2008,Zhou2010}. Fourth, the power allocation $(\bp,q)$ is adapted only to the statistical CSI, which avoids solving an optimization problem in each CI and renders the ergodic rates analytically tractable. Note that both the equal power allocation adopted in~\cite{ZhengTCOM2025} and the information--AN power splitting with equal power among the LUs are special cases of the considered long-term power allocation.

With the ZF-AN design, (P1) is transformed into the following problem of $\bt$, $\bp$, and $q$:
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:P2}
\begin{align}
(\mathrm{P2}):\ \max_{\bt,\bp,q}\ \ & \sum_{m=1}^{M}R_m^{\mathrm{sec}}(\bt,p_m,q)\label{eq:P2obj}\\
\mathrm{s.t.}\ \ & \sum_{m=1}^{M}p_m+(N-M)q\le\Ptot,\label{eq:P2pow}\\
& p_m\ge0,\ \forall m\in\mathcal M,\quad q\ge0,\label{eq:P2pos}\\
& \eqref{eq:P1region},\ \eqref{eq:P1dist},\nonumber
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
where $R_m^{\mathrm{sec}}(\bt,p_m,q)$ denotes the ergodic secrecy rate in~\eqref{eq:esr} with the SINRs in~\eqref{eq:snr} and~\eqref{eq:sinre}. Since the ergodic rates in~\eqref{eq:P2obj} still have no closed-form expressions, we derive their approximations in the sequel.

\subsection{Ergodic Rate of the LUs}\label{sec:lu}

The ergodic rate of ZF precoding over Rician fading channels has been well studied in the literature~\cite{ZhangJSTSP2014,ZhengTCOM2025,HuTVT2025}. To present the result in a form that is convenient for the subsequent analysis, we define $\bB\triangleq\diag\big(\frac{\beta_1}{\kappa_1+1},\ldots,\frac{\beta_M}{\kappa_M+1}\big)$ and $\bL(\bt)\triangleq\Hb(\bt)\bK^{1/2}$. From~\eqref{eq:hm}, the channel matrix of the LUs can be written as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Hdecomp}
\bH(\bt)=\big(\bL(\bt)+\Htl\big)\bB^{1/2},
\end{equation}
where $\bL(\bt)+\Htl$ is the normalized channel matrix, whose LoS component $\bL(\bt)$ is weighted by the square roots of the Rician factors. Accordingly, we define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Phi}
\bPhi(\bt)\triangleq\bL^H(\bt)\bL(\bt)+N\bI_M,\qquad \bOm(\bt)\triangleq\bPhi^{-1}(\bt),
\end{equation}
where $\bPhi(\bt)=\E\{(\bL(\bt)+\Htl)^H(\bL(\bt)+\Htl)\}$ is the correlation matrix of the normalized channels of the LUs.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:lu}
For given $\bt$ and $p_m$, the ergodic rate of LU $m$ is approximated by~\cite{ZhangJSTSP2014,ZhengTCOM2025}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Rm}
\bar R_m\approx\tilde R_m(\bt,p_m)\triangleq\log_2\big(1+\eta_m(\bt)\,p_m\big),
\end{equation}
where $\eta_m(\bt)\triangleq\zeta_m(N-M)/(N\omega_m(\bt))$, $\zeta_m\triangleq\beta_m/((\kappa_m+1)\sigma_m^2)$, and $\omega_m(\bt)\triangleq[\bOm(\bt)]_{m,m}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{IEEEproof}
By applying Jensen's inequality to the convex function $\log_2(1+1/x)$ of $x>0$, we have $\bar R_m\ge\log_2\big(1+p_m/(\sigma_m^2\E\{[(\bH^H\bH)^{-1}]_{m,m}\})\big)$. Following~\cite{ZhangJSTSP2014}, the noncentral Wishart matrix $(\bL+\Htl)^H(\bL+\Htl)$ is approximated by a central Wishart matrix with $N$ degrees of freedom and the same mean $\bPhi(\bt)$, whose inverse has the mean $N\bOm(\bt)/(N-M)$. Together with~\eqref{eq:Hdecomp}, this yields $\E\{[(\bH^H\bH)^{-1}]_{m,m}\}\approx N(\kappa_m+1)\omega_m(\bt)/(\beta_m(N-M))$, which leads to~\eqref{eq:Rm}.
\end{IEEEproof}

Lemma~\ref{lem:lu} is equivalent to the approximation in~\cite[Sec.~IV-A]{ZhengTCOM2025}, since the normalized channel correlation matrix used therein equals $\frac1N(\bK+\bI_M)^{-1/2}\bPhi(\bt)(\bK+\bI_M)^{-1/2}$. Since $\bPhi(\bt)\succeq N\bI_M$ and $[\bOm(\bt)]_{m,m}\ge1/[\bPhi(\bt)]_{m,m}=1/(N(\kappa_m+1))$~\cite{Horn2013}, we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:etabound}
\zeta_m(N-M)\le\eta_m(\bt)\le\zeta_m(N-M)(\kappa_m+1),
\end{equation}
where the lower bound is achieved for $\kappa_m=0$, and the upper bound is achieved if the LoS component of LU $m$ is orthogonal to those of the other LUs, i.e., $\hb_m^H(\bt)\hb_i(\bt)=0$ for all $i\neq m$ with $\kappa_i>0$. Therefore, $1/(N\omega_m(\bt))\in[1,\kappa_m+1]$ can be interpreted as the LoS gain of LU $m$ that survives the ZF precoding, which can be improved by decorrelating the LoS components of the LUs via antenna position optimization.

\remarkhead{Accuracy of Lemma~\ref{lem:lu}}\label{rem:lu} Lemma~\ref{lem:lu} is a lower-bound-type approximation. For Rayleigh fading, i.e., $\bK=\mathbf 0$, the central Wishart approximation is exact, and~\eqref{eq:Rm} reduces to the well-known lower bound $\log_2(1+\zeta_mp_m(N-M))$~\cite{Ngo2013}. For large Rician factors, the degrees-of-freedom loss $(N-M)/N$ of the central Wishart approximation also applies to the deterministic LoS component. Hence, the gap between~\eqref{eq:Rm} and $\bar R_m$ increases with the Rician factors, and approaches $\log_2(N/(N-M))$ at high SNR as $\kappa_i\to\infty$, $\forall i\in\mathcal M$. Nevertheless, this gap mainly depends on the Rician factors and the ratio $N/(N-M)$, and only weakly on the antenna positions, as will be verified in Section~\ref{sec:sim}. Therefore, it has a marginal impact on the optimization of $\bt$, although it renders the approximate ESSR conservative. For this reason, we adopt~\eqref{eq:Rm} due to its simplicity and wide acceptance~\cite{ZhengTCOM2025,HuTVT2025}, and focus on the ergodic wiretap rate of Eve, for which no existing result is available.

\subsection{Ergodic Wiretap Rate of Eve}\label{sec:eve}

Different from $\gamma_m$, the SINR of Eve in~\eqref{eq:sinre} is the ratio of the information leakage $|\bg^H\wb_m|^2$ to the AN-plus-noise power, both of which depend on the random ZF precoding direction and AN subspace induced by $\bH$. The following lemma characterizes their statistics conditioned on $\bH$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:indep}
Conditioned on $\bH$, the information leakage $X_m\triangleq|\bg^H\wb_m|^2$ and the AN power $Y\triangleq\bg^H\PHp\bg$ received by Eve are independent, and their conditional means and variances are given by
\begin{align}
\E\{X_m|\bH\}&=\tilde\beta_e\big(\kappa_ex_m+1\big),\nonumber\\
\mathrm{Var}\{X_m|\bH\}&=\tilde\beta_e^2\big(2\kappa_ex_m+1\big),\label{eq:EX}\\
\E\{Y|\bH\}&=\tilde\beta_e\big(\kappa_ey+N-M\big),\nonumber\\
\mathrm{Var}\{Y|\bH\}&=\tilde\beta_e^2\big(2\kappa_ey+N-M\big),\label{eq:EY}
\end{align}
where $\tilde\beta_e\triangleq\beta_e/(\kappa_e+1)$, $x_m\triangleq|\gb^H\wb_m|^2$, and $y\triangleq\gb^H\PHp\gb$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{IEEEproof}
Let $\mathbf U\triangleq[\mathbf u_1,\ldots,\mathbf u_{N-M}]$ be an orthonormal basis of the null space of $\bH^H$, so that $\PHp=\mathbf U\mathbf U^H$ and $Y=\sum_{i=1}^{N-M}|\mathbf u_i^H\bg|^2$. Since $\wb_m$ is a unit-norm vector in the column space of $\bH$, the matrix $[\wb_m,\mathbf U]$ has orthonormal columns. Since $\gtl$ is independent of $\bH$, we have $\bg|\bH\sim\CN(\sqrt{\kappa_e\tilde\beta_e}\,\gb,\tilde\beta_e\bI_N)$ from~\eqref{eq:g}, and thus $\wb_m^H\bg,\mathbf u_1^H\bg,\ldots,\mathbf u_{N-M}^H\bg$ are independent complex Gaussian random variables with variance $\tilde\beta_e$ given $\bH$. Hence, $X_m=|\wb_m^H\bg|^2$ and $Y$ are conditionally independent. Finally, \eqref{eq:EX} and~\eqref{eq:EY} follow from $\E\{|z|^2\}=|\mu|^2+\sigma^2$ and $\mathrm{Var}\{|z|^2\}=\sigma^4+2|\mu|^2\sigma^2$ for $z\sim\CN(\mu,\sigma^2)$, together with $\sum_{i=1}^{N-M}|\mathbf u_i^H\gb|^2=y$.
\end{IEEEproof}

Lemma~\ref{lem:indep} reveals that, for given channels of the LUs, the information leakage is determined only by the component of the wiretap channel along the ZF direction, which lies in the signal subspace spanned by the channels of the LUs, whereas the AN received by Eve is determined only by the component in the orthogonal complement of this subspace. Moreover, the conditional relative variances of $X_m$ and $Y$ are $(2\kappa_ex_m+1)/(\kappa_ex_m+1)^2$ and $(2\kappa_ey+N-M)/(\kappa_ey+N-M)^2$, respectively. Hence, $X_m$ concentrates around its mean when the LoS leakage $\kappa_ex_m$ is strong, which is the case that dominates the ergodic wiretap rate, and $Y$ concentrates when $N-M$ or $\kappa_ey$ is large. This motivates us to apply to $\bar R_{e,m}$ the approximation $\E\{\log_2(1+X/Y)\}\approx\log_2(1+\E\{X\}/\E\{Y\})$ for nonnegative random variables $X$ and $Y$~\cite[Lemma~1]{ZhangJSTSP2014}, which is accurate when $X$ and $Y$ concentrate around their means, and is asymptotically exact as $\kappa_e\to\infty$ and $\kappa_m\to\infty$, $\forall m\in\mathcal M$. By using Lemma~\ref{lem:indep} and taking the expectation over $\Htl$, we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Re0}
\bar R_{e,m}\approx\log_2\bigg(1+\frac{p_m\big(\kappa_e\E\{x_m\}+1\big)}{q\big(\kappa_e\E\{y\}+N-M\big)+\sbe}\bigg),
\end{equation}
where $\sbe\triangleq\sigma_e^2/\tilde\beta_e=\sigma_e^2(\kappa_e+1)/\beta_e$ is the normalized noise power of Eve, and the remaining expectations are taken over $\Htl$.

The two expectations $\E\{x_m\}=\E\{|\gb^H\wb_m|^2\}$ and $\E\{y\}=\E\{\gb^H\PHp\gb\}$ in~\eqref{eq:Re0} are the average LoS energies of Eve along the ZF precoding direction of LU $m$ and in the AN subspace, respectively. They depend on $\bt$ through both the LoS and the random NLoS components of the LU channels, and have no closed-form expressions. Note that $\wb_m$ and $\PHp$ are invariant to the scaling of the columns of $\bH$. Therefore, from~\eqref{eq:Hdecomp}, they can be equivalently computed based on the normalized channel matrix $\bL(\bt)+\Htl$. This motivates the following lemma for a generic noncentral Gaussian matrix.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:det}
Let $\bF=\Fb+\bW\in\mathbb C^{N\times k}$ with $k<N$, where $\Fb$ is deterministic and the entries of $\bW$ are i.i.d. $\CN(0,1)$ random variables. Let $\bb\in\mathbb C^{N\times1}$ be a deterministic vector, and define $\bPhi_{\bF}\triangleq\E\{\bF^H\bF\}=\Fb^H\Fb+N\bI_k$, $\wb_m(\bF)\triangleq\bF(\bF^H\bF)^{-1}\be_m/\|\bF(\bF^H\bF)^{-1}\be_m\|$, and $\mathbf P_{\bF}^\perp\triangleq\bI_N-\bF(\bF^H\bF)^{-1}\bF^H$. Then, we have
\begin{align}
\E\{|\bb^H\wb_m(\bF)|^2\}&\approx\frac{\big|[\bPhi_{\bF}^{-1}\Fb^H\bb]_m\big|^2+\|\bb\|^2[\bPhi_{\bF}^{-2}]_{m,m}}{[\bPhi_{\bF}^{-1}]_{m,m}},\label{eq:lem_c}\\
\E\{\bb^H\mathbf P_{\bF}^\perp\bb\}&\approx\frac{(N-k)\big(\|\bb\|^2-\bb^H\Fb\bPhi_{\bF}^{-1}\Fb^H\bb\big)}{N-k+N\tr(\bPhi_{\bF}^{-1})}.\label{eq:lem_d}
\end{align}
Moreover, \eqref{eq:lem_c} and~\eqref{eq:lem_d} are exact for $\Fb=\mathbf 0$, and are asymptotically exact for $\Fb=\varrho\Fb_0$ as $\varrho\to\infty$, where $\Fb_0$ has full column rank. The right-hand sides of~\eqref{eq:lem_c} and~\eqref{eq:lem_d} lie in $[0,\|\bb\|^2]$, as do their left-hand sides.
\end{lemma}
\begin{IEEEproof}
See Appendix~\ref{app:det}.
\end{IEEEproof}

Lemma~\ref{lem:det} is based on replacing the random matrix $(\bF^H\bF)^{-1}$ by the inverse of its mean, which is accurate when either the LoS component dominates or $N$ is large compared with $k$. In~\eqref{eq:lem_d}, the NLoS component is further restricted to the part of the space that is not captured by the LoS component, and its share is calibrated by the identity $\tr(\mathbf P_{\bF}^\perp)=N-k$, which holds for every realization of $\bF$ (see Appendix~\ref{app:det}). As a result, Lemma~\ref{lem:det} is exact in the NLoS-dominant regime, where the ZF direction and the null space are isotropically distributed, and asymptotically exact in the LoS-dominant regime, where they are determined by $\Fb$. By applying Lemma~\ref{lem:det} to~\eqref{eq:Re0}, we obtain the following closed-form approximation of the ergodic wiretap rate.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:eve}
Define $\bv(\bt)\triangleq\bL^H(\bt)\gb(\bt)=\bK^{1/2}\Hb^H(\bt)\gb(\bt)$ and
\begin{align}
c_m(\bt)&\triangleq\frac{\big|[\bOm(\bt)\bv(\bt)]_m\big|^2+N[\bOm^2(\bt)]_{m,m}}{\omega_m(\bt)},\label{eq:cm}\\
d(\bt)&\triangleq\frac{(N-M)\big(N-\bv^H(\bt)\bOm(\bt)\bv(\bt)\big)}{N-M+N\tr(\bOm(\bt))}.\label{eq:d}
\end{align}
Then, the ergodic wiretap rate of Eve for $s_m$ is approximated by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Re}
\bar R_{e,m}\approx\tilde R_{e,m}(\bt,p_m,q)\triangleq\log_2\bigg(1+\frac{\xi_m(\bt)\,p_m}{q\,\psi(\bt)+\sbe}\bigg),
\end{equation}
where $\xi_m(\bt)\triangleq\kappa_ec_m(\bt)+1$ and $\psi(\bt)\triangleq\kappa_ed(\bt)+N-M$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{IEEEproof}
From~\eqref{eq:Hdecomp}, $\wb_m=\wb_m(\bL(\bt)+\Htl)$ and $\PHp=\mathbf P_{\bL(\bt)+\Htl}^\perp$. Applying Lemma~\ref{lem:det} with $\Fb=\bL(\bt)$, $\bW=\Htl$, $k=M$, and $\bb=\gb(\bt)$, for which $\bPhi_{\bF}=\bPhi(\bt)$, $\Fb^H\bb=\bv(\bt)$, and $\|\bb\|^2=N$, yields $\E\{x_m\}\approx c_m(\bt)$ and $\E\{y\}\approx d(\bt)$. Substituting them into~\eqref{eq:Re0} completes the proof.
\end{IEEEproof}

In Proposition~\ref{prop:eve}, $c_m(\bt)\in[0,N]$ and $d(\bt)\in[0,N]$ approximate the LoS energy of Eve along the ZF precoding direction of LU $m$ and in the AN subspace, respectively. Accordingly, $\xi_m(\bt)$ and $\psi(\bt)$ can be interpreted as the normalized information leakage for LU $m$ and the normalized AN power received by Eve, respectively. Different from the existing ergodic-rate approximations for ZF precoding, which only involve the diagonal entries of $\bOm(\bt)$, Proposition~\ref{prop:eve} explicitly captures the correlation between the LoS components of Eve and the LUs via $\bv(\bt)$.

\subsection{Approximate ESSR and Discussions}\label{sec:discussion}

For ease of reference, the key symbols are summarized in Table~\ref{tab:symbols}. Based on Lemma~\ref{lem:lu} and Proposition~\ref{prop:eve}, the ergodic secrecy rate of LU $m$ is approximated by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:esrapprox}
R_m^{\mathrm{sec}}\approx\tilde R_m^{\mathrm{sec}}(\bt,p_m,q)\triangleq\big[\tilde R_m(\bt,p_m)-\tilde R_{e,m}(\bt,p_m,q)\big]^+,
\end{equation}
which depends only on the statistical CSI of the LUs and Eve, and is thus suitable for the large-timescale design. In the following, we discuss the insights revealed by~\eqref{eq:esrapprox}.

\remarkhead{Role of the antenna positions}\label{rem:role} From~\eqref{eq:Rm}, \eqref{eq:cm}, and~\eqref{eq:d}, the antenna positions affect the approximate ESSR only through three geometric quantities, i.e., $\omega_m(\bt)$, $c_m(\bt)$, and $d(\bt)$, which in turn depend on $\bt$ only via $\bPhi(\bt)$ and $\bv(\bt)$, i.e., via the LoS array correlations
\begin{align}
\big[\Hb^H(\bt)\Hb(\bt)\big]_{i,j}&=\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\bt_n^T(\ba_j-\ba_i)},\nonumber\\
\big[\Hb^H(\bt)\gb(\bt)\big]_{i}&=\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\bt_n^T(\ba_e-\ba_i)}.\label{eq:arraycorr}
\end{align}
Specifically, a smaller $\omega_m(\bt)$ indicates that the LoS component of LU $m$ is less correlated with those of the other LUs, which yields a larger ZF gain; a smaller $c_m(\bt)$ indicates less information leakage to Eve; and a larger $d(\bt)$ indicates more AN received by Eve. In particular, if $\bv(\bt)=\mathbf 0$, i.e., the LoS component of Eve is orthogonal to those of all the LUs, then $c_m(\bt)$ reduces to its minimum $N[\bOm^2(\bt)]_{m,m}/\omega_m(\bt)$ caused by the NLoS components, and $d(\bt)$ achieves its maximum $N(N-M)/(N-M+N\tr(\bOm(\bt)))$ for a given $\bPhi(\bt)$. Hence, by decorrelating the LoS component of Eve from those of the LUs, the MAs can simultaneously reduce the information leakage and increase the AN received by Eve, which is difficult for an FPA array when Eve is located in a direction close to that of an LU.

\remarkhead{Special cases}\label{rem:special} 1) If $\kappa_e=0$, i.e., the channel of Eve has no LoS component, then $\tilde R_{e,m}=\log_2(1+p_m/(q(N-M)+\sbe))$ does not depend on $\bt$. In this case, the statistical CSI of Eve carries no spatial information, and the MAs enhance the secrecy performance only by improving $\omega_m(\bt)$ as in the rate-oriented design. 2) If $M=1$, then $\bPhi(\bt)=N(\kappa_1+1)$ is a scalar, and thus $\eta_1=\zeta_1(N-1)(\kappa_1+1)$ does not depend on $\bt$.\footnote{In fact, for $M=1$, the ZF precoder reduces to $\wb_1=\bh_1/\|\bh_1\|$, and the exact ergodic rate $\bar R_1=\E\{\log_2(1+p_1\|\bh_1\|^2/\sigma_1^2)\}$ does not depend on $\bt$, since the distribution of $\|\bh_1\|^2$ depends on $\hb_1(\bt)$ only through $\|\hb_1(\bt)\|^2=N$.} In contrast,
\begin{align}
c_1(\bt)&=\frac{\kappa_1|\hb_1^H(\bt)\gb(\bt)|^2/N+1}{\kappa_1+1},\label{eq:c1}\\
d(\bt)&=\frac{(N-1)(\kappa_1+1)}{(N-1)(\kappa_1+1)+1}\bigg(N-\frac{\kappa_1|\hb_1^H(\bt)\gb(\bt)|^2}{N(\kappa_1+1)}\bigg)\label{eq:d1}
\end{align}
depend on $\bt$ through the LoS correlation $|\hb_1^H(\bt)\gb(\bt)|^2$ between the LU and Eve. Hence, different from the rate-oriented two-timescale design, where the MAs bring no gain for a single user~\cite{ZhengTCOM2025}, the MAs enhance the secrecy performance of a single LU by reducing the LoS correlation between the LU and Eve.

\remarkhead{High-SNR behavior and AN saving}\label{rem:highsnr} Consider the information--AN power splitting with equal power among the LUs, i.e., $p_m=\alpha\Ptot/M$ and $q=(1-\alpha)\Ptot/(N-M)$ with $\alpha\in(0,1)$. As $\Ptot\to\infty$, we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:highsnr}
\tilde R_m-\tilde R_{e,m}=\log_2\frac{\alpha\Ptot\eta_m(\bt)}{M}-\log_2\Big(1+\frac{\alpha\Theta_m(\bt)}{1-\alpha}\Big)+o(1),
\end{equation}
where $\Theta_m(\bt)\triangleq\frac{(N-M)\xi_m(\bt)}{M\psi(\bt)}$ is the ratio of the normalized information leakage to the normalized AN power received by Eve. Hence, the high-SNR slope of the ESSR equals $M$ regardless of $\bt$, and the antenna positions affect the ESSR through the constant term. Moreover, the value of $\alpha$ that maximizes the sum of the constant terms in~\eqref{eq:highsnr} over $m$ is the root of $\sum_{m=1}^M\big[\frac1\alpha-\frac1{1-\alpha}-\frac{\Theta_m-1}{1+\alpha(\Theta_m-1)}\big]=0$, which, for $\Theta_m=\Theta$, $\forall m$, is given by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:alphastar}
\alpha^\star=\frac{1}{1+\sqrt{\Theta}}.
\end{equation}
Since the MAs can decrease $c_m(\bt)$ and increase $d(\bt)$, they decrease $\Theta$ and thus increase $\alpha^\star$. In other words, the MAs reduce the AN power required for secure transmission, which is referred to as the AN-saving effect.

\section{Proposed Two-Timescale Optimization Algorithm}\label{sec:opt}
% =============================================================================
In this section, based on the approximate ESSR derived in Section~\ref{sec:analysis}, we reformulate (P2) as a deterministic problem that depends only on the statistical CSI, and develop a low-complexity AO algorithm to solve it.

\subsection{Problem Reformulation}

By replacing the ergodic secrecy rates in (P2) with their approximations in~\eqref{eq:esrapprox}, we consider the following problem:
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:P3}
\begin{align}
(\mathrm{P3}):\ \max_{\bt,\bp,q}\ \ & F(\bt,\bp,q)\triangleq\sum_{m=1}^{M}\bigg[\log_2\big(1+\eta_m(\bt)p_m\big)\nonumber\\
&\qquad\qquad-\log_2\bigg(1+\frac{\xi_m(\bt)p_m}{q\psi(\bt)+\sbe}\bigg)\bigg]\label{eq:P3obj}\\
\mathrm{s.t.}\ \ & \eqref{eq:P1region},\ \eqref{eq:P1dist},\ \eqref{eq:P2pow},\ \eqref{eq:P2pos},\nonumber
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
where the operator $[\cdot]^+$ in~\eqref{eq:esrapprox} is dropped without loss of optimality, as shown in the following proposition.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:plus}
The optimal value of (P3) equals that of the problem obtained by replacing $F(\bt,\bp,q)$ in~\eqref{eq:P3obj} with $\sum_{m=1}^M\tilde R_m^{\mathrm{sec}}(\bt,p_m,q)$. Moreover, at any optimal solution of (P3), every term in the summation of~\eqref{eq:P3obj} is nonnegative.
\end{proposition}
\begin{IEEEproof}
Since $[x]^+\ge x$, the optimal value with $[\cdot]^+$ is no smaller than that of (P3). Conversely, for any feasible $(\bt,\bp,q)$, let $\bp'$ be obtained by setting $p_m'=0$ for all $m$ with $\tilde R_m(\bt,p_m)<\tilde R_{e,m}(\bt,p_m,q)$ and $p_m'=p_m$ otherwise. Then, $(\bt,\bp',q)$ is feasible, and since the $m$-th term of $F$ depends only on $p_m$ and $q$ for a given $\bt$, we have $F(\bt,\bp',q)=\sum_{m=1}^M\tilde R_m^{\mathrm{sec}}(\bt,p_m,q)$. Hence, the two optimal values are equal. If some term were negative at an optimal solution of (P3), setting the corresponding $p_m$ to zero would strictly increase $F$, which is a contradiction.
\end{IEEEproof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:plus} shows that the non-smooth operator $[\cdot]^+$ can be removed by allowing the BS to switch off the LUs with non-positive secrecy rates, which is enabled by the per-user power allocation $\bp$. In contrast, under equal power allocation among the LUs, the operator $[\cdot]^+$ must be retained, and the power allocated to such LUs is wasted. Problem (P3) is still non-convex due to the coupling between $\bt$ and $(\bp,q)$ in~\eqref{eq:P3obj} and the non-convex constraint~\eqref{eq:P1dist}. To tackle these difficulties, we adopt the AO framework, which alternately optimizes $(\bp,q)$ for given $\bt$ and $\bt$ for given $(\bp,q)$.

\subsection{Power Allocation Optimization}\label{sec:power}

For given $\bt$, the coefficients $\eta_m$, $\xi_m$, and $\psi$ are constants, and (P3) reduces to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:P31}
(\mathrm{P3.1}):\ \max_{\bp,q}\ \sum_{m=1}^{M}f_m(p_m;q)\quad\mathrm{s.t.}\ \eqref{eq:P2pow},\ \eqref{eq:P2pos},
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fm}
f_m(p;q)\triangleq\log_2(1+\eta_mp)-\log_2\big(1+\eta_{e,m}(q)\,p\big),
\end{equation}
and $\eta_{e,m}(q)\triangleq\xi_m/(q\psi+\sbe)$ is the effective channel gain of Eve for LU $m$, which decreases with $q$. We first optimize $\bp$ for a given $q$. The first-order and second-order derivatives of $f_m$ with respect to (w.r.t.) $p$ are given by
\begin{align}
\frac{\partial f_m}{\partial p}&=\frac{\eta_m-\eta_{e,m}}{\ln2\,(1+\eta_mp)(1+\eta_{e,m}p)},\label{eq:fm1}\\
\frac{\partial^2 f_m}{\partial p^2}&=\frac{1}{\ln2}\bigg[\frac{\eta_{e,m}^2}{(1+\eta_{e,m}p)^2}-\frac{\eta_m^2}{(1+\eta_mp)^2}\bigg],\label{eq:fm2}
\end{align}
where the argument $q$ of $\eta_{e,m}$ is omitted for brevity. If $\eta_m\le\eta_{e,m}$, then $f_m(p;q)\le0$ for all $p\ge0$, and thus $p_m=0$ is optimal. If $\eta_m>\eta_{e,m}$, then $\eta_m(1+\eta_{e,m}p)-\eta_{e,m}(1+\eta_mp)=\eta_m-\eta_{e,m}>0$, i.e., $\frac{\eta_m}{1+\eta_mp}>\frac{\eta_{e,m}}{1+\eta_{e,m}p}$, which implies that $f_m$ is strictly increasing and strictly concave in $p\ge0$. Let $\mathcal M_{\mathrm a}(q)\triangleq\{m\in\mathcal M\,|\,\eta_m>\eta_{e,m}(q)\}$ denote the set of LUs that can achieve positive secrecy rates, and let $P_{\mathrm I}(q)\triangleq\Ptot-(N-M)q$ denote the power available for the confidential signals. Then, for a given $q$, the optimization of $\bp$ is a convex problem, whose solution is given by the following proposition.

\begin{proposition}[Secrecy water-filling]\label{prop:wf}
For a given $q$ with $P_{\mathrm I}(q)>0$ and $\mathcal M_{\mathrm a}(q)\neq\emptyset$, the optimal power allocation is $p_m^\star=0$ for $m\notin\mathcal M_{\mathrm a}(q)$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wf}
p_m^\star(\nu)=\bigg[\frac{2(\rho_m-1)}{\sqrt{(\eta_m-\eta_{e,m})^2+4\eta_m\eta_{e,m}\rho_m}+\eta_m+\eta_{e,m}}\bigg]^+
\end{equation}
for $m\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm a}(q)$, where $\rho_m=\rho_m(\nu)\triangleq(\eta_m-\eta_{e,m})/(\nu\ln2)$, and the water level $\nu>0$ is the unique solution of $\sum_{m\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm a}(q)}p_m^\star(\nu)=P_{\mathrm I}(q)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{IEEEproof}
Since the objective is strictly concave and increasing in $\{p_m\}_{m\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm a}(q)}$ and Slater's condition holds, the Karush--Kuhn--Tucker (KKT) conditions are necessary and sufficient for optimality, and the power constraint is active at the optimum~\cite{Boyd2004}. Let $\nu>0$ denote the dual variable associated with the power constraint. The KKT conditions yield $\partial f_m/\partial p=\nu$ if $p_m^\star>0$ and $\partial f_m/\partial p|_{p=0}\le\nu$ otherwise. From~\eqref{eq:fm1}, the former is equivalent to $(1+\eta_mp)(1+\eta_{e,m}p)=\rho_m(\nu)$, i.e., a quadratic equation in $p$, whose positive root, written in a numerically stable form, is given by~\eqref{eq:wf}. The latter is equivalent to $\rho_m(\nu)\le1$, in which case~\eqref{eq:wf} gives $p_m^\star=0$. Finally, since $\sum_{m\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm a}(q)}p_m^\star(\nu)$ is continuous and strictly decreasing in $\nu$ over $(0,\max_{m\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm a}(q)}(\eta_m-\eta_{e,m})/\ln2)$, ranging from $+\infty$ to $0$, the water level $\nu$ satisfying the power constraint with equality is unique.
\end{IEEEproof}

Note that~\eqref{eq:wf} reduces to the classic water-filling solution $[1/(\nu\ln2)-1/\eta_m]^+$ when $\eta_{e,m}\to0$. Hence, \eqref{eq:wf} can be regarded as a secrecy water-filling solution, in which the LUs whose effective channel gains do not exceed that of Eve are switched off. Since $p_m^\star(\nu)$ is non-increasing in $\nu$, the water level can be efficiently found by the bisection method.

Next, let $G(q)\triangleq\sum_{m\in\mathcal M}f_m(p_m^\star;q)$ denote the objective value achieved by the optimal power allocation for a given $q$, where $G(q)=0$ if $\mathcal M_{\mathrm a}(q)=\emptyset$ or $P_{\mathrm I}(q)=0$. The optimal AN power per dimension is obtained by solving the one-dimensional problem
\begin{equation}\label{eq:qsearch}
q^\star=\arg\max_{0\le q\le q_{\max}}\ G(q),
\end{equation}
where $q_{\max}\triangleq\Ptot/(N-M)$. Increasing $q$ reduces the effective channel gains $\eta_{e,m}(q)$ of Eve at the cost of less power for the confidential signals, and~\eqref{eq:qsearch} finds the best tradeoff. Since $G(q)$ is not necessarily unimodal, \eqref{eq:qsearch} is solved by a coarse grid search over $[0,q_{\max}]$ followed by a fine grid search around the best grid point. The overall power allocation procedure is summarized in Algorithm~\ref{alg:power}.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Power Allocation for Given $\bt$}\label{alg:power}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $\{\eta_m,\xi_m\}_{m\in\mathcal M}$, $\psi$, $\Ptot$, and grid size $Q$.
\For{each $q$ in a uniform grid of $Q$ points over $[0,q_{\max}]$}
  \State Compute $\eta_{e,m}(q)$ and $\mathcal M_{\mathrm a}(q)$.
  \State Find $\nu$ by bisection such that $\sum_{m\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm a}(q)}p_m^\star(\nu)=P_{\mathrm I}(q)$, where $p_m^\star(\nu)$ is given by~\eqref{eq:wf}.
  \State Evaluate $G(q)$.
\EndFor
\State Repeat Steps 1--5 over a uniform grid of $Q$ points between the two neighbors of the best grid point.
\Ensure $q^\star$ and the corresponding $\bp^\star$.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Antenna Position Optimization}\label{sec:position}

For given $(\bp,q)$, (P3) reduces to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:P32}
(\mathrm{P3.2}):\ \max_{\bt}\ F(\bt)\quad\mathrm{s.t.}\ \eqref{eq:P1region},\ \eqref{eq:P1dist},
\end{equation}
where $F(\bt)\triangleq F(\bt,\bp,q)$ for brevity. Problem (P3.2) is non-convex since $F(\bt)$ is neither concave nor convex w.r.t. $\bt$ and constraint~\eqref{eq:P1dist} is non-convex. Similar to~\cite{ZhengTCOM2025}, we optimize the position of each MA in an element-wise manner with the positions of the other MAs being fixed, and update $\bt_n$ by the MM method~\cite{Sun2017}.

\textit{1) Dependence on $\bt_n$:} Let $\bl_n(\bt_n)\in\mathbb C^{M\times1}$ denote the conjugate transpose of the $n$-th row of $\bL(\bt)$, i.e.,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ln}
\bl_n(\bt_n)=\bK^{1/2}\Big[e^{-j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\bt_n^T\ba_1},\ldots,e^{-j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\bt_n^T\ba_M}\Big]^T.
\end{equation}
Since $\bL^H\bL=\sum_{i=1}^N\bl_i\bl_i^H$ and $\bL^H\gb=\sum_{i=1}^N\bl_ie^{j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\bt_i^T\ba_e}$, $\bPhi(\bt)$ and $\bv(\bt)$ can be expressed as
\begin{align}
\bPhi(\bt)&=\bGam_n+\bl_n(\bt_n)\bl_n^H(\bt_n),\label{eq:Phin}\\
\bv(\bt)&=\br_n+\bl_n(\bt_n)e^{j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\bt_n^T\ba_e},\label{eq:vn}
\end{align}
where $\bGam_n\triangleq\sum_{i\neq n}\bl_i(\bt_i)\bl_i^H(\bt_i)+N\bI_M$ and $\br_n\triangleq\sum_{i\neq n}\bl_i(\bt_i)e^{j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\bt_i^T\ba_e}$ do not depend on $\bt_n$. By the Sherman--Morrison formula~\cite{Horn2013},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sm}
\bOm(\bt)=\bGam_n^{-1}-\frac{\bGam_n^{-1}\bl_n(\bt_n)\bl_n^H(\bt_n)\bGam_n^{-1}}{1+\bl_n^H(\bt_n)\bGam_n^{-1}\bl_n(\bt_n)}.
\end{equation}
Hence, for given $\bGam_n^{-1}$ and $\br_n$, evaluating $\omega_m$, $c_m$, $d$, and thus $F$ as functions of $\bt_n$ requires only $\mathcal O(M^2)$ operations.

\textit{2) Gradient:} For $u\in\{x_n,y_n\}$, let $\bD_u\triangleq\diag(a_{1,u},\ldots,a_{M,u})$, where $a_{m,x}\triangleq\cos\theta_m\sin\phi_m$ and $a_{m,y}\triangleq\sin\theta_m$ are the entries of $\ba_m$, and let $a_{e,u}$ denote the corresponding entry of $\ba_e$. From~\eqref{eq:ln}--\eqref{eq:vn}, the derivatives of $\bl_n$, $\bv$, and $\bOm$ w.r.t. $u$ are given by
\begin{align}
\dot{\bl}_n&\triangleq\frac{\partial\bl_n}{\partial u}=-j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\bD_u\bl_n,\label{eq:dl}\\
\dot{\bv}&\triangleq\frac{\partial\bv}{\partial u}=j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\big(a_{e,u}\bI_M-\bD_u\big)\bl_ne^{j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\bt_n^T\ba_e},\label{eq:dv}\\
\dot{\bOm}&\triangleq\frac{\partial\bOm}{\partial u}=-\bOm\big(\dot{\bl}_n\bl_n^H+\bl_n\dot{\bl}_n^H\big)\bOm,\label{eq:dOm}
\end{align}
where~\eqref{eq:dOm} follows from $\partial\bPhi^{-1}=-\bPhi^{-1}(\partial\bPhi)\bPhi^{-1}$. Based on~\eqref{eq:dl}--\eqref{eq:dOm}, the derivatives $\dot\omega_m\triangleq\partial\omega_m/\partial u$, $\dot c_m\triangleq\partial c_m/\partial u$, and $\dot d\triangleq\partial d/\partial u$ are derived in closed form in Appendix~\ref{app:grad}. Let $\chi_m\triangleq\xi_mp_m$ and $\Upsilon\triangleq q\psi+\sbe$, whose derivatives are $\dot\chi_m=\kappa_ep_m\dot c_m$ and $\dot\Upsilon=\kappa_eq\dot d$, respectively. Moreover, $\dot\eta_m=-\eta_m\dot\omega_m/\omega_m$. By the chain rule, the derivative of $F$ w.r.t. $u$ is given by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dF}
\frac{\partial F}{\partial u}=\frac{1}{\ln2}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\bigg[\frac{p_m\dot\eta_m}{1+\eta_mp_m}-\frac{\kappa_ep_m\dot c_m}{\chi_m+\Upsilon}+\frac{\kappa_eq\chi_m\dot d}{\Upsilon(\chi_m+\Upsilon)}\bigg],
\end{equation}
and the gradient of $F$ w.r.t. $\bt_n$ is $\nabla_{\bt_n}F=[\partial F/\partial x_n,\ \partial F/\partial y_n]^T$.

\textit{3) Surrogate function:} With a slight abuse of notation, let $F(\bt_n)$ denote $F$ as a function of $\bt_n$, with the positions of the other MAs being fixed at their latest values. Since $F(\bt_n)$ is twice continuously differentiable and $\mathcal C$ is compact, $\nabla_{\bt_n}F$ is Lipschitz continuous over $\mathcal C$ with some constant $\mathcal L_n$. According to the descent lemma~\cite[Lemma~5.7]{Beck2017}, for any $\delta_n\ge\mathcal L_n$, the following quadratic function is a lower bound of $F(\bt_n)$ that is tight at the local point $\bt_n^{(\ell)}$ in the $\ell$-th iteration:
\begin{align}
F(\bt_n)\ge\hat F_n(\bt_n|\bt_n^{(\ell)})\triangleq\ &F(\bt_n^{(\ell)})+\nabla_{\bt_n}F(\bt_n^{(\ell)})^T\big(\bt_n-\bt_n^{(\ell)}\big)\nonumber\\
&-\frac{\delta_n}{2}\big\|\bt_n-\bt_n^{(\ell)}\big\|^2.\label{eq:surrogate}
\end{align}
Since $\mathcal L_n$ is difficult to obtain in closed form, $\delta_n$ is determined by backtracking~\cite{Beck2017}, i.e., starting from an initial value $\delta_n^{\mathrm{ini}}$, $\delta_n$ is doubled until the solution of the subproblem below satisfies $F(\bt_n^{(\ell+1)})\ge\hat F_n(\bt_n^{(\ell+1)}|\bt_n^{(\ell)})$, which is guaranteed once $\delta_n\ge\mathcal L_n$. To reduce the number of backtracking trials, we set $\delta_n^{\mathrm{ini}}=\max\{\delta_0,\delta_n^{\mathrm{pre}}/2\}$, where $\delta_0>0$ is a prescribed constant and $\delta_n^{\mathrm{pre}}$ is the value of $\delta_n$ accepted in the previous iteration.

\textit{4) Minimum-distance constraints:} Since $\|\bt_n-\bt_i\|^2$ is convex w.r.t. $\bt_n$, it is lower-bounded by its first-order Taylor expansion at $\bt_n^{(\ell)}$~\cite{ZhengTCOM2025}, i.e.,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:distlin}
\|\bt_n-\bt_i\|^2\ge\|\bDel_{n,i}^{(\ell)}\|^2+2\big(\bDel_{n,i}^{(\ell)}\big)^T\big(\bt_n-\bt_n^{(\ell)}\big),
\end{equation}
where $\bDel_{n,i}^{(\ell)}\triangleq\bt_n^{(\ell)}-\bt_i$. By replacing $\|\bt_n-\bt_i\|^2$ in~\eqref{eq:P1dist} with the right-hand side of~\eqref{eq:distlin}, we obtain the linear constraint
\begin{equation}\label{eq:linc}
\big(\bDel_{n,i}^{(\ell)}\big)^T\bt_n\ge\big(\bDel_{n,i}^{(\ell)}\big)^T\bt_n^{(\ell)}+\frac{\Dmin^2-\|\bDel_{n,i}^{(\ell)}\|^2}{2},\quad\forall i\neq n,
\end{equation}
whose feasible set is a convex subset of that of~\eqref{eq:P1dist} w.r.t. $\bt_n$.

\textit{5) Subproblem for MA $n$:} Based on~\eqref{eq:surrogate} and~\eqref{eq:linc}, $\bt_n$ is updated by solving
\begin{equation}\label{eq:P32n}
(\mathrm{P3.2}.n):\ \max_{\bt_n}\ \hat F_n(\bt_n|\bt_n^{(\ell)})\quad\mathrm{s.t.}\ \bt_n\in\mathcal C,\ \eqref{eq:linc}.
\end{equation}
By completing the square, (P3.2.$n$) is equivalent to projecting $\bt_n^{(\ell)}+\nabla_{\bt_n}F(\bt_n^{(\ell)})/\delta_n$ onto the convex polygon defined by $\mathcal C$ and~\eqref{eq:linc}, which is a convex quadratic program with two variables. Its optimal solution lies either at the projected point itself (if feasible), on an edge, or at a vertex of the polygon, and can thus be obtained exactly by enumerating the projections onto the $N+3$ constraint lines and their pairwise intersections, or by standard solvers. Since $\bt_n^{(\ell)}$ is feasible for (P3.2.$n$), the update satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mono}
F(\bt_n^{(\ell+1)})\ge\hat F_n(\bt_n^{(\ell+1)}|\bt_n^{(\ell)})\ge\hat F_n(\bt_n^{(\ell)}|\bt_n^{(\ell)})=F(\bt_n^{(\ell)}),
\end{equation}
and $\bt^{(\ell+1)}$ is feasible for (P3.2) due to~\eqref{eq:distlin}. Hence, the objective value of (P3.2) is non-decreasing after each element-wise update.

\subsection{Overall Algorithm, Convergence, and Complexity}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Two-Timescale AO Algorithm for (P3)}\label{alg:ao}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Statistical CSI, $\Ptot$, $\mathcal C$, $\Dmin$, $\delta_0$, and tolerance $\epsilon$.
\State Initialize a feasible $\bt^{(0)}$, obtain $(\bp^{(0)},q^{(0)})$ by Algorithm~\ref{alg:power}, and set $\ell=0$.
\Repeat
  \For{$n=1$ to $N$}
    \State Compute $\nabla_{\bt_n}F$ by~\eqref{eq:dF} and set $\delta_n=\delta_n^{\mathrm{ini}}$.
    \Repeat
      \State Solve (P3.2.$n$) to obtain $\bt_n^{(\ell+1)}$.
      \State \textbf{if} $F(\bt_n^{(\ell+1)})<\hat F_n(\bt_n^{(\ell+1)}|\bt_n^{(\ell)})$ \textbf{then} $\delta_n\leftarrow2\delta_n$.
    \Until{$F(\bt_n^{(\ell+1)})\ge\hat F_n(\bt_n^{(\ell+1)}|\bt_n^{(\ell)})$}
  \EndFor
  \State Obtain $(\bp',q')$ by Algorithm~\ref{alg:power} for $\bt^{(\ell+1)}$.
  \State If $F(\bt^{(\ell+1)},\bp',q')\ge F(\bt^{(\ell+1)},\bp^{(\ell)},q^{(\ell)})$, set $(\bp^{(\ell+1)},q^{(\ell+1)})=(\bp',q')$; otherwise, set $(\bp^{(\ell+1)},q^{(\ell+1)})=(\bp^{(\ell)},q^{(\ell)})$.
  \State $\ell\leftarrow\ell+1$.
\Until{the fractional increase of $F$ is below $\epsilon$}
\Ensure $\bt^\star=\bt^{(\ell)}$, $\bp^\star=\bp^{(\ell)}$, and $q^\star=q^{(\ell)}$.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The overall algorithm for solving (P3) is summarized in Algorithm~\ref{alg:ao}. Since (P3) is non-convex, the obtained solution depends on the initial antenna positions. To alleviate this issue, Algorithm~\ref{alg:ao} can be executed from multiple initial positions, e.g., the FPA array and several random feasible positions, and the solution with the largest objective value is selected.

\textit{Convergence:} In each iteration of Algorithm~\ref{alg:ao}, the element-wise position updates do not decrease the objective value of (P3) according to~\eqref{eq:mono}, and the power allocation is updated only if the objective value does not decrease. Hence, the objective value of (P3) is non-decreasing over the iterations. Moreover, it is upper-bounded due to the finite transmit power, e.g., $F\le\sum_{m=1}^M\log_2(1+\zeta_m(N-M)(\kappa_m+1)\Ptot)$ according to~\eqref{eq:etabound} and $p_m\le\Ptot$. Therefore, Algorithm~\ref{alg:ao} is guaranteed to converge.

\textit{Complexity:} In each iteration of Algorithm~\ref{alg:ao}, forming $\bPhi$ and $\bOm$ requires $\mathcal O(NM^2+M^3)$ operations. For each MA, $\bGam_n^{-1}$ is obtained from $\bOm$ by a rank-one update with $\mathcal O(M^2)$ operations, and the gradient in~\eqref{eq:dF} and each evaluation of $F$ require $\mathcal O(M^2)$ operations based on~\eqref{eq:sm}. Solving (P3.2.$n$) by enumeration requires $\mathcal O(N^3)$ operations. Algorithm~\ref{alg:power} requires $\mathcal O(QI_{\mathrm b}M)$ operations, where $I_{\mathrm b}$ is the number of bisection iterations. Hence, the complexity of Algorithm~\ref{alg:ao} is $\mathcal O\big(I_{\mathrm o}\big(NM^2+M^3+NJ(M^2+N^3)+QI_{\mathrm b}M\big)\big)$, where $I_{\mathrm o}$ is the number of iterations and $J$ is the average number of backtracking trials. Note that the complexity does not depend on the number of channel realizations, in contrast to the stochastic optimization methods based on channel samples~\cite{ZhaoTWC2021}. Moreover, Algorithm~\ref{alg:ao} is executed only once per frame, whereas the ZF precoding and AN generation in each CI require $\mathcal O(NM^2+M^3)$ operations.

\section{Numerical Results}\label{sec:sim}
% =============================================================================
In this section, we provide numerical results to validate the accuracy of the derived approximation and to evaluate the performance of the proposed two-timescale design.

\subsection{Simulation Setup and Benchmarks}

Unless otherwise specified, the BS is equipped with $N=8$ MAs to serve $M=3$ LUs, the movable region is a square with side length $A=4\lambda$, and $\Dmin=\lambda/2$. The elevation and azimuth AoDs of the LUs are independently and uniformly distributed within $[-\pi/3,\pi/3]$. To model a challenging eavesdropping scenario, Eve is located in a direction close to that of LU~1, i.e., $\theta_e=\theta_1+\Delta\cos\tilde\phi$ and $\phi_e=\phi_1+\Delta\sin\tilde\phi$, where $\tilde\phi$ is uniformly distributed over $[0,2\pi)$ and $\Delta=0.1$~rad denotes the angular offset between Eve and LU~1. The Rician factors are $\kappa_m=\kappa_e=10$~dB, the large-scale fading coefficients are normalized as $\beta_m=\beta_e=1$, and the noise powers are $\sigma_m^2=\sigma_e^2=\sigma^2$, such that $\Ptot/\sigma^2$ represents the transmit SNR, which is set to $20$~dB. For Algorithm~\ref{alg:ao}, we set $\delta_0=1$, $\epsilon=10^{-4}$, at most $60$ iterations, $Q=41$, and $60$ bisection iterations, and select the best solution obtained from three initializations, i.e., a $2\times4$ uniform planar array (UPA) with $\lambda/2$ spacing centered at the origin and two random feasible positions. All results are averaged over $100$ random realizations of the AoDs, which are shared by all schemes. For each obtained solution, the ESSR is evaluated by Monte Carlo simulations over $5\times10^3$ channel realizations with the exact ZF precoding and null-space AN in~\eqref{eq:zf}--\eqref{eq:sinre}, i.e., the closed-form approximations are used only for optimization. The proposed design is compared with the following benchmarks, which are used consistently in Figs.~\ref{fig:snr}--\ref{fig:region}:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textit{Rate-oriented MA:} The antenna positions are optimized to maximize the approximate ergodic sum rate of the LUs with equal power allocation as in~\cite{ZhengTCOM2025}, i.e., $\sum_{m=1}^M\log_2(1+\eta_m(\bt)\Ptot/M)$, which does not take Eve into account. This problem is solved by the element-wise MM method in Section~\ref{sec:position} with the same initializations, and then $(\bp,q)$ is optimized by Algorithm~\ref{alg:power} for the obtained positions. This benchmark combines the existing two-timescale MA design with AN, and thus reveals the gain of the secrecy-aware antenna positioning enabled by Proposition~\ref{prop:eve}.
\item \textit{Sparse FPA:} The BS adopts a $2\times4$ UPA that spans the entire region $\mathcal C$, i.e., with spacings $A/3$ and $A$ along the $x$- and $y$-axes, respectively, and $(\bp,q)$ is optimized by Algorithm~\ref{alg:power}.
\item \textit{Compact FPA:} The BS adopts the $2\times4$ UPA with $\lambda/2$ spacing, and $(\bp,q)$ is optimized by Algorithm~\ref{alg:power}.
\item \textit{Proposed w/o AN:} The antenna positions and $\bp$ are optimized by Algorithm~\ref{alg:ao} with $q=0$.
\end{itemize}
The benchmarks for the long-term power allocation are introduced in Section~\ref{sec:sim_pa}.

\begin{figure*}[t]

Fig.~\ref{fig:snr}(a) shows the ESSR versus the transmit SNR. The proposed design achieves the highest ESSR over the entire SNR range. At $\Ptot/\sigma^2=20$~dB, it improves the ESSR by 12.5\%, 13.3\%, and 27.1\% compared with the rate-oriented MA, the sparse FPA, and the compact FPA, respectively, and it outperforms the rate-oriented MA and the compact FPA in every one of the $100$ AoD realizations. Notably, the rate-oriented MA, whose antenna positions decorrelate the channels of the LUs but ignore Eve, performs comparably to the sparse FPA, which simply enlarges the aperture. This shows that the secrecy gain of the proposed design stems mainly from the Eve-aware positioning enabled by Proposition~\ref{prop:eve}, rather than from the enlarged aperture or the decorrelation among the LUs. As the SNR increases, the absolute gains of the proposed design over the compact FPA and the rate-oriented MA saturate at about 4.5 and 2.3~bps/Hz, respectively, i.e., the curves become parallel. This agrees with Remark~\ref{rem:highsnr}: the antenna positions do not change the high-SNR slope of the ESSR but improve its constant term. In contrast, the ESSR of the proposed design without AN saturates at high SNR, since the wiretap rates of Eve increase with the transmit power as fast as the rates of the LUs. At low SNR, the optimized AN power is (almost) zero, and the designs with and without AN achieve similar ESSRs.

Fig.~\ref{fig:snr}(b) shows the corresponding optimized AN power fraction $(N-M)q/\Ptot$, which increases with the SNR and saturates at high SNR, in agreement with the asymptotically optimal power splitting in~\eqref{eq:alphastar}. More importantly, the proposed design requires considerably less AN power than all the benchmarks, e.g., 0.31 versus 0.44, 0.40, and 0.47 for the rate-oriented MA, the sparse FPA, and the compact FPA, respectively, at $\Ptot/\sigma^2=20$~dB. Moreover, the rate-oriented MA reduces the AN power only marginally compared with the compact FPA, i.e., decorrelating the LUs alone does not save AN power. This verifies the AN-saving effect revealed in Remark~\ref{rem:highsnr}: by decorrelating the LoS component of Eve from those of the LUs, the proposed positions reduce the information leakage and increase the AN received by Eve, so that more power can be allocated to the confidential signals.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
% =============================================================================
This paper proposed a two-timescale secure transmission framework for an MA-enabled multiuser downlink system with a passive Eve, where the antenna positions and the long-term power allocation among the confidential signals and AN are optimized based on the statistical CSI, and ZF precoding and null-space AN are designed based on the instantaneous CSI of the LUs. We proved that the information leakage and the AN received by Eve are conditionally independent given the channels of the LUs, and derived a closed-form approximation of the ergodic wiretap rate, which shows that the antenna positions affect the ESSR only through three geometric quantities characterizing the decorrelation among the LUs, the information leakage, and the AN received by Eve. Based on this approximation, an AO algorithm was developed, where the power allocation admits a secrecy water-filling structure and the antenna positions are optimized element-wise by the MM method. Numerical results demonstrated that the proposed secrecy-aware antenna positioning outperforms both the rate-oriented two-timescale MA design and FPA arrays, enhances the secrecy performance even for a single LU, and reduces the AN power required for secure transmission. They also showed that the information--AN power splitting is the dominant component of the long-term power allocation, whereas the per-user power allocation is beneficial for LUs with heterogeneous large-scale fading at low-to-moderate SNR. Extending the proposed framework to multiple colluding or non-colluding eavesdroppers, multi-antenna eavesdroppers, imperfect statistical CSI of Eve, and imperfect instantaneous CSI of the LUs is an interesting direction for future work.

\section{Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:det}}\label{app:det}
% =============================================================================
For brevity, let $\bPhi\triangleq\bPhi_{\bF}$. We first prove~\eqref{eq:lem_c}. Replacing $(\bF^H\bF)^{-1}$ in the unnormalized ZF direction by $\bPhi^{-1}=(\E\{\bF^H\bF\})^{-1}$ yields $\wb_m(\bF)\approx\bF\bPhi^{-1}\be_m/\|\bF\bPhi^{-1}\be_m\|$. Then, we approximate the mean of $|\bb^H\bF\bPhi^{-1}\be_m|^2/\|\bF\bPhi^{-1}\be_m\|^2$ by the ratio of the means of its numerator and denominator, in the same spirit as~\cite[Lemma~1]{ZhangJSTSP2014}. Since $\E\{\bW\}=\mathbf 0$ and $\E\{\bW\mathbf x\mathbf x^H\bW^H\}=\|\mathbf x\|^2\bI_N$ for any deterministic $\mathbf x$, we have
\begin{align}
\E\{|\bb^H\bF\bPhi^{-1}\be_m|^2\}&=|\bb^H\Fb\bPhi^{-1}\be_m|^2+\|\bb\|^2\|\bPhi^{-1}\be_m\|^2\nonumber\\
&=\big|[\bPhi^{-1}\Fb^H\bb]_m\big|^2+\|\bb\|^2[\bPhi^{-2}]_{m,m},\label{eq:app1}\\
\E\{\|\bF\bPhi^{-1}\be_m\|^2\}&=\be_m^T\bPhi^{-1}\bPhi\bPhi^{-1}\be_m=[\bPhi^{-1}]_{m,m},\label{eq:app2}
\end{align}
which lead to~\eqref{eq:lem_c}. Next, we prove~\eqref{eq:lem_d}. Replacing $(\bF^H\bF)^{-1}$ in $\mathbf P_{\bF}\triangleq\bI_N-\mathbf P_{\bF}^\perp=\bF(\bF^H\bF)^{-1}\bF^H$ by $\bPhi^{-1}$ and using $\E\{\bW\bPhi^{-1}\bW^H\}=\tr(\bPhi^{-1})\bI_N$ yield
\begin{equation}\label{eq:app3}
\E\{\mathbf P_{\bF}\}\approx\bPi+\tr(\bPhi^{-1})\bI_N,\qquad\bPi\triangleq\Fb\bPhi^{-1}\Fb^H,
\end{equation}
where $\bPi$ and $\tr(\bPhi^{-1})\bI_N$ represent the parts of the signal subspace induced by the LoS and NLoS components, respectively. However, \eqref{eq:app3} lets the NLoS component capture the same fraction $\tr(\bPhi^{-1})$ of every direction, including the directions that have already been captured by the LoS component. Hence, it counts the energy of $\bb$ in these directions twice, and $\bb^H(\bI_N-\bPi-\tr(\bPhi^{-1})\bI_N)\bb$ may even become negative when $\bb$ is closely aligned with the column space of $\Fb$. To avoid this double counting, we let the NLoS component capture a fraction $\epsilon\in[0,1]$ of the complement of $\bPi$ only, i.e.,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:app4}
\E\{\mathbf P_{\bF}\}\approx\bPi+\epsilon(\bI_N-\bPi),
\end{equation}
which satisfies $\mathbf 0\preceq\bPi+\epsilon(\bI_N-\bPi)\preceq\bI_N$ since $\mathbf 0\preceq\bPi\prec\bI_N$, as shown below. The fraction $\epsilon$ is determined by the identity $\tr(\mathbf P_{\bF})=k$, which holds for every realization of $\bF$. Since $\tr(\bPi)=\tr\big(\bPhi^{-1}(\bPhi-N\bI_k)\big)=k-N\tr(\bPhi^{-1})$, taking the trace of both sides of~\eqref{eq:app4} yields $k-N\tr(\bPhi^{-1})+\epsilon\big(N-k+N\tr(\bPhi^{-1})\big)=k$, i.e., $\epsilon=N\tr(\bPhi^{-1})/(N-k+N\tr(\bPhi^{-1}))$. Therefore, $\E\{\mathbf P_{\bF}^\perp\}\approx(1-\epsilon)(\bI_N-\bPi)$ with $1-\epsilon=(N-k)/(N-k+N\tr(\bPhi^{-1}))$, which leads to~\eqref{eq:lem_d}.

\textit{Range:} Let $\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_k$ denote the singular values of $\Fb$. The nonzero eigenvalues of $\bPi$ are $\mu_i^2/(\mu_i^2+N)<1$, and thus $\mathbf 0\preceq\bPi\prec\bI_N$. Moreover, $\bPhi\succeq N\bI_k$ implies $0<\tr(\bPhi^{-1})\le k/N$, and thus $1-\epsilon\in[1-k/N,1)$. Hence, the right-hand side of~\eqref{eq:lem_d} lies in $[0,\|\bb\|^2]$. For~\eqref{eq:lem_c}, by the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality, $|[\bPhi^{-1}\Fb^H\bb]_m|^2\le\|\bb\|^2\be_m^T\bPhi^{-1}\Fb^H\Fb\bPhi^{-1}\be_m$, and $\|\bb\|^2[\bPhi^{-2}]_{m,m}\le N\|\bb\|^2[\bPhi^{-2}]_{m,m}$. Since $\be_m^T\bPhi^{-1}(\Fb^H\Fb+N\bI_k)\bPhi^{-1}\be_m=[\bPhi^{-1}]_{m,m}$, the right-hand side of~\eqref{eq:lem_c} lies in $[0,\|\bb\|^2]$.

\textit{Exactness for $\Fb=\mathbf 0$:} In this case, $\bPhi=N\bI_k$ and $\bPi=\mathbf 0$, and the right-hand sides of~\eqref{eq:lem_c} and~\eqref{eq:lem_d} reduce to $\|\bb\|^2/N$ and $\|\bb\|^2(1-k/N)$, respectively. On the other hand, since $\bW$ is unitarily invariant, i.e., $\mathbf Q\bW$ has the same distribution as $\bW$ for any unitary matrix $\mathbf Q$, and $\wb_m(\mathbf Q\bW)=\mathbf Q\wb_m(\bW)$, $\wb_m(\bW)$ is uniformly distributed on the unit sphere, and $\E\{\mathbf P_{\bW}^\perp\}$ commutes with every unitary matrix. Hence, $\E\{|\bb^H\wb_m(\bW)|^2\}=\|\bb\|^2/N$ and $\E\{\mathbf P_{\bW}^\perp\}=(1-k/N)\bI_N$, where the latter follows from $\tr(\mathbf P_{\bW}^\perp)=N-k$.

\textit{Asymptotic exactness for $\Fb=\varrho\Fb_0$:} As $\varrho\to\infty$, $\bF/\varrho\to\Fb_0$ almost surely. Since $\wb_m(\cdot)$ and $\mathbf P_{(\cdot)}^\perp$ are invariant to the scaling of their arguments and continuous at full-column-rank matrices, and $|\bb^H\wb_m(\bF)|^2$ and $\bb^H\mathbf P_{\bF}^\perp\bb$ are bounded by $\|\bb\|^2$, the dominated convergence theorem yields $\E\{|\bb^H\wb_m(\bF)|^2\}\to|\bb^H\wb_m(\Fb_0)|^2$ and $\E\{\bb^H\mathbf P_{\bF}^\perp\bb\}\to\bb^H\mathbf P_{\Fb_0}^\perp\bb$. On the other hand, $\varrho^2\bPhi^{-1}\to(\Fb_0^H\Fb_0)^{-1}$, $\bPi\to\Fb_0(\Fb_0^H\Fb_0)^{-1}\Fb_0^H$, and $\tr(\bPhi^{-1})\to0$. Hence, the right-hand side of~\eqref{eq:lem_d} converges to $\bb^H\mathbf P_{\Fb_0}^\perp\bb$, and that of~\eqref{eq:lem_c} converges to $|[(\Fb_0^H\Fb_0)^{-1}\Fb_0^H\bb]_m|^2/[(\Fb_0^H\Fb_0)^{-1}]_{m,m}=|\bb^H\wb_m(\Fb_0)|^2$, since the second term in the numerator of~\eqref{eq:lem_c} decays as $\mathcal O(\varrho^{-4})$ whereas the denominator decays as $\mathcal O(\varrho^{-2})$. This completes the proof.

\section{Derivatives of $\omega_m$, $c_m$, and $d$}\label{app:grad}
% =============================================================================
Let $o_m\triangleq[\bOm^2]_{m,m}$, $\tau\triangleq\tr(\bOm)$, $\tilde{\bv}\triangleq\bOm\bv$, and $\varpi\triangleq\bv^H\bOm\bv$. Since $\bOm$ is Hermitian, \eqref{eq:dOm} can be rewritten as $\dot{\bOm}=-(\bOm\dot{\bl}_n)(\bOm\bl_n)^H-(\bOm\bl_n)(\bOm\dot{\bl}_n)^H$. Hence, the derivatives of $\omega_m$, $o_m$, and $\tau$ w.r.t. $u\in\{x_n,y_n\}$ are given by
\begin{align}
\dot\omega_m&=[\dot{\bOm}]_{m,m}=-2\RE\big\{[\bOm\dot{\bl}_n]_m[\bOm\bl_n]_m^*\big\},\label{eq:a1}\\
\dot o_m&=2\RE\big\{[\bOm\dot{\bOm}]_{m,m}\big\}=-2\RE\big\{[\bOm^2\dot{\bl}_n]_m[\bOm\bl_n]_m^*\nonumber\\
&\qquad\qquad+[\bOm^2\bl_n]_m[\bOm\dot{\bl}_n]_m^*\big\},\label{eq:a2}\\
\dot\tau&=\tr(\dot{\bOm})=-2\RE\big\{\bl_n^H\bOm^2\dot{\bl}_n\big\},\label{eq:a3}
\end{align}
where~\eqref{eq:a2} follows from $[\dot{\bOm}\bOm]_{m,m}=[\bOm\dot{\bOm}]_{m,m}^*$. For $c_m$ in~\eqref{eq:cm}, the derivative of $\tilde{\bv}=\bOm\bv$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:a4}
\dot{\tilde{\bv}}=\dot{\bOm}\bv+\bOm\dot{\bv}=-\bOm\dot{\bl}_n\big(\bl_n^H\tilde{\bv}\big)-\bOm\bl_n\big(\dot{\bl}_n^H\tilde{\bv}\big)+\bOm\dot{\bv},
\end{equation}
and thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:a5}
\dot c_m=\frac{2\RE\big\{[\tilde{\bv}]_m^*[\dot{\tilde{\bv}}]_m\big\}+N\dot o_m-c_m\dot\omega_m}{\omega_m}.
\end{equation}
For $d=(1-\epsilon)(N-\varpi)$ in~\eqref{eq:d}, where $1-\epsilon=(N-M)/(N-M+N\tau)$ as in Appendix~\ref{app:det}, since $\bv^H\dot{\bOm}\bv=-2\RE\{(\tilde{\bv}^H\dot{\bl}_n)(\bl_n^H\tilde{\bv})\}$, we have
\begin{align}
\dot\varpi&=2\RE\{\tilde{\bv}^H\dot{\bv}\}-2\RE\big\{(\tilde{\bv}^H\dot{\bl}_n)(\bl_n^H\tilde{\bv})\big\},\label{eq:a6}\\
\dot d&=-(1-\epsilon)\bigg(\dot\varpi+\frac{N(N-\varpi)\,\dot\tau}{N-M+N\tau}\bigg).\label{eq:a7}
\end{align}
Substituting $\dot\eta_m=-\eta_m\dot\omega_m/\omega_m$, \eqref{eq:a5}, and~\eqref{eq:a7} into~\eqref{eq:dF} yields $\nabla_{\bt_n}F$. All the quantities in~\eqref{eq:a1}--\eqref{eq:a7} are obtained from matrix-vector products with $\bOm$, which require $\mathcal O(M^2)$ operations.
