\begin{abstract}
Movable antennas (MAs) can reshape the spatial correlation between the channels of legitimate users (LUs) and an eavesdropper (Eve), but repositioning them in every channel coherence interval incurs substantial movement and channel acquisition overhead. This paper investigates two-timescale secure transmission for an MA-enabled multiuser downlink system with a passive Eve, where the antenna positions are optimized based on statistical channel state information (CSI), and the zero-forcing (ZF) precoding and null-space artificial noise (AN) are designed based on the instantaneous CSI of the LUs. Specifically, the antenna positions and the long-term power allocation among the confidential signals and the AN are jointly optimized to maximize the ergodic secrecy sum rate (ESSR) subject to the transmit power, movable-region, and minimum-distance constraints. To solve this nonconvex stochastic problem, we first transform it into a deterministic problem by approximating the ergodic wiretap rate in closed form, where the information leakage and the AN received by Eve are proved to be conditionally independent given the channels of the LUs. The approximation reveals that the MAs can reduce the information leakage and increase the AN received by Eve, even for a single LU. Then, an alternating optimization algorithm is developed for the deterministic problem, where the power allocation is obtained in a semi-closed secrecy water-filling form, and each antenna position is updated by a grid search followed by majorization--minimization refinement, such that the objective value is guaranteed to converge. Simulation results demonstrate that the proposed design outperforms the benchmark schemes, e.g., by $14.8\%$ over the rate-oriented MA design, while requiring less AN power and fewer antennas.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
% =============================================================================
\IEEEPARstart{P}{hysical} layer security (PLS) exploits the randomness and spatial characteristics of wireless channels to protect confidential messages against eavesdropping, thereby complementing upper-layer cryptography~\cite{Wyner1975,Mukherjee2014,WuJSAC2018}. In multi-antenna systems, PLS is typically achieved by secure precoding and artificial noise (AN)~\cite{Goel2008,Zhou2010}, which steer the confidential signals toward the legitimate users (LUs) and degrade the channel of the eavesdropper (Eve), respectively. However, both techniques rely on the spatial separability between the LUs and Eve~\cite{Mukherjee2014}, which is limited for a conventional fixed-position antenna (FPA) array when Eve is located in a direction close to that of an LU and the line-of-sight (LoS) propagation is dominant. Recently, movable antennas (MAs)~\cite{ZhuMag2024,ZhuTWC2024Model} and the closely related fluid antennas~\cite{WongTWC2021} have emerged as promising technologies to overcome this limitation, where adjusting the antenna positions within a given region reshapes the channel correlation among different receivers without additional radio-frequency (RF) chains~\cite{MaTWC2024,ZhuTWC2024MU,ZhuCL2023}. For PLS, the transmit beamforming and the antenna positions were jointly optimized in~\cite{HuSPL2024} to maximize the secrecy rate with perfect channel state information (CSI), and the case without the instantaneous CSI of the eavesdroppers was considered in~\cite{HuTMC2024}. More recently, a deterministic equivalent of the ergodic secrecy rate has been derived in~\cite{XieArxiv2026} for a multiple-input multiple-output MA system with a multi-antenna Eve and its statistical CSI, and a sensing-assisted secure design has been developed in~\cite{ChenArxiv2026}, where the antenna positions enhance both the estimation of the angles of departure (AoDs) of Eve and the secrecy rate. However, the designs in~\cite{HuSPL2024,HuTMC2024,XieArxiv2026} optimize the antenna positions based on the instantaneous CSI of the legitimate receivers. Under fast fading, such designs require the antennas to be repositioned in each channel coherence interval (CI), thereby incurring nonnegligible movement delay and energy consumption. Moreover, they require the CSI over the entire movable region, whose acquisition entails channel measurements at many antenna positions~\cite{ZhengTCOM2025,HuTVT2025}. On the other hand, the design in~\cite{ChenArxiv2026} assumes LoS channels and thus does not capture the small-scale fading.

To reduce the movement and CSI acquisition overhead, the two-timescale design, which has been studied for intelligent reflecting surfaces~\cite{ZhaoTWC2021}, has been extended to MA systems~\cite{ZhengTCOM2025,HuTVT2025}, where the antenna positions are optimized based on the statistical CSI, and the beamforming is designed based on the instantaneous CSI at the selected positions. Specifically, the antenna positions of a multiuser downlink were optimized in~\cite{ZhengTCOM2025} to maximize the ergodic sum rate under maximum-ratio transmission and zero-forcing (ZF) precoding, and the multiuser uplink with linear and successive interference cancellation receivers was considered in~\cite{HuTVT2025}. However, these works focus on maximizing the ergodic sum rate without considering eavesdropping. In particular, the ergodic-rate approximations for ZF precoding adopted in~\cite{ZhangJSTSP2014,ZhengTCOM2025,HuTVT2025} characterize only the signal power received by the intended users, and the MAs bring no rate gain for a single user~\cite[Remark~2]{ZhengTCOM2025}. To the best of our knowledge, the two-timescale MA design for secure transmission remains unexplored.

In this paper, we jointly optimize the antenna positions and the long-term power allocation of an MA-enabled multiuser downlink with ZF precoding and null-space AN to maximize the ergodic secrecy sum rate (ESSR). The resulting design requires neither the instantaneous CSI of Eve nor the antenna movement in each CI. Since the ergodic wiretap rate depends on the random ZF directions and AN subspace induced by the channels of the LUs, we first prove that, given these channels, the information leakage and the AN received by Eve depend on orthogonal components of the wiretap channel and are thus conditionally independent. Based on this property, we derive a closed-form approximation of the ergodic wiretap rate, which captures the LoS correlation between Eve and the LUs and is accurate over a wide range of Rician factors. The approximation reveals that, unlike in the rate-oriented design, the MAs enhance secrecy by reducing the information leakage and increasing the AN received by Eve, even for a single LU. Then, we develop an alternating optimization (AO) algorithm for the resulting deterministic problem. For given antenna positions and AN power, the optimal power allocation among the LUs follows a secrecy water-filling structure, which switches off the LUs whose effective channel gains do not exceed that of Eve. For a given power allocation, each antenna position is updated by a grid search followed by majorization--minimization (MM) refinement, where a rank-one structure of the objective function renders the grid search affordable. The objective value of the AO algorithm is guaranteed to converge, and its complexity does not depend on the number of channel realizations. Finally, simulation results show that the proposed design outperforms the rate-oriented two-timescale MA design~\cite{ZhengTCOM2025}, antenna selection (AS) from twice as many antennas, and both compact and sparse FPA arrays, while requiring considerably less AN power. With only four MAs, it even outperforms a compact FPA array with $12$ antennas. The secrecy gain persists for a single LU, against an Eve that treats the inter-user interference as noise, under moderate AoD errors, and for independent AoDs of Eve and the LUs.

As illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:system}, we consider the downlink of a multiuser system, where a base station (BS) equipped with $N$ MAs serves $M$ single-antenna LUs in the presence of a single-antenna passive Eve. Let $\mathcal M\triangleq\{1,\ldots,M\}$ and $\mathcal N\triangleq\{1,\ldots,N\}$ denote the index sets of the LUs and the MAs, respectively, where $N\ge M+1$ is required to transmit AN in the null space of the channels of the LUs. The MAs are connected to the RF chains via flexible cables and can move within a two-dimensional square region $\mathcal C\triangleq[-A/2,A/2]\times[-A/2,A/2]$ of side length $A$~\cite{ZhuMag2024,ZhuTWC2024Model}. In the local Cartesian coordinate system of $\mathcal C$, the position of the $n$-th MA is denoted by $\bt_n\triangleq[x_n,y_n]^{\mathrm T}\in\mathcal C$, and the positions of all the MAs are collected in $\bt\triangleq[\bt_1^{\mathrm T},\ldots,\bt_N^{\mathrm T}]^{\mathrm T}\in\mathbb R^{2N\times1}$. To mitigate the mutual coupling, the distance between any two MAs is required to be no smaller than a minimum distance $\Dmin$, and the residual coupling is neglected.

Since the size of $\mathcal C$ is much smaller than the propagation distance, moving the MAs changes only the phases of the LoS paths, whereas their AoDs and amplitudes remain unchanged~\cite{ZhuTWC2024Model}. Let $\theta_m\in[-\pi/2,\pi/2]$ and $\phi_m\in[-\pi/2,\pi/2]$ denote the elevation and azimuth AoDs of the LoS path from the BS to the $m$-th LU, respectively, and define $\ba_m\triangleq[\cos\theta_m\sin\phi_m,\ \sin\theta_m]^{\mathrm T}$. Then, the propagation distance of the LoS path from the $n$-th MA differs from that from the origin of $\mathcal C$ by $\bt_n^{\mathrm T}\ba_m$, and the LoS component of the channel from the BS to the $m$-th LU is given by~\cite{ZhengTCOM2025}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hbar}
\hb_m(\bt)\triangleq\Big[e^{j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\bt_1^{\mathrm T}\ba_m},\ldots,e^{j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\bt_N^{\mathrm T}\ba_m}\Big]^{\mathrm T}\in\mathbb C^{N\times1},
\end{equation}
where $\lambda$ is the carrier wavelength. As in~\cite{ZhengTCOM2025,HuTVT2025}, the channel from the BS to the $m$-th LU is modeled by Rician fading, i.e.,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hm}
\bh_m(\bt)=\sqrt{\frac{\kappa_m\beta_m}{\kappa_m+1}}\,\hb_m(\bt)+\sqrt{\frac{\beta_m}{\kappa_m+1}}\,\htl_m,
\end{equation}
where $\beta_m$ is the large-scale fading coefficient, $\kappa_m\ge0$ is the Rician factor, and $\htl_m\sim\CN(\mathbf 0,\bI_N)$ is the non-LoS (NLoS) component.\footnote{The NLoS components at different antennas are assumed to be independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.)~\cite{ZhengTCOM2025,HuTVT2025}. This idealized rich-scattering model is adopted for analytical tractability; the minimum distance $\Dmin\ge\lambda/2$ reduces, but does not eliminate, the spatial correlation in practice.} Similarly, the channel from the BS to Eve is given by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:g}
\bg(\bt)=\sqrt{\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm e}\beta_{\mathrm e}}{\kappa_{\mathrm e}+1}}\,\gb(\bt)+\sqrt{\frac{\beta_{\mathrm e}}{\kappa_{\mathrm e}+1}}\,\gtl,
\end{equation}
where $\beta_{\mathrm e}$, $\kappa_{\mathrm e}$, and $\gtl\sim\CN(\mathbf 0,\bI_N)$ are defined similarly to those in~\eqref{eq:hm}, and $\gb(\bt)$ is given by~\eqref{eq:hbar} with $\ba_m$ replaced by $\ba_{\mathrm e}\triangleq[\cos\theta_{\mathrm e}\sin\phi_{\mathrm e},\ \sin\theta_{\mathrm e}]^{\mathrm T}$ for the elevation and azimuth AoDs $\theta_{\mathrm e}$ and $\phi_{\mathrm e}$ of Eve. The NLoS components $\htl_1,\ldots,\htl_M$ and $\gtl$ are mutually independent. Let $\bH(\bt)\triangleq[\bh_1(\bt),\ldots,\bh_M(\bt)]\in\mathbb C^{N\times M}$ denote the channel matrix from the BS to the LUs, and let $\Hb(\bt)\triangleq[\hb_1(\bt),\ldots,\hb_M(\bt)]$ and $\Htl\triangleq[\htl_1,\ldots,\htl_M]$ collect its LoS and NLoS components, respectively. Moreover, $\bK\triangleq\diag(\kappa_1,\ldots,\kappa_M)$ collects the Rician factors of the LUs.

Unlike the instantaneous CSI over the entire region $\mathcal C$, the statistical CSI, i.e., $\{\beta_m,\kappa_m,\theta_m,\phi_m\}_{m\in\mathcal M}$ of the LUs and $\{\beta_{\mathrm e},\kappa_{\mathrm e},\theta_{\mathrm e},\phi_{\mathrm e}\}$ of Eve, is determined by the large-scale fading and the LoS geometry, which vary much more slowly than the small-scale fading. Following~\cite{HuTMC2024}, we assume that the statistical CSI of Eve is known at the BS.\footnote{The statistical CSI of Eve can be obtained, e.g., from the prior knowledge of the location of Eve, by sensing its direction~\cite{ChenArxiv2026}, or from its historical signals if it is an untrusted node.}

Accordingly, we adopt the two-timescale protocol in Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol}. The time is divided into frames, each of which consists of $N_{\mathrm{CI}}\gg1$ CIs over which the statistical CSI remains constant. At the beginning of each frame, the BS optimizes the antenna positions $\bt$ and the long-term power allocation $(\bp,q)$ (see Section~\ref{sec:zfan}) based on the statistical CSI and moves the MAs accordingly. Then, in each CI, the BS estimates $\bH(\bt)$ via uplink pilots by exploiting the channel reciprocity of time-division duplexing, and designs the precoding and AN. For ease of exposition, $\bH(\bt)$ is assumed to be perfectly estimated, whereas $\gtl$ is never available at the BS. Hence, the MAs are moved only once per frame, and the CSI of the LUs is required only at the current antenna positions. The movement delay, which is amortized over the $N_{\mathrm{CI}}$ CIs of a frame, and the pilot overhead are not included in the ergodic rates defined below.

\subsection{Signal Model and Ergodic Secrecy Rate}

In each CI, the BS transmits the confidential symbols $s_m\sim\CN(0,1)$, $m\in\mathcal M$, together with AN, and the transmit signal is given by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:x}
\bx=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\bw_m s_m+\bz,
\end{equation}
where $\bw_m\in\mathbb C^{N\times1}$ is the precoding vector for the $m$-th LU, and $\bz\sim\CN(\mathbf 0,\bSig)$ is the AN with covariance matrix $\bSig\succeq\mathbf 0$. The symbols $s_1,\ldots,s_M$ and the AN $\bz$ are mutually independent. The transmit power satisfies $\sum_{m=1}^M\|\bw_m\|^2+\tr(\bSig)\le\Ptot$, where $\Ptot$ is the maximum transmit power of the BS. Since $\bw_m$ and $\bSig$ depend on the instantaneous CSI of the LUs, they are denoted by $\bw_m(\bH(\bt))$ and $\bSig(\bH(\bt))$.

The signal received at the $m$-th LU is $y_m=\bh_m^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\bx+n_m$, where $n_m\sim\CN(0,\sigma_m^2)$ is the additive white Gaussian noise. The resulting signal-to-interference-plus-noise ratio (SINR) of the $m$-th LU is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sinr_gen}
\gamma_m=\frac{|\bh_m^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\bw_m|^2}{\sum_{i=1,i\neq m}^{M}|\bh_m^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\bw_i|^2+\bh_m^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\bSig\bh_m(\bt)+\sigma_m^2}.
\end{equation}
The signal received at Eve is $y_{\mathrm e}=\bg^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\bx+n_{\mathrm e}$ with $n_{\mathrm e}\sim\CN(0,\sigma_{\mathrm e}^2)$. Since the receiver of a passive Eve is unknown to the BS, we consider the worst case where Eve can cancel the signals of the other LUs when intercepting $s_m$, e.g., via multiuser detection~\cite{ZhuTWC2014}. This worst-case model yields a secrecy guarantee regardless of the decoding capability of Eve. The SINR of Eve for $s_m$ is then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sinre_gen}
\gamma_{\mathrm{e},m}=\frac{|\bg^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\bw_m|^2}{\bg^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\bSig\bg(\bt)+\sigma_{\mathrm e}^2},
\end{equation}
which upper-bounds that of an Eve treating the signals of the other LUs as interference.

Since the instantaneous CSI of Eve is unknown, the confidential message of each LU is encoded over the CIs of a frame, which experience independent realizations of the NLoS components. Under this coding scheme, an achievable ergodic secrecy rate of the $m$-th LU is given by~\cite{Gopala2008,ZhuTWC2014}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:esr}
R_m^{\mathrm{sec}}\triangleq\big[\bar R_m-\bar R_{\mathrm{e},m}\big]^+,
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ergodic}
\bar R_m\triangleq\E\{\log_2(1+\gamma_m)\},\quad \bar R_{\mathrm{e},m}\triangleq\E\{\log_2(1+\gamma_{\mathrm{e},m})\}
\end{equation}
denote the ergodic rate of the $m$-th LU and the ergodic wiretap rate of Eve for $s_m$, respectively, with the expectations taken over $\Htl$ and $\gtl$ for a given $\bt$. The ESSR is then $\sum_{m=1}^MR_m^{\mathrm{sec}}$.

\subsection{Problem Formulation}

We aim to maximize the ESSR by jointly optimizing the antenna positions and the short-timescale policy $\mathcal W\triangleq\{\bw_1(\bH(\bt)),\ldots,\bw_M(\bH(\bt)),\bSig(\bH(\bt))\}$, i.e., the mapping from the instantaneous CSI of the LUs to the precoders and the AN covariance matrix. The corresponding problem is formulated as
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:P1}
\begin{align}
(\mathrm{P1}):\ \max_{\bt,\,\mathcal W}\ \ & \sum_{m=1}^{M}R_m^{\mathrm{sec}}\label{eq:P1obj}\\
\mathrm{s.t.}\ \ & \sum_{m=1}^M\big\|\bw_m(\bH(\bt))\big\|^2+\tr\big(\bSig(\bH(\bt))\big)\le\Ptot,\nonumber\\
& \bSig(\bH(\bt))\succeq\mathbf 0,\ \forall\bH(\bt),\label{eq:P1pow}\\
& \bt_n\in\mathcal C,\ \forall n\in\mathcal N,\label{eq:P1region}\\
& \|\bt_n-\bt_i\|\ge\Dmin,\ \forall n,i\in\mathcal N,\ n\neq i.\label{eq:P1dist}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
Here, \eqref{eq:P1pow} must hold for every realization of $\bH(\bt)$.

Problem (P1) is a nonconvex stochastic optimization problem, which is difficult to solve for two reasons. First, the ergodic rates in~\eqref{eq:ergodic} have no closed-form expressions, and the policy $\mathcal W$ has to be designed for every realization of $\bH(\bt)$. Second, since the phases of the LoS components in~\eqref{eq:hbar} vary with $\bt$, the objective function is nonconcave in $\bt$, and the minimum-distance constraints in~\eqref{eq:P1dist} are nonconvex. To address the first difficulty, Section~\ref{sec:analysis} restricts $\mathcal W$ to ZF precoding and null-space AN with a long-term power allocation and approximates the ergodic rates in closed form, thereby yielding a deterministic problem with respect to the long-term variables only. To address the second difficulty, Section~\ref{sec:opt} develops an AO algorithm for this problem.

\section{Two-Timescale ZF-AN Design and Ergodic Secrecy Rate Analysis}\label{sec:analysis}
% =============================================================================

\subsection{Short-Timescale ZF Precoding and Null-Space AN}\label{sec:zfan}

To avoid designing $\mathcal W$ for every realization of $\bH(\bt)$, we restrict the short-timescale policy to ZF precoding and null-space AN with a long-term power allocation. Specifically, in each CI, the precoder of the $m$-th LU is $\bw_m(\bH(\bt))=\sqrt{p_m}\,\wb_m$, where $p_m\ge0$ denotes the power allocated to the $m$-th LU, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zf}
\wb_m\triangleq\frac{\bH(\bt)\big(\bH^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\bH(\bt)\big)^{-1}\be_m}{\big\|\bH(\bt)\big(\bH^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\bH(\bt)\big)^{-1}\be_m\big\|}
\end{equation}
is the normalized ZF precoding vector of the $m$-th LU, which satisfies $\bh_i^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\wb_m=0$, $\forall i\neq m$. Meanwhile, the AN is isotropically distributed in the null space of $\bH^{\mathrm H}(\bt)$~\cite{Zhou2010,ZhuTWC2014}, i.e., $\bSig(\bH(\bt))=q\,\PHp$, where $q\ge0$ denotes the AN power per dimension, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:an}
\PHp\triangleq\bI_N-\bH(\bt)\big(\bH^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\bH(\bt)\big)^{-1}\bH^{\mathrm H}(\bt)
\end{equation}
is the orthogonal projection onto the null space of $\bH^{\mathrm H}(\bt)$. Since $\bH(\bt)$ has full column rank with probability one, we have $\bH^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\PHp=\mathbf 0$ and $\tr(\PHp)=N-M$. Hence, the AN causes no interference to the LUs, and the transmit power in each CI is $\sum_{m=1}^M p_m+(N-M)q$.

We refer to $\bp\triangleq[p_1,\ldots,p_M]^{\mathrm T}$ and $q$ as the long-term power allocation, which is optimized based on the statistical CSI and remains fixed within a frame. In contrast, $\wb_m$ and $\PHp$ are updated in each CI according to $\bH(\bt)$. With this design, the SINRs in~\eqref{eq:sinr_gen} and~\eqref{eq:sinre_gen} reduce to
\begin{align}
\gamma_m&=\frac{p_m|\bh_m^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\wb_m|^2}{\sigma_m^2}=\frac{p_m}{\sigma_m^2\big[(\bH^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\bH(\bt))^{-1}\big]_{m,m}},\label{eq:snr}\\
\gamma_{\mathrm{e},m}&=\frac{p_m|\bg^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\wb_m|^2}{q\,\bg^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\PHp\bg(\bt)+\sigma_{\mathrm e}^2},\label{eq:sinre}
\end{align}
where the second equality in~\eqref{eq:snr} follows from $|\bh_m^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\wb_m|^2=1/[(\bH^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\bH(\bt))^{-1}]_{m,m}$ by~\eqref{eq:zf}.

The adopted ZF-AN design requires only the instantaneous CSI of the LUs, and no optimization problem needs to be solved in each CI, since $\bp$ and $q$ depend only on the statistical CSI. Moreover, the equal power allocation in~\cite{ZhengTCOM2025} and the information--AN power splitting with equal power per LU are special cases of $(\bp,q)$. With the ZF-AN design, (P1) is restricted to
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:P2}
\begin{align}
(\mathrm{P2}):\ \max_{\bt,\bp,q}\ \ & \sum_{m=1}^{M}R_m^{\mathrm{sec}}(\bt,p_m,q)\label{eq:P2obj}\\
\mathrm{s.t.}\ \ & \sum_{m=1}^{M}p_m+(N-M)q\le\Ptot,\label{eq:P2pow}\\
& p_m\ge0,\ \forall m\in\mathcal M,\quad q\ge0,\label{eq:P2pos}\\
& \bt_n\in\mathcal C,\ \forall n\in\mathcal N,\label{eq:P2region}\\
& \|\bt_n-\bt_i\|\ge\Dmin,\ \forall n,i\in\mathcal N,\ n\neq i,\label{eq:P2dist}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
where $R_m^{\mathrm{sec}}(\bt,p_m,q)$ denotes~\eqref{eq:esr} with the SINRs in~\eqref{eq:snr} and~\eqref{eq:sinre}, and~\eqref{eq:P2pow} and~\eqref{eq:P2pos} guarantee~\eqref{eq:P1pow} in every CI. Since the ZF-AN design is a particular policy $\mathcal W$, every feasible solution of (P2) is feasible for (P1), i.e., (P2) is a restriction of (P1). However, (P2) is still intractable, since the ergodic rates in~\eqref{eq:P2obj} have no closed-form expressions. Next, we approximate the ergodic rates of the LUs and Eve in Sections~\ref{sec:lu} and~\ref{sec:eve}, respectively.

\subsection{Ergodic Rate of the LUs}\label{sec:lu}

Define $\bB\triangleq\diag\big(\frac{\beta_1}{\kappa_1+1},\ldots,\frac{\beta_M}{\kappa_M+1}\big)$ and $\bL(\bt)\triangleq\Hb(\bt)\bK^{1/2}$. From~\eqref{eq:hm}, the channel matrix of the LUs can be written as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Hdecomp}
\bH(\bt)=\Hn(\bt)\bB^{1/2},\qquad\Hn(\bt)\triangleq\bL(\bt)+\Htl,
\end{equation}
where $\Hn(\bt)$ is the normalized channel matrix, whose LoS component is weighted by the square roots of the Rician factors. Accordingly, we define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Phi}
\bPhi(\bt)\triangleq\bL^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\bL(\bt)+N\bI_M,\qquad \bOm(\bt)\triangleq\bPhi^{-1}(\bt),
\end{equation}
where $\bPhi(\bt)=\E\{\Hn^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\Hn(\bt)\}$ is the mean of the Gram matrix of the normalized channels of the LUs.

Since $\log_2(1+p_m/(\sigma_m^2z))$ is convex in $z>0$, applying Jensen's inequality to~\eqref{eq:snr} and using~\eqref{eq:Hdecomp} yield
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lujensen}
\bar R_m\ge\log_2\bigg(1+\frac{p_m[\bB]_{m,m}}{\sigma_m^2\E\big\{[(\Hn^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\Hn(\bt))^{-1}]_{m,m}\big\}}\bigg).
\end{equation}
Following~\cite{ZhangJSTSP2014}, we approximate the Gram matrix $\Hn^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\Hn(\bt)$ by a central Wishart matrix with $N$ degrees of freedom and the same mean $\bPhi(\bt)$, whose inverse has the mean $N\bOm(\bt)/(N-M)$. Substituting this mean into~\eqref{eq:lujensen} yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Rm}
\bar R_m\approx\tilde R_m(\bt,p_m)\triangleq\log_2\big(1+\eta_m(\bt)\,p_m\big),
\end{equation}
where $\eta_m(\bt)\triangleq\zeta_m(N-M)/(N\omega_m(\bt))$ is the effective channel gain of the $m$-th LU, $\zeta_m\triangleq\beta_m/((\kappa_m+1)\sigma_m^2)$, and $\omega_m(\bt)\triangleq[\bOm(\bt)]_{m,m}$. The approximation in~\eqref{eq:Rm} is equivalent to that in~\cite[Sec.~IV-A]{ZhengTCOM2025}. For Rayleigh fading, the central Wishart model is exact, and~\eqref{eq:Rm} reduces to the well-known lower bound $\log_2(1+\zeta_mp_m(N-M))$~\cite{Ngo2013}. In the LoS-dominant regime, \eqref{eq:Rm} is conservative, as shown in Section~\ref{sec:sim_acc}. Moreover, the antenna positions affect $\tilde R_m(\bt,p_m)$ only through $\omega_m(\bt)$. Since $\bPhi(\bt)\succeq N\bI_M$ and $[\bOm(\bt)]_{m,m}\ge1/[\bPhi(\bt)]_{m,m}=1/(N(\kappa_m+1))$~\cite{Horn2013}, we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:etabound}
\zeta_m(N-M)\le\eta_m(\bt)\le\zeta_m(N-M)(\kappa_m+1),
\end{equation}
where the upper bound is achieved if $\hb_m^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\hb_i(\bt)=0$ for all $i\neq m$ with $\kappa_i>0$. Hence, $1/(N\omega_m(\bt))\in[1,\kappa_m+1]$ is the LoS gain of the $m$-th LU that survives the ZF precoding. The antenna positions can improve this gain by decorrelating the LoS components of the LUs.

\subsection{Ergodic Wiretap Rate of Eve}\label{sec:eve}

Unlike $\gamma_m$ in~\eqref{eq:snr}, which depends on $\bH(\bt)$ only through $[(\bH^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\bH(\bt))^{-1}]_{m,m}$, the SINR of Eve in~\eqref{eq:sinre} depends on the random ZF direction $\wb_m$ and AN subspace, both determined by $\bH(\bt)$. This dependence is the main difficulty in deriving $\bar R_{\mathrm{e},m}$. To address this issue, we first characterize the information leakage and the AN received by Eve given $\bH(\bt)$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:indep}
Conditioned on $\bH(\bt)$, the information leakage $X_m\triangleq|\bg^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\wb_m|^2$ and the AN power $Y\triangleq\bg^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\PHp\bg(\bt)$ received by Eve are independent, and their conditional means and variances are given by
\begin{align}
\E\{X_m|\bH(\bt)\}&=\tilde\beta_{\mathrm e}\big(\kappa_{\mathrm e}\bar X_m+1\big),\nonumber\\
\mathrm{Var}\{X_m|\bH(\bt)\}&=\tilde\beta_{\mathrm e}^2\big(2\kappa_{\mathrm e}\bar X_m+1\big),\label{eq:EX}\\
\E\{Y|\bH(\bt)\}&=\tilde\beta_{\mathrm e}\big(\kappa_{\mathrm e}\bar Y+N-M\big),\nonumber\\
\mathrm{Var}\{Y|\bH(\bt)\}&=\tilde\beta_{\mathrm e}^2\big(2\kappa_{\mathrm e}\bar Y+N-M\big),\label{eq:EY}
\end{align}
where $\tilde\beta_{\mathrm e}\triangleq\beta_{\mathrm e}/(\kappa_{\mathrm e}+1)$, and $\bar X_m\triangleq|\gb^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\wb_m|^2$ and $\bar Y\triangleq\gb^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\PHp\gb(\bt)$ are the LoS counterparts of $X_m$ and $Y$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{IEEEproof}
See Appendix~\ref{app:indep}.
\end{IEEEproof}

Lemma~\ref{lem:indep} reveals that, given the channels of the LUs, the information leakage is determined only by the component of the wiretap channel along the ZF direction, whereas the AN received by Eve is determined only by its component in the null space of $\bH^{\mathrm H}(\bt)$. Moreover, the conditional relative variances of $X_m$ and $Y$, i.e., $(2\kappa_{\mathrm e}\bar X_m+1)/(\kappa_{\mathrm e}\bar X_m+1)^2$ and $(2\kappa_{\mathrm e}\bar Y+N-M)/(\kappa_{\mathrm e}\bar Y+N-M)^2$, are small when the LoS leakage $\kappa_{\mathrm e}\bar X_m$ is strong and when $N-M$ or $\kappa_{\mathrm e}\bar Y$ is large, respectively. Since $\bar X_m$ and $\bar Y$ also vary with $\Htl$, e.g., $\mathrm{Var}\{X_m\}=\tilde\beta_{\mathrm e}^2(2\kappa_{\mathrm e}\E\{\bar X_m\}+1+\kappa_{\mathrm e}^2\mathrm{Var}\{\bar X_m\})$, these conditions alone do not ensure the concentration of $X_m$ and $Y$. Nevertheless, as in~\cite[Lemma~1]{ZhangJSTSP2014}, we approximate $\bar R_{\mathrm{e},m}=\E\{\log_2(1+p_mX_m/(qY+\sigma_{\mathrm e}^2))\}$ by $\log_2(1+p_m\E\{X_m\}/(q\E\{Y\}+\sigma_{\mathrm e}^2))$, which is accurate when $X_m$ and $Y$ concentrate around their means and is asymptotically exact as $\kappa_{\mathrm e},\kappa_m\to\infty$, $\forall m\in\mathcal M$, provided that the LoS components of the LUs are linearly independent. Since the rate is concave in $X_m$ and convex in $Y$, this approximation is neither an upper nor a lower bound, and its accuracy is evaluated in Section~\ref{sec:sim_acc}. Using Lemma~\ref{lem:indep} and taking the expectation over $\Htl$, we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Re0}
\bar R_{\mathrm{e},m}\approx\log_2\bigg(1+\frac{p_m\big(\kappa_{\mathrm e}\E\{\bar X_m\}+1\big)}{q\big(\kappa_{\mathrm e}\E\{\bar Y\}+N-M\big)+\sbe}\bigg),
\end{equation}
where $\sbe\triangleq\sigma_{\mathrm e}^2/\tilde\beta_{\mathrm e}$ is the normalized noise power of Eve.

However, $\E\{\bar X_m\}$ and $\E\{\bar Y\}$ in~\eqref{eq:Re0}, i.e., the average LoS energies of Eve along the ZF direction of the $m$-th LU and in the AN subspace, respectively, still have no closed-form expressions. Since $\wb_m$ and $\PHp$ are invariant to the scaling of the columns of $\bH(\bt)$, they depend on $\bH(\bt)$ only through $\Hn(\bt)$ in~\eqref{eq:Hdecomp}. As derived in Appendix~\ref{app:det}, replacing the inverse of the random Gram matrix $\Hn^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\Hn(\bt)$ with the inverse of its mean $\bPhi(\bt)$, where the NLoS contribution to the AN subspace is calibrated by the identity $\tr(\PHp)=N-M$, yields $\E\{\bar X_m\}\approx c_m(\bt)$ and $\E\{\bar Y\}\approx d(\bt)$ with
\begin{align}
c_m(\bt)&\triangleq\frac{\big|[\bOm(\bt)\bv(\bt)]_m\big|^2+N[\bOm^2(\bt)]_{m,m}}{\omega_m(\bt)},\label{eq:cm}\\
d(\bt)&\triangleq\frac{(N-M)\big(N-\bv^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\bOm(\bt)\bv(\bt)\big)}{N-M+N\tr(\bOm(\bt))},\label{eq:d}
\end{align}
where $\bv(\bt)\triangleq\bL^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\gb(\bt)=\bK^{1/2}\Hb^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\gb(\bt)$. Substituting them into~\eqref{eq:Re0}, the ergodic wiretap rate of Eve for $s_m$ is approximated by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Re}
\bar R_{\mathrm{e},m}\approx\tilde R_{\mathrm{e},m}(\bt,p_m,q)\triangleq\log_2\bigg(1+\frac{\xi_m(\bt)\,p_m}{q\,\psi(\bt)+\sbe}\bigg),
\end{equation}
where $\xi_m(\bt)\triangleq\kappa_ec_m(\bt)+1$ and $\psi(\bt)\triangleq\kappa_ed(\bt)+N-M$ represent the normalized information leakage for the $m$-th LU and the normalized AN power received by Eve, respectively. The following proposition characterizes these approximations.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:eve}
The quantities $c_m(\bt)$, $d(\bt)$, $\E\{\bar X_m\}$, and $\E\{\bar Y\}$ lie in $[0,N]$. Moreover, $c_m(\bt)=\E\{\bar X_m\}$ and $d(\bt)=\E\{\bar Y\}$ if $\kappa_m=0$, $\forall m\in\mathcal M$. For linearly independent LoS components of the LUs, $c_m(\bt)-\E\{\bar X_m\}$ and $d(\bt)-\E\{\bar Y\}$ vanish as $\bK=\kappa\bK_0$ with $\kappa\to\infty$ for any diagonal $\bK_0\succ\mathbf 0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{IEEEproof}
See Appendix~\ref{app:det}.
\end{IEEEproof}

Unlike the existing approximations for ZF precoding, which involve only the diagonal entries of $\bOm(\bt)$, \eqref{eq:Re} captures the LoS correlation between Eve and the LUs via $\bv(\bt)$.

\subsection{Approximate ESSR Maximization Problem}\label{sec:discussion}

Based on~\eqref{eq:Rm} and~\eqref{eq:Re}, the ergodic secrecy rate of the $m$-th LU is approximated by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:esrapprox}
R_m^{\mathrm{sec}}\approx\tilde R_m^{\mathrm{sec}}(\bt,p_m,q)\triangleq\big[\tilde R_m(\bt,p_m)-\tilde R_{\mathrm{e},m}(\bt,p_m,q)\big]^+,
\end{equation}
which depends only on the statistical CSI and is thus suitable for the large-timescale design. The following remarks reveal how the antenna positions affect the approximate ESSR.

\begin{remark}[Secrecy Mechanisms of the MAs]\label{rem:mech}
From~\eqref{eq:Rm}, \eqref{eq:cm}, and~\eqref{eq:d}, the antenna positions affect the approximate ESSR via $\omega_m(\bt)$, $c_m(\bt)$, and $d(\bt)$, i.e., via the LoS array correlations $[\Hb^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\Hb(\bt)]_{i,j}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\bt_n^{\mathrm T}(\ba_j-\ba_i)}$ among the LUs and $[\Hb^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\gb(\bt)]_{i}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\bt_n^{\mathrm T}(\ba_{\mathrm e}-\ba_i)}$ between Eve and the LUs. A smaller $\omega_m(\bt)$, i.e., a weaker LoS correlation between the $m$-th LU and the other LUs, yields a larger ZF gain in~\eqref{eq:etabound}. This decorrelation among the LUs is the only mechanism exploited by the rate-oriented design~\cite{ZhengTCOM2025}. In secure transmission, a smaller $c_m(\bt)$ and a larger $d(\bt)$ provide two additional mechanisms, i.e., less information leakage and more AN received by Eve. In particular, if $\bv(\bt)=\mathbf 0$, i.e., the LoS component of Eve is orthogonal to those of all the LUs, then $c_m(\bt)$ reduces to its minimum $N[\bOm^2(\bt)]_{m,m}/\omega_m(\bt)$ caused by the NLoS components, and $d(\bt)$ achieves its maximum $N(N-M)/(N-M+N\tr(\bOm(\bt)))$ for a given $\bPhi(\bt)$. Hence, by decorrelating the LoS component of Eve from those of the LUs, the MAs simultaneously reduce the information leakage and increase the AN received by Eve, whereas an FPA array can hardly achieve this decorrelation when Eve is located in a direction close to that of an LU. Note that if $\kappa_{\mathrm e}=0$, then $\tilde R_{\mathrm{e},m}=\log_2(1+p_m/(q(N-M)+\sbe))$ does not depend on $\bt$, i.e., the statistical CSI of Eve carries no spatial information, and only the first mechanism remains.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Single LU]\label{rem:single}
If $M=1$, then $\bPhi(\bt)=N(\kappa_1+1)$ is a scalar, and $\eta_1=\zeta_1(N-1)(\kappa_1+1)$ does not depend on $\bt$.\footnote{For $M=1$, even the exact ergodic rate $\E\{\log_2(1+p_1\|\bh_1(\bt)\|^2/\sigma_1^2)\}$ does not depend on $\bt$, because the distribution of $\|\bh_1(\bt)\|^2$ depends on $\hb_1(\bt)$ only through $\|\hb_1(\bt)\|^2=N$.} In contrast,
\begin{align}
c_1(\bt)&=\frac{\kappa_1|\hb_1^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\gb(\bt)|^2/N+1}{\kappa_1+1},\label{eq:c1}\\
d(\bt)&=\frac{(N-1)(\kappa_1+1)}{(N-1)(\kappa_1+1)+1}\Big(N+\frac{1}{\kappa_1+1}-c_1(\bt)\Big)\label{eq:d1}
\end{align}
depend on $\bt$ through the LoS correlation $|\hb_1^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\gb(\bt)|^2$ between the LU and Eve. Hence, unlike the rate-oriented two-timescale design, where the MAs bring no gain for a single user~\cite[Remark~2]{ZhengTCOM2025}, the MAs enhance the secrecy of a single LU by reducing this correlation. For $M=1$, $\wb_1\wb_1^{\mathrm H}+\PHp=\bI_N$ further implies $\bar X_1+\bar Y=N$, i.e., reducing the leakage and increasing the AN received by Eve are equivalent. This identity is nearly preserved by~\eqref{eq:c1} and~\eqref{eq:d1}, since $c_1(\bt)+d(\bt)-N=(c_1(\bt)-1)/((N-1)(\kappa_1+1)+1)\in[-1/4,1/4]$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[High-SNR Behavior and AN Saving]\label{rem:highsnr}
Consider the information--AN power splitting with equal power among the LUs, i.e., $p_m=\alpha\Ptot/M$ and $q=(1-\alpha)\Ptot/(N-M)$, where $\alpha\in(0,1)$ is the power-splitting factor. In the high signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) regime, i.e., as $\Ptot\to\infty$, the approximate secrecy rate of the $m$-th LU satisfies
\begin{align}
\tilde R_m-\tilde R_{\mathrm{e},m}&=\log_2\frac{\alpha\Ptot\eta_m(\bt)}{M}\nonumber\\
&\quad-\log_2\Big(1+\frac{\alpha\Theta_m(\bt)}{1-\alpha}\Big)+o(1),\label{eq:highsnr}
\end{align}
where $o(1)$ vanishes as $\Ptot\to\infty$, and $\Theta_m(\bt)\triangleq\frac{(N-M)\xi_m(\bt)}{M\psi(\bt)}$ is the ratio of the normalized information leakage to the normalized AN power received by Eve. Hence, the high-SNR slope of the approximate ESSR equals $M$ regardless of $\bt$, and the antenna positions affect only the constant term. Moreover, the value of $\alpha$ that maximizes the sum of the constant terms in~\eqref{eq:highsnr} satisfies $\sum_{m=1}^M\big[\frac1\alpha-\frac1{1-\alpha}-\frac{\Theta_m-1}{1+\alpha(\Theta_m-1)}\big]=0$, whose solution for $\Theta_m=\Theta$, $\forall m\in\mathcal M$, is given by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:alphastar}
\alpha^\star=\frac{1}{1+\sqrt{\Theta}}.
\end{equation}
Since the MAs can decrease $c_m(\bt)$ and increase $d(\bt)$, they can decrease $\Theta$ and thus increase $\alpha^\star$, i.e., reduce the required AN power. We refer to this effect as the AN-saving effect.
\end{remark}

Replacing the ergodic secrecy rates in (P2) with~\eqref{eq:esrapprox} yields the following deterministic approximate problem:
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:P3}
\begin{align}
(\mathrm{P3}):\ \max_{\bt,\bp,q}\ \ & F(\bt,\bp,q)\triangleq\sum_{m=1}^{M}\bigg[\log_2\big(1+\eta_m(\bt)p_m\big)\nonumber\\
&\qquad\qquad-\log_2\bigg(1+\frac{\xi_m(\bt)p_m}{q\psi(\bt)+\sbe}\bigg)\bigg]\label{eq:P3obj}\\
\mathrm{s.t.}\ \ & \eqref{eq:P2pow}\text{--}\eqref{eq:P2dist},\nonumber
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
where the operator $[\cdot]^+$ in~\eqref{eq:esrapprox} is dropped without loss of optimality. Specifically, for any feasible $(\bt,\bp,q)$, setting $p_m=0$ for every LU whose term in~\eqref{eq:P3obj} is negative yields another feasible solution, whose objective value equals the approximate ESSR $\sum_{m=1}^M\tilde R_m^{\mathrm{sec}}(\bt,p_m,q)$ of the original solution, since the $m$-th term depends only on $p_m$ and $q$ for a given $\bt$. Hence, (P3) has the same optimal value as its counterpart with $[\cdot]^+$, every term in~\eqref{eq:P3obj} is nonnegative at any optimal solution of (P3), and $F(\bt,\bp,q)\le\sum_{m=1}^M\tilde R_m^{\mathrm{sec}}(\bt,p_m,q)$ holds for any feasible solution. In contrast, under equal power allocation among the LUs, the operator $[\cdot]^+$ must be retained.

\section{Proposed Two-Timescale Optimization Algorithm}\label{sec:opt}
% =============================================================================
Problem (P3) is still nonconvex, since $F$ is nonconcave with respect to $\bt$ and the minimum-distance constraints in~\eqref{eq:P2dist} are nonconvex. Moreover, $\bt$, $\bp$, and $q$ are coupled in the objective function, and $\bp$ and $q$ are further coupled by the power constraint~\eqref{eq:P2pow}. Nevertheless, (P3) has two useful structures. First, for given $\bt$, the coefficients $\eta_m$, $\xi_m$, and $\psi$ in~\eqref{eq:P3obj} are constants, and the optimal power allocation among the LUs for a given $q$ can be derived in semi-closed form. Second, for given $\bp$ and $q$, each antenna position affects $F$ only through a rank-one update of $\bPhi(\bt)$ and $\bv(\bt)$, which renders a grid search for each position affordable. Based on these structures, we alternately optimize the power allocation and the antenna positions in Sections~\ref{sec:power} and~\ref{sec:position}, respectively.

\subsection{Power Allocation Optimization for Given $\bt$}\label{sec:power}

For given $\bt$, (P3) reduces to
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:P31}
\begin{align}
(\mathrm{P3.1}):\ \max_{\bp,q}\ \ & \sum_{m=1}^{M}f_m(p_m;q)\\
\mathrm{s.t.}\ \ & \eqref{eq:P2pow},\ \eqref{eq:P2pos},\nonumber
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fm}
f_m(p;q)\triangleq\log_2(1+\eta_mp)-\log_2\big(1+\eta_{\mathrm{e},m}(q)\,p\big),
\end{equation}
and $\eta_{\mathrm{e},m}(q)\triangleq\xi_m/(q\psi+\sbe)$, which decreases with $q$, is the effective channel gain of Eve for $s_m$. For brevity, the argument $\bt$ is omitted in this subsection. Problem (P3.1) is nonconvex in general, because $f_m$ is not jointly concave in $p_m$ and $q$. Nevertheless, for a given $q$, its optimal $\bp$ can be obtained in semi-closed form, as shown below. The first-order and second-order derivatives of $f_m$ with respect to $p$ are given by
\begin{align}
\frac{\partial f_m}{\partial p}&=\frac{\eta_m-\eta_{\mathrm{e},m}}{\ln2\,(1+\eta_mp)(1+\eta_{\mathrm{e},m}p)},\label{eq:fm1}\\
\frac{\partial^2 f_m}{\partial p^2}&=\frac{1}{\ln2}\bigg[\frac{\eta_{\mathrm{e},m}^2}{(1+\eta_{\mathrm{e},m}p)^2}-\frac{\eta_m^2}{(1+\eta_mp)^2}\bigg],\label{eq:fm2}
\end{align}
where the argument $q$ of $\eta_{\mathrm{e},m}$ is omitted. If $\eta_m\le\eta_{\mathrm{e},m}$, then $f_m(p;q)\le0$ for all $p\ge0$, and thus $p_m=0$ is optimal. If $\eta_m>\eta_{\mathrm{e},m}$, then $\eta_m(1+\eta_{\mathrm{e},m}p)-\eta_{\mathrm{e},m}(1+\eta_mp)=\eta_m-\eta_{\mathrm{e},m}>0$, i.e., $\frac{\eta_m}{1+\eta_mp}>\frac{\eta_{\mathrm{e},m}}{1+\eta_{\mathrm{e},m}p}$. Hence, by~\eqref{eq:fm1} and~\eqref{eq:fm2}, $f_m$ is strictly increasing and strictly concave in $p\ge0$. Let $\mathcal M_{\mathrm a}(q)\triangleq\{m\in\mathcal M\,|\,\eta_m>\eta_{\mathrm{e},m}(q)\}$ denote the set of LUs that can achieve positive secrecy rates, and $P_{\mathrm s}(q)\triangleq\Ptot-(N-M)q$ the power available for the confidential signals. Then, the optimal $\bp$ for a given $q$ is given as follows.

\begin{proposition}[Secrecy Water-Filling]\label{prop:wf}
For a given $q$ with $P_{\mathrm s}(q)>0$ and $\mathcal M_{\mathrm a}(q)\neq\emptyset$, the optimal power allocation is $p_m^\star=0$ for $m\notin\mathcal M_{\mathrm a}(q)$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wf}
p_m^\star(\nu)=\bigg[\frac{2(\rho_m-1)}{\sqrt{(\eta_m-\eta_{\mathrm{e},m})^2+4\eta_m\eta_{\mathrm{e},m}\rho_m}+\eta_m+\eta_{\mathrm{e},m}}\bigg]^+
\end{equation}
for $m\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm a}(q)$, where $\rho_m=\rho_m(\nu)\triangleq(\eta_m-\eta_{\mathrm{e},m})/(\nu\ln2)$, and the water level $\nu>0$ is the unique solution of $\sum_{m\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm a}(q)}p_m^\star(\nu)=P_{\mathrm s}(q)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{IEEEproof}
Since the objective is strictly concave and increasing in $\{p_m\}_{m\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm a}(q)}$ and Slater's condition holds, the Karush--Kuhn--Tucker (KKT) conditions are necessary and sufficient for optimality, and the power constraint is active at the optimum~\cite{Boyd2004}. Let $\nu>0$ denote the dual variable associated with the power constraint. The KKT conditions yield $\partial f_m/\partial p=\nu$ if $p_m^\star>0$ and $\partial f_m/\partial p|_{p=0}\le\nu$ otherwise. From~\eqref{eq:fm1}, the former is equivalent to $(1+\eta_mp)(1+\eta_{\mathrm{e},m}p)=\rho_m(\nu)$, i.e., a quadratic equation in $p$, whose positive root, written in a numerically stable form, is given by~\eqref{eq:wf}. The latter is equivalent to $\rho_m(\nu)\le1$, in which case~\eqref{eq:wf} gives $p_m^\star=0$. Finally, since $\sum_{m\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm a}(q)}p_m^\star(\nu)$ is continuous and strictly decreasing in $\nu$ over $(0,\max_{m\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm a}(q)}(\eta_m-\eta_{\mathrm{e},m})/\ln2)$, ranging from $+\infty$ to $0$, the water level is unique.
\end{IEEEproof}

Since $p_m^\star(\nu)$ is nonincreasing in $\nu$, the water level in Proposition~\ref{prop:wf} can be found by bisection.

\begin{remark}[Relation to Classic Water-Filling]\label{rem:wf}
As $\eta_{\mathrm{e},m}\to0$, \eqref{eq:wf} reduces to the classic water-filling solution $[1/(\nu\ln2)-1/\eta_m]^+$. Hence, \eqref{eq:wf} can be regarded as a secrecy water-filling solution, in which the LUs whose effective channel gains do not exceed that of Eve are switched off.
\end{remark}

Next, let $F^\star(q)\triangleq\sum_{m\in\mathcal M}f_m(p_m^\star;q)$ denote the optimal value of (P3.1) for a given $q$, where $F^\star(q)=0$ if $\mathcal M_{\mathrm a}(q)=\emptyset$ or $P_{\mathrm s}(q)=0$. The AN power per dimension is then optimized as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:qsearch}
q^\star=\arg\max_{0\le q\le q_{\max}}\ F^\star(q),
\end{equation}
where $q_{\max}\triangleq\Ptot/(N-M)$. Increasing $q$ reduces the effective channel gains of Eve at the cost of less power for the confidential signals. Since $F^\star(q)$ is not necessarily unimodal, \eqref{eq:qsearch} is tackled by a coarse grid search followed by a finer one around the best grid point (Algorithm~\ref{alg:power}).

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Power Allocation for Given $\bt$}\label{alg:power}
\small\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $\{\eta_m,\xi_m\}_{m\in\mathcal M}$, $\psi$, $\sbe$, $\Ptot$, and the grid size $Q$.
\Ensure $q^\star$ and $\bp^\star$.
\For{each $q$ in a uniform grid of $Q$ points over $[0,q_{\max}]$}
  \State Obtain $\bp^\star$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:wf}, where $\nu$ is found by bisection.
  \State Evaluate $F^\star(q)$.
\EndFor
\State Repeat Steps 1--4 over a uniform grid of $Q$ points between the two neighbors of the best grid point.
\State \Return the best $q$ as $q^\star$ and its optimal power allocation as $\bp^\star$.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Antenna Position Optimization for Given $\bp$ and $q$}\label{sec:position}

For given $\bp$ and $q$, (P3) reduces to
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:P32}
\begin{align}
(\mathrm{P3.2}):\ \max_{\bt}\ \ & F(\bt)\\
\mathrm{s.t.}\ \ & \bt_n\in\mathcal C,\ \forall n\in\mathcal N,\\
& \|\bt_n-\bt_i\|\ge\Dmin,\ \forall n,i\in\mathcal N,\ n\neq i,
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
where $F(\bt)\triangleq F(\bt,\bp,q)$. Problem (P3.2) is challenging for two reasons. First, according to Remark~\ref{rem:mech}, $F(\bt)$ depends on $\bt_n$ through the phases $\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\bt_n^{\mathrm T}(\ba_j-\ba_i)$, $i,j\in\mathcal M\cup\{\mathrm e\}$, which rotate by $2\pi$ when $\bt_n$ moves by $\lambda/\|\ba_j-\ba_i\|$ along $\ba_j-\ba_i$. Hence, $F(\bt)$ is highly oscillatory and has numerous local maxima within $\mathcal C$. Second, the minimum-distance constraints are nonconvex. To reduce the problem dimension, we optimize the antenna positions in an element-wise manner similar to~\cite{ZhengTCOM2025}, i.e., we update $\bt_n$ with the other positions fixed, for $n=1,\ldots,N$ in turn. Let $F(\bt_n)$ denote $F$ as a function of $\bt_n$, with the other positions fixed at their latest values. The subproblem for the $n$-th MA is given by
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:P32n0}
\begin{align}
(\mathrm{P3.2}.n):\ \max_{\bt_n}\ \ & F(\bt_n)\\
\mathrm{s.t.}\ \ & \bt_n\in\mathcal C,\\
& \|\bt_n-\bt_i\|\ge\Dmin,\ \forall i\in\mathcal N\setminus\{n\}.
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
Although (P3.2.$n$) has only two real variables, $F(\bt_n)$ is multimodal. Therefore, a purely local method, e.g., the MM method adopted in~\cite{ZhengTCOM2025}, typically converges to a local maximum near the current position of the MA, so that the MAs move slowly across $\mathcal C$ and the AO iterations may stagnate at an inferior solution (see Section~\ref{sec:sim_conv}). To overcome this issue, we tackle (P3.2.$n$) by a coarse-to-fine approach: in Stage~1, a search over the feasible grid points selects the best candidate position; in Stage~2, the MM method refines this position. The grid search is affordable due to the following rank-one structure.

\textit{1) Rank-one structure:} Let $\bl_n(\bt_n)\in\mathbb C^{M\times1}$ denote the conjugate transpose of the $n$-th row of $\bL(\bt)$, i.e.,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ln}
\bl_n(\bt_n)=\bK^{1/2}\Big[e^{-j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\bt_n^{\mathrm T}\ba_1},\ldots,e^{-j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\bt_n^{\mathrm T}\ba_M}\Big]^{\mathrm T}.
\end{equation}
Since $\bL^{\mathrm H}\bL=\sum_{i=1}^N\bl_i\bl_i^{\mathrm H}$ and $\bL^{\mathrm H}\gb=\sum_{i=1}^N\bl_ie^{j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\bt_i^{\mathrm T}\ba_{\mathrm e}}$, we have
\begin{align}
\bPhi(\bt)&=\bGam_n+\bl_n(\bt_n)\bl_n^{\mathrm H}(\bt_n),\label{eq:Phin}\\
\bv(\bt)&=\br_n+\bl_n(\bt_n)e^{j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\bt_n^{\mathrm T}\ba_{\mathrm e}},\label{eq:vn}
\end{align}
where $\bGam_n\triangleq\sum_{i\neq n}\bl_i(\bt_i)\bl_i^{\mathrm H}(\bt_i)+N\bI_M$ and $\br_n\triangleq\sum_{i\neq n}\bl_i(\bt_i)e^{j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\bt_i^{\mathrm T}\ba_{\mathrm e}}$ do not depend on $\bt_n$. By the Sherman--Morrison formula~\cite{Horn2013},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sm}
\bOm(\bt)=\bGam_n^{-1}-\frac{\bGam_n^{-1}\bl_n(\bt_n)\bl_n^{\mathrm H}(\bt_n)\bGam_n^{-1}}{1+\bl_n^{\mathrm H}(\bt_n)\bGam_n^{-1}\bl_n(\bt_n)}.
\end{equation}
Hence, given $\bGam_n^{-1}$ and $\br_n$, $\omega_m$, $c_m$, $d$, and thus $F(\bt_n)$ can be evaluated at any position with only $\mathcal O(M^2)$ operations.

\textit{2) Stage 1 (grid search):} Let $\mathcal G\subset\mathcal C$ denote a uniform grid over $\mathcal C$, $\bt_n^{\mathrm{cur}}$ the current position of the $n$-th MA, and $\mathcal G_n\triangleq\{\bt_n\in\mathcal G\,|\,\|\bt_n-\bt_i\|\ge\Dmin,\ \forall i\neq n\}$ the set of grid points that are feasible for (P3.2.$n$). Based on~\eqref{eq:sm}, we evaluate $F(\bt_n)$ at all the candidates in $\mathcal G_n\cup\{\bt_n^{\mathrm{cur}}\}$ and obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gridsearch}
\hat\bt_n=\arg\max_{\bt_n\in\mathcal G_n\cup\{\bt_n^{\mathrm{cur}}\}}F(\bt_n),
\end{equation}
which requires $\mathcal O(|\mathcal G|M^2)$ operations. Since the current position is included in the candidates, $F(\hat\bt_n)\ge F(\bt_n^{\mathrm{cur}})$. The grid spacing is chosen as a fraction of the shortest period $\lambda/\max_{i,j\in\mathcal M\cup\{\mathrm e\}}\|\ba_j-\ba_i\|$ of the above phases, which is at least $\lambda/2$ due to $\|\ba_i\|\le1$. Since $F(\bt_n)$ is a rational function of these phase terms, the sufficiency of the grid spacing is verified numerically in Section~\ref{sec:sim_setup}.

\textit{3) Stage 2 (MM refinement):} Starting from $\bt_n^{(0)}=\hat\bt_n$, we refine the position by the MM method~\cite{Sun2017}, where $\bt_n^{(k)}$ denotes the position of the $n$-th MA in the $k$-th MM iteration. To construct a surrogate function, we derive the gradient of $F(\bt_n)$. Let $u_n\in\{x_n,y_n\}$ denote the coordinate of $\bt_n$ along the axis $u\in\{x,y\}$, and define $\bLam_u\triangleq\diag(a_{1,u},\ldots,a_{M,u})$, where $a_{m,x}\triangleq\cos\theta_m\sin\phi_m$ and $a_{m,y}\triangleq\sin\theta_m$ are the entries of $\ba_m$, and $a_{\mathrm{e},u}$ is the corresponding entry of $\ba_{\mathrm e}$. From~\eqref{eq:ln}--\eqref{eq:vn}, the derivatives of $\bl_n$, $\bv$, and $\bOm$ with respect to $u_n$ are given by
\begin{align}
\dot{\bl}_n&\triangleq\frac{\partial\bl_n}{\partial u_n}=-j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\bLam_u\bl_n,\label{eq:dl}\\
\dot{\bv}&\triangleq\frac{\partial\bv}{\partial u_n}=j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\big(a_{\mathrm{e},u}\bI_M-\bLam_u\big)\bl_ne^{j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\bt_n^{\mathrm T}\ba_{\mathrm e}},\label{eq:dv}\\
\dot{\bOm}&\triangleq\frac{\partial\bOm}{\partial u_n}=-\bOm\big(\dot{\bl}_n\bl_n^{\mathrm H}+\bl_n\dot{\bl}_n^{\mathrm H}\big)\bOm,\label{eq:dOm}
\end{align}
where~\eqref{eq:dOm} follows from $\partial\bPhi^{-1}=-\bPhi^{-1}(\partial\bPhi)\bPhi^{-1}$. Based on~\eqref{eq:dl}--\eqref{eq:dOm}, the derivatives $\dot\omega_m\triangleq\partial\omega_m/\partial u_n$, $\dot c_m\triangleq\partial c_m/\partial u_n$, and $\dot d\triangleq\partial d/\partial u_n$ are derived in closed form in Appendix~\ref{app:grad}. Then, the derivatives of the effective channel gains $\eta_m$ and $\eta_{\mathrm{e},m}=\xi_m/(q\psi+\sbe)$ are given by $\dot\eta_m\triangleq\partial\eta_m/\partial u_n=-\eta_m\dot\omega_m/\omega_m$ and $\dot\eta_{\mathrm{e},m}\triangleq\partial\eta_{\mathrm{e},m}/\partial u_n=\kappa_{\mathrm e}\eta_{\mathrm{e},m}\big(\dot c_m/\xi_m-q\dot d/(q\psi+\sbe)\big)$, respectively. Since $F(\bt_n)=\sum_{m=1}^M\big[\log_2(1+\eta_mp_m)-\log_2(1+\eta_{\mathrm{e},m}p_m)\big]$, the entries of the gradient $\nabla_{\bt_n}F=[\partial F/\partial x_n,\ \partial F/\partial y_n]^{\mathrm T}$ are given by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dF}
\frac{\partial F}{\partial u_n}=\frac{1}{\ln2}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\bigg[\frac{p_m\dot\eta_m}{1+\eta_mp_m}-\frac{p_m\dot\eta_{\mathrm{e},m}}{1+\eta_{\mathrm{e},m}p_m}\bigg].
\end{equation}

Since $F(\bt_n)$ is twice continuously differentiable and $\mathcal C$ is compact, $\nabla_{\bt_n}F$ is Lipschitz continuous over $\mathcal C$. According to the descent lemma~\cite[Lemma~5.7]{Beck2017}, for any $\delta_n$ no smaller than its Lipschitz constant, the following quadratic function, which is tight at $\bt_n^{(k)}$, is a lower bound of $F(\bt_n)$:
\begin{align}
F(\bt_n)\ge\hat F_n(\bt_n|\bt_n^{(k)})\triangleq\ &F(\bt_n^{(k)})+\nabla_{\bt_n}F(\bt_n^{(k)})^{\mathrm T}\big(\bt_n-\bt_n^{(k)}\big)\nonumber\\
&-\frac{\delta_n}{2}\big\|\bt_n-\bt_n^{(k)}\big\|^2.\label{eq:surrogate}
\end{align}
Since the Lipschitz constant is difficult to obtain, $\delta_n$ is determined by backtracking~\cite{Beck2017}, i.e., starting from $\max\{\delta_0,\delta_n^{\mathrm{pre}}/2\}$, where $\delta_0>0$ is a constant and $\delta_n^{\mathrm{pre}}$ is the previously accepted value, $\delta_n$ is doubled until the update below satisfies $F(\bt_n^{(k+1)})\ge\hat F_n(\bt_n^{(k+1)}|\bt_n^{(k)})$, which holds once $\delta_n$ reaches the Lipschitz constant.

To handle the minimum-distance constraints, we note that $\|\bt_n-\bt_i\|^2$ is convex with respect to $\bt_n$ and is thus lower-bounded by its first-order Taylor expansion at $\bt_n^{(k)}$~\cite{ZhengTCOM2025}, i.e.,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:distlin}
\|\bt_n-\bt_i\|^2\ge\big(\bt_n^{(k)}-\bt_i\big)^{\mathrm T}\big(2\bt_n-\bt_n^{(k)}-\bt_i\big).
\end{equation}
Replacing $\|\bt_n-\bt_i\|^2$ in the minimum-distance constraints with the right-hand side of~\eqref{eq:distlin} yields the linear constraints
\begin{equation}\label{eq:linc}
\big(\bt_n^{(k)}-\bt_i\big)^{\mathrm T}\big(2\bt_n-\bt_n^{(k)}-\bt_i\big)\ge\Dmin^2,\quad\forall i\neq n,
\end{equation}
whose feasible set is a convex subset of that of the minimum-distance constraints with respect to $\bt_n$. Based on~\eqref{eq:surrogate} and~\eqref{eq:linc}, $\bt_n$ is updated by solving
\begin{equation}\label{eq:P32n}
\bt_n^{(k+1)}=\arg\max_{\bt_n}\ \hat F_n(\bt_n|\bt_n^{(k)})\quad\mathrm{s.t.}\ \bt_n\in\mathcal C,\ \eqref{eq:linc}.
\end{equation}
Completing the square shows that~\eqref{eq:P32n} is equivalent to projecting $\bt_n^{(k)}+\nabla_{\bt_n}F(\bt_n^{(k)})/\delta_n$ onto the convex polygon defined by $\mathcal C$ and~\eqref{eq:linc}. The solution is this point itself if it is feasible, and otherwise lies on an edge or at a vertex of the polygon. Hence, enumerating the projections onto the $N+3$ constraint lines and their pairwise intersections yields the exact solution. Since $\bt_n^{(k)}$ is feasible for~\eqref{eq:P32n}, the update satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mono}
F(\bt_n^{(k+1)})\ge\hat F_n(\bt_n^{(k+1)}|\bt_n^{(k)})\ge\hat F_n(\bt_n^{(k)}|\bt_n^{(k)})=F(\bt_n^{(k)}),
\end{equation}
and $\bt_n^{(k+1)}$ is feasible for (P3.2.$n$) due to~\eqref{eq:distlin}. The MM iterations are repeated until the increase of $F$ normalized by $\max\{1,|F|\}$ is below $\epsilon_{\mathrm{in}}$ or $\NMM$ iterations are reached.

\subsection{Overall Algorithm, Convergence, and Complexity}\label{sec:overall}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Two-Timescale AO Algorithm for (P3)}\label{alg:ao}
\small\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Statistical CSI, $\Ptot$, $\mathcal C$, $\Dmin$, $Q$, $\mathcal G$, $\delta_0$, $\epsilon_{\mathrm{in}}$, $\NMM$, $\epsilon$, $\NAO$.
\Ensure $\bt^\star$, $\bp^\star$, and $q^\star$.
\State Initialize feasible positions $\bt$ and obtain $(\bp,q)$ by Algorithm~\ref{alg:power}.
\Repeat
  \For{$n=1$ to $N$}
    \State \textit{Stage 1:} Compute $\bGam_n^{-1}$ and $\br_n$, and obtain $\hat\bt_n$ by~\eqref{eq:gridsearch}.
    \State \textit{Stage 2:} Starting from $\bt_n^{(0)}=\hat\bt_n$, update $\bt_n$ by~\eqref{eq:P32n} with backtracking on $\delta_n$ until the increase of $F$ normalized by $\max\{1,|F|\}$ is below $\epsilon_{\mathrm{in}}$ or $\NMM$ iterations are reached.
  \EndFor
  \State Obtain $(\bp',q')$ by Algorithm~\ref{alg:power} for the updated $\bt$, and set $(\bp,q)\leftarrow(\bp',q')$ if $F(\bt,\bp',q')\ge F(\bt,\bp,q)$.
\Until{the increase of $F$ normalized by $\max\{1,|F|\}$ is below $\epsilon$ or $\NAO$ iterations are reached}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Based on Sections~\ref{sec:power} and~\ref{sec:position}, the overall AO algorithm for (P3) is summarized in Algorithm~\ref{alg:ao}, whose convergence, initialization, and complexity are discussed below.

\textit{Convergence:} In each iteration of Algorithm~\ref{alg:ao}, the grid search in~\eqref{eq:gridsearch} does not decrease the objective value of (P3), since the current position is included in the candidates, and neither do the MM updates in~\eqref{eq:P32n} due to~\eqref{eq:mono}. Since the power allocation is also updated only if the objective value does not decrease, the objective value is nondecreasing over the iterations. Moreover, it is upper-bounded by $\sum_{m=1}^M\log_2(1+\zeta_m(N-M)(\kappa_m+1)\Ptot)$ according to~\eqref{eq:etabound} and $p_m\le\Ptot$. Therefore, the objective value achieved by Algorithm~\ref{alg:ao} is guaranteed to converge. Note that this result concerns the objective value, rather than the convergence of the iterates to a stationary point or a globally optimal solution of (P3).

\textit{Initialization:} Since (P3) is nonconvex, the solution obtained by Algorithm~\ref{alg:ao} may depend on the initial positions. This dependence is weaker than for purely local updates, since the grid search in Stage~1 allows each MA to move to any feasible grid point in $\mathcal C$ in every iteration. To further improve the solution, Algorithm~\ref{alg:ao} can be executed from several initializations, e.g., the positions of the FPA array and random feasible positions, and the best solution is selected.

\textit{Complexity:} In each AO iteration, forming $\bPhi$ and $\bOm$ requires $\mathcal O(NM^2+M^3)$ operations. For each MA, $\bGam_n^{-1}$ is obtained from $\bOm$ by a rank-one update. Stage~1 requires $\mathcal O(|\mathcal G|(M^2+N))$ operations, including the feasibility check of the grid points. In Stage~2, the gradient in~\eqref{eq:dF} and each evaluation of $F$ require $\mathcal O(M^2)$ operations based on~\eqref{eq:sm}, and solving~\eqref{eq:P32n} by enumeration requires $\mathcal O(N^3)$ operations. Algorithm~\ref{alg:power} requires $\mathcal O(QN_{\mathrm b}M)$ operations, where $N_{\mathrm b}$ is the number of bisection iterations. Hence, the complexity of Algorithm~\ref{alg:ao} per initialization is $\mathcal O\big(\NAO\big(NM^2+M^3+N|\mathcal G|(M^2+N)+N\NMM N_{\mathrm{bt}}(M^2+N^3)+QN_{\mathrm b}M\big)\big)$, where $N_{\mathrm{bt}}$ is the average number of backtracking trials. In contrast to stochastic optimization~\cite{ZhaoTWC2021}, this complexity does not depend on the number of channel realizations. Since Algorithm~\ref{alg:ao} depends only on the slowly varying statistical CSI, it is executed only once per frame, whereas the ZF precoding and AN generation in each CI require $\mathcal O(NM^2+M^3)$ operations.

\subsection{Simulation Setup and Benchmarks}\label{sec:sim_setup}

Unless otherwise specified, we set $N=8$, $M=3$, $A=4\lambda$, $\Dmin=\lambda/2$, $\kappa_m=\kappa_{\mathrm e}=10$~dB, $\beta_m=\beta_{\mathrm e}=1$, and $\sigma_m^2=\sigma_{\mathrm e}^2=\sigma^2$, such that $\Ptot/\sigma^2$ is the transmit SNR, which is $20$~dB by default. The elevation and azimuth AoDs of the LUs are independently and uniformly distributed within $[-\pi/3,\pi/3]$. Similar to~\cite{ChenArxiv2026}, Eve is located in a direction close to that of LU~$1$ to model the challenging case in Section~\ref{sec:intro}. Specifically, its AoDs are $\theta_{\mathrm e}=\theta_1+\Delta\cos\tilde\phi$ and $\phi_{\mathrm e}=\phi_1+\Delta\sin\tilde\phi$, i.e., they deviate from those of LU~$1$ by the AoD offset $\Delta$ in the direction specified by $\tilde\phi$, which is uniformly distributed over $[0,2\pi)$. Note that $\Delta$ is an offset in the elevation--azimuth coordinates rather than the physical angle between the two directions. By default, $\Delta=0.1$~rad, for which the normalized LoS correlation $|\hb_1^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\gb(\bt)|/N$ between Eve and LU~$1$ exceeds $0.94$ for the FPA, i.e., Eve lies well within the main lobe of the beam steered toward LU~$1$. The case where the AoDs of Eve are independent of those of the LUs is considered in Section~\ref{sec:sim_rob}.

For Algorithm~\ref{alg:ao}, the grid $\mathcal G$ in Stage~1 has the spacing $\lambda/10$, i.e., at most one fifth of the shortest phase period in Section~\ref{sec:position}, which yields $|\mathcal G|=1681$ for $A=4\lambda$. Halving this spacing changes the average ESSR of the proposed design by only $0.01\%$ over $20$ AoD realizations. The remaining parameters are $\delta_0=1$, $\epsilon_{\mathrm{in}}=10^{-6}$, $\NMM=20$, $\epsilon=10^{-4}$, $\NAO=50$, $Q=41$, and $N_{\mathrm b}=60$. Algorithm~\ref{alg:ao} is executed from a $2\times(N/2)$ uniform planar array (UPA) with $\lambda/2$ spacing and two sets of random feasible positions, and the best solution is selected. All the results are averaged over $100$ random realizations of the AoDs shared by all the schemes, i.e., the comparisons are paired, and the standard error of the average ESSR of the proposed design at $20$~dB is $0.55\%$ of its value. Since the closed-form approximations are used only for optimization, the ergodic rates of each design are evaluated with the exact SINRs in~\eqref{eq:snr} and~\eqref{eq:sinre} over $5\times10^3$ channel realizations ($10^4$ in Fig.~\ref{fig:acc}).

We compare the proposed design with four benchmarks: 1) \textbf{Rate-oriented MA}: The antenna positions maximize the approximate ergodic sum rate of the LUs with equal power, i.e., $\sum_{m=1}^M\log_2(1+\eta_m(\bt)\Ptot/M)$, which ignores Eve~\cite{ZhengTCOM2025}. For a fair comparison, this problem is tackled by the position update in Section~\ref{sec:position} with the same initializations. This benchmark reveals the gain of the secrecy-aware positioning enabled by~\eqref{eq:Re}. 2) \textbf{AS}: Similar to~\cite{ChenArxiv2026}, the BS selects $N$ of $2N$ candidate antennas, which form a UPA with $4$ rows, $N/2$ columns, and $\lambda/2$ spacing, by an exhaustive search over all the $\binom{2N}{N}$ subsets, e.g., $12870$ for $N=8$, where the subsets are ranked by the approximate ESSR in (P3) with the AN power searched over $21$ grid points, and the power allocation of the selected subset is refined by Algorithm~\ref{alg:power}. AS requires no antenna movement but twice as many antennas and an RF switching network. 3) \textbf{Sparse FPA}: A $2\times(N/2)$ UPA spans $\mathcal C$ with spacings $2A/(N-2)$ and $A$ along the $x$- and $y$-axes, respectively. This benchmark has the same aperture as the MAs and thus separates the gain of the optimized positions from that of the large aperture. 4) \textbf{FPA}: The BS adopts the $2\times(N/2)$ UPA with $\lambda/2$ spacing. All the schemes adopt the power allocation in Algorithm~\ref{alg:power}, so that their performance differences are caused only by the antenna positions.

Fig.~\ref{fig:acc} evaluates the accuracy of~\eqref{eq:Rm} and~\eqref{eq:Re} at the designs obtained by Algorithm~\ref{alg:ao}. The approximate wiretap rate in~\eqref{eq:Re} closely matches the Monte Carlo results, with maximum absolute errors of $0.052$ and $0.013$~bps/Hz over the SNRs in Fig.~\ref{fig:acc}(a) and the Rician factors in Fig.~\ref{fig:acc}(b), respectively, indicating that it accurately captures the LoS correlation between Eve and the LUs. Nevertheless, this gap is similar for all the schemes, i.e., between $1.52$ and $1.76$~bps/Hz at $20$~dB, and smaller than the gains of the proposed design, so that the approximation remains a suitable design objective.

\subsection{ESSR and AN Power Versus Transmit SNR}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\subfloat[]{\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{fig5a_essr_snr}\label{fig:snr_essr}}\hfil
\subfloat[]{\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{fig5b_an_fraction}\label{fig:snr_an}}
\caption{Performance versus the transmit SNR $\Ptot/\sigma^2$: (a) ESSR; (b) optimized AN power fraction $(N-M)q/\Ptot$.}
\label{fig:snr}

Fig.~\ref{fig:snr}(a) shows that the proposed design achieves the highest ESSR over the entire SNR range, e.g., $14.8\%$ and $31.2\%$ higher than those of the rate-oriented MA and the FPA at $20$~dB, respectively. Although the rate-oriented MA exploits the same movable region, its gain over the FPA is less than half of that of the proposed design. The reason is that the rate-oriented positions only decorrelate the LUs, whereas the proposed positions additionally reduce the information leakage and increase the AN received by Eve (see Remark~\ref{rem:mech}). AS achieves an even smaller gain, because its $2N$ candidates form a compact array, whose spatial resolution is insufficient to decorrelate the LoS components of Eve and LU~$1$. The sparse FPA has the same aperture as the MAs but also achieves less than half of the gain of the proposed design over the FPA, because its fixed positions cannot adapt the LoS correlations to the AoDs of the LUs and Eve. Hence, the secrecy gain of the MAs results mainly from the optimized positions rather than from the large aperture. Moreover, the absolute ESSR gains of the proposed design approach constants at high SNR, since the antenna positions improve the constant term rather than the slope of the ESSR (see Remark~\ref{rem:highsnr}). In contrast, without AN, i.e., with $q=0$ in Algorithm~\ref{alg:ao}, the ESSR of the proposed design saturates at high SNR, because the SINRs of the LUs and Eve then grow at the same rate with the transmit power~\cite{Goel2008}.

Fig.~\ref{fig:snr}(b) shows that the optimized AN power fraction increases with the SNR and saturates, as predicted by~\eqref{eq:alphastar}. The proposed design requires much less AN power than all the benchmarks, e.g., an AN power fraction of $0.27$ versus $0.47$ for the FPA at $20$~dB, whereas the AN power fractions of the other benchmarks are comparable to that of the FPA. This result verifies the AN-saving effect in Remark~\ref{rem:highsnr}: since the proposed positions reduce the information leakage and increase the AN received by Eve, less AN suffices, leaving more power for the confidential signals.

Moreover, the sparse FPA, whose grating lobes are strong for small $N$, outperforms the rate-oriented MA for $N\ge14$, because more antennas in $\mathcal C$ suppress them.

Fig.~\ref{fig:sep_pa}(a) shows the ESSR versus the AoD offset $\Delta$ for $M=3$ and $M=1$. For $M=3$, all the schemes benefit from a larger $\Delta$, because Eve becomes more separable from LU~$1$. \subsection{Benefit of the Long-Term Power Allocation}\label{sec:sim_pa}

Fig.~\ref{fig:sep_pa}(b) evaluates the benefit of the proposed long-term power allocation, i.e., the secrecy water-filling in Proposition~\ref{prop:wf} combined with the optimized AN power. It is compared with two equal-power (EP) benchmarks with $p_m=\alpha\Ptot/M$ and $q=(1-\alpha)\Ptot/(N-M)$, where $\alpha$ is either optimized by a one-dimensional search~\cite{Zhou2010} or fixed to $0.5$~\cite{ChenArxiv2026}. For all the schemes, the antenna positions are optimized by Algorithm~\ref{alg:ao}, where the objective of the EP benchmarks retains $[\cdot]^+$ (see Section~\ref{sec:discussion}), and its gradient in Stage~2 is computed over the LUs with positive terms, whose sum minorizes the objective. The large-scale fading of the LUs is $[\beta_1,\beta_2,\beta_3]=[0,-S/2,-S]$~dB, where $S$ is the fading spread.

EP with optimized $\alpha$ performs comparably to the proposed power allocation for small fading spreads, because the LUs then have similar effective channel gains, which are further balanced by the optimized positions. At $0$~dB and $S\le5$~dB, it is even slightly better, since the proposed allocation is optimal for the approximate objective rather than for the actual ESSR. In contrast, the gain of the proposed power allocation increases with $S$, since it switches off or reduces the power of the LUs whose effective channel gains are comparable to that of Eve (Proposition~\ref{prop:wf}). This gain decreases with the SNR, e.g., from $40\%$ at $0$~dB to $14\%$ at $10$~dB and to below $1\%$ at $20$~dB for $S=20$~dB, where the $20$-dB values are not shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:sep_pa}(b). The reason is that, at high SNR, all the LUs achieve positive secrecy rates and nearly equal powers become optimal, similar to classic water-filling. EP with $\alpha=0.5$ performs worst in all the cases, especially at $0$~dB, where little or no AN is needed. Hence, both components of the long-term power allocation are important: the information--AN power splitting improves the ESSR in all cases, and the per-user power allocation further benefits heterogeneous LUs at low-to-moderate SNR.

\subsection{Robustness of the Secrecy Gain}\label{sec:sim_rob}

Finally, we examine whether the gain of the proposed design relies on the worst-case eavesdropper model, the accurate statistical CSI of Eve, and the location of Eve close to an LU. First, we evaluate the designs against an Eve that treats the signals of the other LUs as interference (TIN), i.e., whose SINR has the additional term $\sum_{i\neq m}p_i|\bg^{\mathrm H}(\bt)\wb_i|^2$ in the denominator of~\eqref{eq:sinre}. For $\Ptot/\sigma^2\in\{10,20,30\}$~dB, the ESSRs of all the schemes with AN increase by at most $1.53$~bps/Hz, and the proposed design still achieves the highest ESSR, i.e., its gain is not an artifact of the worst-case model. Moreover, the proposed design benefits the least from the TIN Eve, e.g., by $0.5$ versus $1.5$~bps/Hz for the rate-oriented MA at $20$~dB, because its positions reduce the leakage of all the LUs and thus also the inter-user interference received by Eve. Without AN, however, its ESSR against the TIN Eve keeps increasing with the SNR, e.g., to $27.1$~bps/Hz at $30$~dB, because the inter-user interference then acts as AN, whereas it saturates at about $14$~bps/Hz under the worst-case model (see Fig.~\ref{fig:snr}(a)). Hence, the AN is indispensable for a secrecy guarantee that does not rely on the decoding capability of Eve.

Second, all the schemes are designed with AoDs of Eve that deviate from the true ones by $\Derr$ in a random direction, as for $\Delta$ in Section~\ref{sec:sim_setup}, and are evaluated with the true AoDs. The ESSRs of the benchmarks decrease by less than $2\%$, because only their power allocation and the antenna subset of AS depend on the AoDs of Eve. In contrast, the ESSR of the proposed design decreases with $\Derr$, because its positions are tailored to the AoDs of Eve. Nevertheless, at $20$~dB, it decreases by only $3\%$ for $\Derr=0.05$~rad and still exceeds those of all benchmarks for $\Derr=\Delta=0.1$~rad ($18.73$ versus at most $17.93$~bps/Hz). This robustness is due to the smooth variation of the LoS correlations with the AoDs of Eve.

Third, when the AoDs of Eve are independent of those of the LUs and follow the same distribution, the proposed design still outperforms all the benchmarks, e.g., by $10\%$ and $18\%$ compared with the rate-oriented MA and the sparse FPA for $M=3$, whereas AS performs close to the proposed design for $M=1$. In this case, Eve is mostly well separated from the LUs, so that the compact candidates of AS can already decorrelate it from a single LU, whereas the large aperture of the MAs is essential when Eve is close to an LU.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
% =============================================================================
This paper investigated two-timescale secure transmission for an MA-enabled multiuser downlink system with a passive Eve. The derived closed-form approximation of the ergodic wiretap rate revealed that, beyond decorrelating the LUs, the MAs enhance secrecy by reducing the information leakage and increasing the AN received by Eve. Based on this result, we developed an AO algorithm, which combines secrecy water-filling with a two-stage position update and whose objective value converges monotonically and quickly. Simulation results demonstrated that the proposed secrecy-aware positioning significantly outperforms the rate-oriented two-timescale MA design, AS, and FPA arrays, enhances the secrecy even for a single LU, and reduces the AN power and the number of antennas required for secure transmission. Since this paper assumes a single-antenna Eve and perfect CSI of the LUs, extending the design to multiple multi-antenna eavesdroppers and imperfect CSI is an important direction for future work.

\section{Derivation of~\eqref{eq:cm} and~\eqref{eq:d} and Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:eve}}\label{app:det}
% =============================================================================
For brevity, the argument $\bt$ is omitted. Since $\wb_m$ and $\PHp$ are invariant to the scaling of the columns of $\bH$, \eqref{eq:Hdecomp} yields $\wb_m=\Hn(\Hn^{\mathrm H}\Hn)^{-1}\be_m/\|\Hn(\Hn^{\mathrm H}\Hn)^{-1}\be_m\|$ and $\PHp=\bI_N-\Hn(\Hn^{\mathrm H}\Hn)^{-1}\Hn^{\mathrm H}$. Moreover, $\E\{\Hn^{\mathrm H}\Hn\}=\bPhi$, $\E\{\Htl\}=\mathbf 0$, and $\E\{\Htl\mathbf x\mathbf x^{\mathrm H}\Htl^{\mathrm H}\}=\|\mathbf x\|^2\bI_N$ for any deterministic $\mathbf x$.

\textit{Derivation of~\eqref{eq:cm}:} Replacing $(\Hn^{\mathrm H}\Hn)^{-1}$ in the unnormalized ZF direction by $\bOm=\bPhi^{-1}$ yields $\wb_m\approx\Hn\bOm\be_m/\|\Hn\bOm\be_m\|$. Then, we approximate the mean of $|\gb^{\mathrm H}\Hn\bOm\be_m|^2/\|\Hn\bOm\be_m\|^2$ by the ratio of the means of its numerator and denominator, in the same spirit as~\cite[Lemma~1]{ZhangJSTSP2014}. Note that these are two separate approximations, since $\E\{(\Hn^{\mathrm H}\Hn)^{-1}\}\neq\bOm$ in general. Since $\Hn=\bL+\Htl$ and $\bL^{\mathrm H}\gb=\bv$, we have
\begin{align}
\E\{|\gb^{\mathrm H}\Hn\bOm\be_m|^2\}&=|\gb^{\mathrm H}\bL\bOm\be_m|^2+\|\gb\|^2\|\bOm\be_m\|^2\nonumber\\
&=\big|[\bOm\bv]_m\big|^2+N[\bOm^2]_{m,m},\label{eq:app1}\\
\E\{\|\Hn\bOm\be_m\|^2\}&=\be_m^{\mathrm T}\bOm\bPhi\bOm\be_m=\omega_m,\label{eq:app2}
\end{align}
whose ratio is given by~\eqref{eq:cm}.

\textit{Derivation of~\eqref{eq:d}:} Replacing $(\Hn^{\mathrm H}\Hn)^{-1}$ in $\bI_N-\PHp$ by $\bOm$ and using $\E\{\Htl\bOm\Htl^{\mathrm H}\}=\tr(\bOm)\bI_N$ yield
\begin{equation}\label{eq:app3}
\E\{\bI_N-\PHp\}\approx\bPi+\tr(\bOm)\bI_N,\qquad\bPi\triangleq\bL\bOm\bL^{\mathrm H},
\end{equation}
where $\bPi$ and $\tr(\bOm)\bI_N$ represent the parts of the signal subspace induced by the LoS and NLoS components, respectively. However, \eqref{eq:app3} lets the NLoS component capture the fraction $\tr(\bOm)$ of every direction, including those already captured by the LoS component, so that $\gb^{\mathrm H}(\bI_N-\bPi-\tr(\bOm)\bI_N)\gb$ may even become negative when $\gb$ is closely aligned with the column space of $\bL$. To avoid this double counting, we let the NLoS component capture a fraction $\chi\in[0,1]$ of the complement of $\bPi$ only, i.e.,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:app4}
\E\{\bI_N-\PHp\}\approx\bPi+\chi(\bI_N-\bPi).
\end{equation}
The fraction $\chi$ is determined by the identity $\tr(\PHp)=N-M$, which holds for every realization of $\Htl$. Since $\tr(\bPi)=\tr\big(\bOm(\bPhi-N\bI_M)\big)=M-N\tr(\bOm)$, taking the trace of both sides of~\eqref{eq:app4} yields $M-N\tr(\bOm)+\chi\big(N-M+N\tr(\bOm)\big)=M$, i.e., $\chi=N\tr(\bOm)/(N-M+N\tr(\bOm))$. Therefore, $\E\{\PHp\}\approx(1-\chi)(\bI_N-\bPi)$, which yields~\eqref{eq:d} due to $\gb^{\mathrm H}\bPi\gb=\bv^{\mathrm H}\bOm\bv$.

\textit{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:eve}:} For the range, the matrix inversion lemma yields $\bI_N-\bPi=\bI_N-\bL(\bL^{\mathrm H}\bL+N\bI_M)^{-1}\bL^{\mathrm H}=(\bI_N+\bL\bL^{\mathrm H}/N)^{-1}\succ\mathbf 0$. Together with $\bPi\succeq\mathbf 0$, this implies $\mathbf 0\preceq\bPi\prec\bI_N$. Moreover, $\bPhi\succeq N\bI_M$ implies $0<\tr(\bOm)\le M/N$, i.e., $1-\chi\in[1-M/N,1)$. Hence, $d=(1-\chi)\gb^{\mathrm H}(\bI_N-\bPi)\gb\in[0,N]$ due to $\|\gb\|^2=N$. For $c_m$, the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality yields $|[\bOm\bv]_m|^2=|\gb^{\mathrm H}\bL\bOm\be_m|^2\le N\be_m^{\mathrm T}\bOm\bL^{\mathrm H}\bL\bOm\be_m$, so that the numerator of~\eqref{eq:cm} is at most $N\be_m^{\mathrm T}\bOm(\bL^{\mathrm H}\bL+N\bI_M)\bOm\be_m=N\omega_m$ due to $N\ge1$, i.e., $c_m\in[0,N]$. The exact counterparts also lie in $[0,N]$, since $\bar X_m\le\|\gb\|^2$ and $\bar Y\le\|\gb\|^2$ hold for the unit-norm $\wb_m$ and the projector $\PHp$.

If $\kappa_m=0$, $\forall m\in\mathcal M$, then $\bL=\mathbf 0$, $\Hn=\Htl$, $\bPhi=N\bI_M$, and $\bPi=\mathbf 0$, so that~\eqref{eq:cm} and~\eqref{eq:d} reduce to $c_m=1$ and $d=N-M$. In this case, the distribution of $\Htl$ is invariant to left multiplication by any unitary matrix, which is inherited by $\wb_m$ and $\PHp$. Hence, $\wb_m$ is uniformly distributed on the unit sphere, which yields $\E\{\bar X_m\}=\|\gb\|^2/N=1$, and $\E\{\PHp\}$ commutes with every unitary matrix and thus equals $(1-M/N)\bI_N$ due to $\tr(\PHp)=N-M$, which yields $\E\{\bar Y\}=N-M$.

Finally, let $\bK=\kappa\bK_0$ for a fixed diagonal matrix $\bK_0\succ\mathbf 0$, so that $\bL=\sqrt{\kappa}\bL_0$ with $\bL_0\triangleq\Hb\bK_0^{1/2}$, which has full column rank if the LoS components of the LUs are linearly independent. As $\kappa\to\infty$, $\Hn/\sqrt\kappa\to\bL_0$ almost surely. Since $\wb_m$ and $\PHp$ are invariant to the scaling of $\Hn$ and continuous at full-column-rank matrices, and $\bar X_m$ and $\bar Y$ are bounded by $N$, the dominated convergence theorem yields $\E\{\bar X_m\}\to|\gb^{\mathrm H}\wb_m^{\infty}|^2$ and $\E\{\bar Y\}\to\gb^{\mathrm H}\mathbf P_{\infty}^\perp\gb$, where $\wb_m^{\infty}\triangleq\bL_0(\bL_0^{\mathrm H}\bL_0)^{-1}\be_m/\|\bL_0(\bL_0^{\mathrm H}\bL_0)^{-1}\be_m\|$ and $\mathbf P_{\infty}^\perp\triangleq\bI_N-\bL_0(\bL_0^{\mathrm H}\bL_0)^{-1}\bL_0^{\mathrm H}$. On the other hand, $\kappa\bOm\to(\bL_0^{\mathrm H}\bL_0)^{-1}$, $\bPi\to\bI_N-\mathbf P_{\infty}^\perp$, and $\tr(\bOm)\to0$. Hence, $d$ converges to $\gb^{\mathrm H}\mathbf P_{\infty}^\perp\gb$, and $c_m$ converges to $|[(\bL_0^{\mathrm H}\bL_0)^{-1}\bL_0^{\mathrm H}\gb]_m|^2/[(\bL_0^{\mathrm H}\bL_0)^{-1}]_{m,m}=|\gb^{\mathrm H}\wb_m^{\infty}|^2$, since $N[\bOm^2]_{m,m}$ decays as $\mathcal O(\kappa^{-2})$ whereas $\omega_m$ decays as $\mathcal O(\kappa^{-1})$. This completes the proof.

\section{Derivatives of $\omega_m$, $c_m$, and $d$}\label{app:grad}
% =============================================================================
Let $\tau_m\triangleq[\bOm^2]_{m,m}$ and $\tilde{\bv}\triangleq\bOm\bv$. Since $\bOm$ is Hermitian, \eqref{eq:dOm} can be rewritten as $\dot{\bOm}=-(\bOm\dot{\bl}_n)(\bOm\bl_n)^{\mathrm H}-(\bOm\bl_n)(\bOm\dot{\bl}_n)^{\mathrm H}$. Hence, the derivatives of $\omega_m$, $\tau_m$, and $\tr(\bOm)$ with respect to $u_n$ are given by
\begin{align}
\dot\omega_m&=[\dot{\bOm}]_{m,m}=-2\RE\big\{[\bOm\dot{\bl}_n]_m[\bOm\bl_n]_m^*\big\},\label{eq:a1}\\
\dot\tau_m&=2\RE\big\{[\bOm\dot{\bOm}]_{m,m}\big\}=-2\RE\big\{[\bOm^2\dot{\bl}_n]_m[\bOm\bl_n]_m^*\nonumber\\
&\qquad\qquad+[\bOm^2\bl_n]_m[\bOm\dot{\bl}_n]_m^*\big\},\label{eq:a2}\\
\frac{\partial\tr(\bOm)}{\partial u_n}&=\tr(\dot{\bOm})=-2\RE\big\{\bl_n^{\mathrm H}\bOm^2\dot{\bl}_n\big\},\label{eq:a3}
\end{align}
where~\eqref{eq:a2} follows from $[\dot{\bOm}\bOm]_{m,m}=[\bOm\dot{\bOm}]_{m,m}^*$. For $c_m$ in~\eqref{eq:cm}, the derivative of $\tilde{\bv}=\bOm\bv$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:a4}
\dot{\tilde{\bv}}=\dot{\bOm}\bv+\bOm\dot{\bv}=-\bOm\dot{\bl}_n\big(\bl_n^{\mathrm H}\tilde{\bv}\big)-\bOm\bl_n\big(\dot{\bl}_n^{\mathrm H}\tilde{\bv}\big)+\bOm\dot{\bv},
\end{equation}
and thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:a5}
\dot c_m=\frac{2\RE\big\{[\tilde{\bv}]_m^*[\dot{\tilde{\bv}}]_m\big\}+N\dot\tau_m-c_m\dot\omega_m}{\omega_m}.
\end{equation}
For $d=(1-\chi)(N-\bv^{\mathrm H}\bOm\bv)$ in~\eqref{eq:d} with $1-\chi=(N-M)/(N-M+N\tr(\bOm))$ as in Appendix~\ref{app:det}, we have
\begin{align}
\frac{\partial(\bv^{\mathrm H}\bOm\bv)}{\partial u_n}&=2\RE\{\tilde{\bv}^{\mathrm H}\dot{\bv}\}-2\RE\big\{(\tilde{\bv}^{\mathrm H}\dot{\bl}_n)(\bl_n^{\mathrm H}\tilde{\bv})\big\},\label{eq:a6}\\
\dot d&=-(1-\chi)\bigg(\frac{\partial(\bv^{\mathrm H}\bOm\bv)}{\partial u_n}\nonumber\\
&\qquad+\frac{N(N-\bv^{\mathrm H}\bOm\bv)}{N-M+N\tr(\bOm)}\,\frac{\partial\tr(\bOm)}{\partial u_n}\bigg),\label{eq:a7}
\end{align}
where~\eqref{eq:a6} follows from $\bv^{\mathrm H}\dot{\bOm}\bv=-2\RE\{(\tilde{\bv}^{\mathrm H}\dot{\bl}_n)(\bl_n^{\mathrm H}\tilde{\bv})\}$. Substituting~\eqref{eq:a1}, \eqref{eq:a5}, and~\eqref{eq:a7} into~\eqref{eq:dF} yields $\nabla_{\bt_n}F$. All the quantities in~\eqref{eq:a1}--\eqref{eq:a7} follow from matrix-vector products with $\bOm$, requiring $\mathcal O(M^2)$ operations.
